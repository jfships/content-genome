# Budget: pace, words, beats

## Pace (per video, never one fixed number)
A creator's pace changes with the content (a rant runs faster than an explanation) and with practice (it rises over weeks). So pace is picked per video, from the best source available:
1. **This video's own takes**, once recorded: the take decides. Re-time the card from the take; cut or re-cut there.
2. **The creator's recent pace for this delivery mode**, from the brand's pace log (`00 Decisions/voice-kit.md`, section "Pace log": date · video · mode · words/s, natural take before any speed-up). Use the latest 3 entries for the mode; recent beats old.
3. **Defaults by mode** when there's no log yet:
| Mode | Planning pace (w/s) |
|---|---|
| Explain / teach | 2.4 |
| Story | 2.6 |
| Rant / opinion | 3.0 |
| Demo beats (screen needs reading time) | 1.5–2.2 |
Write the chosen pace and its source in the card header (`pace: 3.1 w/s · rant · log`). After every recorded take, add a line to the pace log (job A step 7, or video-genome's timing step) so the number tracks the creator's improvement.

Speed-ups: speeding a take up in the edit is a normal tightening tool (video-genome's timing step measures and applies it, up to about 12% per line; past ~10% voices start to sound squeezed, judge by listening). It is not a reason to plan a script that can't be said at the creator's pace for that mode. The check flags (never fails) a plan that would need more than ~12%, and any line above ~4.5 w/s after the edit (comprehension drops).
Evidence: speaking-rate references and vendor templates (130–200 wpm); MOOC data where faster, enthusiastic delivery held viewers longer (long-form, correlational). Mode defaults are craft starting points; the creator's own takes replace them.

## Words per runtime (spoken words only)
| Runtime | at 2.2 | at 2.4 (explain) | at 2.6 (story) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 33 | 36 | 39 |
| 20 s | 44 | 48 | 52 |
| 30 s | 66 | 72 | 78 |
| 45 s | 99 | 108 | 117 |
| 60 s | 132 | 144 | 156 |
Subtract ~2.4 words for every second of a silent beat (a demo without voice, a held reaction, a planned silence). The script must fit the cap; footage may run over and gets cut after watching.

## Beats per runtime
| Runtime | Beats | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 15–30 s | 3–4 | hook lives inside beat 1 |
| 31–45 s | 4–5 | |
| 46–60 s | 5–6 | 7 only for lists and ladders |
Beat floor: about 5 s and 12 words, except the hook beat. Fewer, fuller beats beat many thin ones. (Inference from word counts; craft default.)

## The opening
- Skip rate counts people who leave in the first 3 s (Instagram's own metric). The point must be carried by then: ≤8 spoken words, or the first image + screen text carry it while the line finishes.
- First payoff (laugh, twist, result) ideally within 3 s; at the latest, the promise is clear by ~5 s.
- Nothing before the hook: no greeting, name, logo, intro.

## Platform lengths (defaults; shortest length that lands the idea)
Instagram Reels 20–60 s (recommendation to non-followers up to 3 min); YouTube Shorts 20–45 s; TikTok 25–45 s. Longer Reels are fine when every beat carries new information; the largest 2026 dataset (brand accounts, correlational) found 45–60 s Reels got the most views and 0–30 s the fewest. There is no official ideal length.

## Timing a script
Count words → divide by pace → add 10–15% for pauses → the creator reads it aloud with a timer (always; it beats the estimate). Long → cut content first; a small speed-up in the edit is fine (see Pace). Short → add a specific detail rather than slowing down.
