# Arcs: curiosity

Family: the Reel opens a visible gap in what the viewer knows and closes it late; variants differ in how the gap is filled (clues, evidence steps with a wrong guess, a finding, a flipped assumption, a new angle, a guessing game).
Shared skeleton: `[what you can see + what is hidden] [clue or evidence] [clue, bigger] [reveal, shown while said] [what it means]`.
Evidence: no arc tested at 30–60 s; the mechanism has research behind it in other media: curiosity is a felt gap that needs enough shown to see the hole; suspense is best when twists get bigger and rarer and the answer is held to the last beat (economic theory); a surprise holds attention only for a moment and pulls focus off the story, so re-anchor right after it (ads, eye-tracking). "Open loops are remembered" is false (38-paper meta-analysis); the urge to resume is real, so write "stay for the part where…" as a promise, never a memory claim.
Runtimes: reversal, kishotenketsu-twist and discovery fit 20–45 s; mystery-box 30–60 s (false answer only at ≥45 s); investigation and two-truths-and-a-lie 40–60 s (statements under 5 s at 30 s).

Card fields as in `arcs-setback.md`. Words per beat ≈ seconds × 2.4. All examples are made up.

### mystery-box · Mystery box
Type: ORDER (operator; also usable as the whole arc)
Does: shows a result and names the hidden cause, drops clues that each raise a new question, reveals the cause in the last third.
Use when: demo, tip, news, comparison; the hybrid order (promise the result, hold the proof). 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 BOX 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) hook · B2 CLUE 1 23% (3.5–10.5 | 7–21) · B3 CLUE 2 25% (10.5–18 | 21–36) · B4 FALSE ANSWER 12% (n/a | 36–43) 60 s only · B5 REVEAL 18% (18–27 | 43–54) · B6 AFTERTASTE 10% (27–30 | 54–60). @30: 4 beats, no false answer; aftertaste is the last line of the reveal.
V: [+1, +1, +1, 0, +2, +1] `▆▆▆▄█▆`
Lines: B1 says what is known and what is hidden; each clue is a `so` that opens a new question; reveal in the last third, not before. `[<result> is real; the thing that did it isn't what you think] [clue: <fact 1>] [clue: <fact 2>] [you'd guess <wrong answer>; it isn't] [it's <reveal>] [so <implication>]`
  e.g. "This bread rose twice as high, and the trick isn't the yeast." / "Clue one: look at what I did the night before."
Edit: B1 visual-prop or still of the result, CUT-M, music without payload · each clue ends on a small HIT or POP; RISE builds across clues · B4 INT: fake-out cut · B5 SIL 0.5 s before the reveal word, then PUNCH @reveal word + CARD-N; reveal on screen while said (temporal contiguity).
Pairs: hooks curiosity-gap, question, visual-prop, secret, interrupt; endings send-line, payoff-stop; tease-next, comment stage-gated. Clashes: result-first or proof-first that also gives the cause; bold-claim that states the answer. Showing the output while hiding the cause is the box itself.
Fails when: the reveal is smaller than the promise; no clue for more than ~8–10 s (INFERENCE); the box is vague, so there is no real gap.
Evidence: RESEARCH (mechanism, other media) [4]: curiosity gap, withhold to the end; memory claim for open loops not supported.

### investigation · Investigation (wrong guess first)
Type: ORDER · Aliases: evidence-led
Does: an anomaly, a first hypothesis that turns out wrong, a test, a pivot, the finding, what it means.
Use when: comparison, news, tip. 40–60 s. Differs from mystery-box: every beat is an evidence step and the first guess is wrong on purpose.
Beats: B1 ANOMALY 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 HYPOTHESIS 25% (3–10.5 | 6–21) · B3 TEST 25% (10.5–18 | 21–36) · B4 PIVOT 18% (18–23.5 | 36–47) · B5 FINDING 16% (23.5–28 | 47–56.5) · B6 IMPLICATION 6% (28–30 | 56.5–60). @30: pivot + finding one beat; implication the last line → 4 beats.
V: [+1, 0, -1, +1, +2] `▆▄▂▆█`
Lines: `I assumed …` · `so I tested …` · `which showed …`. `[<anomaly>] [I assumed <x>] [so I tested; result <y>] [which meant <pivot>] [finding: <z>] [so <implication>]`
  e.g. "Same paint, two walls, one peeling in a month." / "I assumed bad paint. It wasn't. The peeling wall was never primed."
Edit: evidence frames as CUTAWAY or SCREEN with CARD-N · B4 INT · B5 PUNCH @finding word + HIT · re-anchor CARD-L after the pivot.
Pairs: hooks curiosity-gap, question, number; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: result-first.
Fails when: no wrong hypothesis (no tension); evidence is hand-waved.
Evidence: RESEARCH-adjacent [1] (curiosity gap) + INFERENCE. Flag: weak

### discovery · Discovery ("I found out")
Type: ORDER
Does: an everyday moment, a surprising finding, a check that it is real, what it means for the viewer.
Use when: news, tip, demo. 20–45 s.
Beats: B1 CONTEXT 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook carries the finding teaser · B2 FINDING 20% (3–9 | 6–18) · B3 VERIFY 30% (9–18 | 18–36) · B4 IMPLICATION 40% (18–30 | 36–60) includes the close.
V: [0, +2, +1, +1] `▄█▆▆`
Lines: `I opened <thing> and found <surprise>` · then proof · then `which means`. `[I was doing <routine>] [and I found <surprise>] [so I tested it: <result>] [which means you can <x>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "I was booking a train and found a fare option nobody mentions." / "Tested it on five trips: a third off every time."
Edit: B2 PUNCH @finding word + HIT, SCREEN or CUTAWAY with highlight, CARD-N · B3 CUT-F proof · B4 face, CUT-S, CARD-L.
Pairs: hooks secret, mid-action, visual-prop, number; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: bold-claim before anything is shown.
Fails when: the discovery is well known; no verify beat.
Evidence: RESEARCH-adjacent [1]: surprise, interest and practical use predict sharing (news articles, other medium). Flag: weak

### reversal · Reversal (expectation flip)
Type: ORDER (operator; also usable as the whole arc) · Aliases: plot-twist
Does: builds an assumption with two signals, flips it in one line, re-anchors the viewer on the new reading.
Use when: opinion, news, comparison. 20–45 s. One twist only.
Beats: B1 BUILD 45% (0–13.5 | 0–27) hook = the expected claim · B2 PIVOT 13% (13.5–17.5 | 27–35) one line · B3 REFRAME 30% (17.5–26.5 | 35–53) · B4 BUTTON 12% (26.5–30 | 53–60). @30: pivot is the first line of B3; button the last.
V: win-to-trap [+1, -2, 0, +1] `▆▁▄▆` (flip the signs for trap-to-win)
Lines: B1 two signals toward the obvious conclusion · B2 `except` / `but here's what I missed` · B3 `which means` + the new reading, said plainly (re-anchor). `[it looks like <x>: <sign 1>, <sign 2>] [except <flip>] [which means <new reading>; so <what to do>] [button]`
  e.g. "This supplier is cheap, fast, and has perfect reviews." / "Except every review is from the same week."
Edit: B1 steady CUT-M, confident music · B2 SIL 0.5 s + SMASH + HIT (the INT) · B3 CARD-L re-anchor sentence, CUT-S, CUTAWAY of the evidence.
Pairs: hooks bold-claim, question, contrarian (state the expected claim); endings send-line, payoff-stop, button. Clashes: curiosity-gap hooks that announce a twist is coming.
Fails when: the twist is visible from the hook; several twists; B3 does not re-orient the viewer.
Evidence: RESEARCH (mechanism, other media) [3]: reversal + recognition as the strongest plot (Aristotle, craft); bigger-rarer twists (theory); re-anchor after surprise (ads).

### kishotenketsu-twist · Kishotenketsu (new angle, no conflict)
Type: CONTAINER
Does: introduces a thing, deepens it with no change, then an unexpected link to something unrelated, then what that link lets the viewer do.
Use when: demo, tip, comparison. 20–60 s. No conflict needed; the twist is a new angle on the same thing.
Beats: B1 KI 18% (0–5.5 | 0–11) hook · B2 SHO 30% (5.5–14.5 | 11–29) · B3 TEN 27% (14.5–22.5 | 29–45) · B4 KETSU 25% (22.5–30 | 45–60).
V: [0, +1, +2, +1] `▄▆█▆`
Lines: KI introduces; SHO deepens with no change; TEN `and yet` / `turns out`; KETSU what it means. `[here's <thing A>] [and here's how it normally works] [turns out the same <thing> does <unrelated surprise>] [so <new way to use it>]`
  e.g. "This is a rice cooker. It steams rice and keeps it warm." / "Turns out the steamer tray makes a perfect soft-boiled egg."
Edit: KI + SHO calm, CUT-M, MUS-LOW · TEN: SMASH, PUNCH @turns, HIT, new angle (the INT) · KETSU CUT-S, CARD-L.
Pairs: hooks curiosity-gap, visual-prop, secret, interrupt; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: stakes, warning (they promise conflict).
Fails when: TEN is only a bigger SHO (that is escalation, `arcs-ladder.md`); no KETSU meaning.
Evidence: CRAFT [1] (classical four-line poetry origin; "no conflict" is a simplification). No data. Flag: weak

### two-truths-and-a-lie · Two truths and a lie
Type: ARGUMENT
Does: sets the game, gives three numbered claims, holds a guess pause, reveals the lie and teaches the truth.
Use when: tip, list, news. 40–60 s. The lie must be a plausible myth; the truth must teach something.
Beats: B1 RULES 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) hook · B2–B4 STATEMENTS 54% (3.5–20 | 7–40) three at ~18% each · B5 GUESS PAUSE 6% (20–21.5 | 40–43) 1.5–2.5 s, a held beat · B6 REVEAL + WHY 23% (21.5–28.5 | 43–57) · B7 CLOSE 5% (28.5–30 | 57–60). @30: statements fall under 5 s; prefer ≥40 s.
V: [+1, +1, +1, +1, 0, +2, +1] `▆▆▆▆▄█▆`
Lines: "One, two, three" as first words; after the reveal, repeat the truth, never the lie (familiarity risk). `[one of these is false] [1 <claim>] [2 <claim>] [3 <claim>] [guess] [the lie is <n>; the truth: <fact> because <why>] [close]`
  e.g. "Two truths and a lie about carry-on bags." / "Number three is the lie: the size limit changes by airline, so check yours."
Edit: each statement its own CARD-L with STEP "1/2/3", CUT-M · B5 SIL on a countdown ring (the INT) · B6 CARD-N on the truth; mark over the lie is small; the last card shows the truth.
Pairs: hooks question, interrupt, secret; endings loop, payoff-stop; comment (guess) stage-gated. Clashes: stakes.
Fails when: the lie is obvious; the reveal teaches nothing; statements shrink under 4 s.
Evidence: INFERENCE; myth-repetition caution RESEARCH (other medium) [1]. Flag: weak
