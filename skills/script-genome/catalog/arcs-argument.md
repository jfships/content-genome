# Arcs: argument

Family: the Reel weighs claims and lands on a verdict the viewer can use; variants differ in what is weighed (one event seen twice, two cases, a myth against the truth, a test, lessons from a past mistake).
Shared skeleton: `[question or event] [side or evidence A] [side or evidence B] [verdict with conditions] [button]`.
Evidence: no arc tested at 30–60 s; the research that changes lines here is from misinformation correction in text (other medium): lead with the fact, warn before naming a myth, say the myth once, give an alternative explanation, use at most three counter-points (three beat twelve); practical usefulness predicts sharing (news articles). Verdicts without conditions read as fence-sitting (INFERENCE).
Runtimes: myth-vs-truth and mentor-wish-i-knew fit 30–60 s (4 beats at 30 s); two-sides, debate-verdict and experiment-test-result fit 30–60 s with ~8 s per side at 30 s.

Card fields as in `arcs-setback.md`. Words per beat ≈ seconds × 2.4. All examples are made up.

### two-sides · Two sides of one event
Type: ARGUMENT
Does: one event, seen from two viewpoints at equal strength, then the thing both missed or a verdict.
Use when: opinion, comparison, news. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 EVENT 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) hook · B2 SIDE A 28% (3.5–12 | 7–24) · B3 SIDE B 28% (12–20.5 | 24–41) · B4 COLLISION/VERDICT 22% (20.5–27 | 41–54) · B5 BUTTON 10% (27–30 | 54–60). @30: button is the last line of B4.
V: [0, +1, -1, +2, +1] `▄▆▂█▆`
Lines: same event nouns, opposite verbs; parallel sentence frames; verdict is a `but` neither side saw, or criteria for choosing. `[<event>] [to <A> it means <view A> because <reason>] [to <B> it means <view B> because <reason>] [but both miss <third thing>; so <verdict>] [button]`
  e.g. "A customer sent back a cake because it 'looked homemade'." / "To her, homemade meant messy. To me, it was the whole point."
Edit: SPLIT or a hard POV swap (wardrobe, angle or color swap) · same b-roll replayed with a different POP for each side · side B's cut rhythm mirrors side A's · INT at the collision.
Pairs: hooks callout, contrarian, question; endings send-line, button; comment (pick a side) stage-gated. Clashes: result-first.
Fails when: one side is a strawman; no verdict; both sides over-claimed.
Evidence: INFERENCE [1]; counter-argument cap from RESEARCH (other medium). Flag: weak

### debate-verdict · Debate, then verdict
Type: ARGUMENT · Aliases: debate, both-sides-verdict (shape twin: comparison)
Does: a question, the best case for A, the best case for B, then a verdict that names the condition for each.
Use when: comparison, opinion. 30–60 s. Differs from two-sides: here claims clash and the creator rules; two-sides is one event seen twice.
Beats: B1 QUESTION 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 CASE A 25% (3–10.5 | 6–21) · B3 CASE B 25% (10.5–18 | 21–36) · B4 VERDICT 40% (18–30 | 36–60) criteria + verdict + close.
V: [0, +1, +1, +2] `▄▆▆█`
Lines: parallel ("The case for <A> is …"); verdict names the condition ("if you <x>, pick A; if <y>, B"); ≤3 arguments per side. `[<A> or <B>?] [A: <best argument>] [B: <best argument>] [pick <A> if <x>, <B> if <y>]`
  e.g. "Cast iron or non-stick: which is worth it?" / "If you cook eggs every day, non-stick. If you sear twice a week, cast iron pays you back."
Edit: SPLIT or two-set swap, CARD-L pro/con per side · verdict in a different framing (centered, CUT-S), INT @verdict, CARD-L condition.
Pairs: hooks question, contrarian, number; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: result-first.
Fails when: "it depends" with no conditions; a strawman side.
Evidence: INFERENCE [1]; overkill caution RESEARCH (other medium). Flag: weak

### myth-vs-truth · Myth vs truth
Type: ARGUMENT · Shape twin: myth-bust (`shapes.md`)
Does: warning + truth first, the myth once and labeled, why people believe it, evidence, what to do, and the truth again as the last card.
Use when: tip, opinion, news. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 WARNING + TRUTH 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) hook · B2 MYTH 10% (3.5–6.5 | 7–13) one line · B3 WHY BELIEVED 13% (6.5–10.5 | 13–21) · B4 EVIDENCE 40% (10.5–22.5 | 21–45) · B5 DO THIS 17% (22.5–27.5 | 45–55) · B6 CLOSE 8% (27.5–30 | 55–60). @30: B1+B2 one beat; B3 joins B4; B6 the last line of B5 → 3–4 beats.
V: [+1, -1, 0, +1, +2] `▆▂▄▆█`
Lines: order: fact, explicit warning before the myth, the myth once, an alternative explanation, the fact again; ≤3 pieces of evidence; the hook never uses the myth as its main sound bite. `[here's a myth that costs you <x>; truth: <fact>] [the myth: "<myth>"] [people believe it because <reason>] [what actually happens: <shown>] [so do <x>]`
  e.g. "Truth first: rinsing raw chicken spreads germs, it doesn't remove them." / "The myth: 'a rinse makes it cleaner.'"
Edit: B2 small labeled card (WARN "MYTH"), never the biggest text · B4 CUTAWAY or SCREEN evidence, CARD-N, INT @evidence · last card: the true statement (CARD-L), never the myth.
Pairs: hooks warning, secret, contrarian (as a warning), callout; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: curiosity-gap that withholds the truth.
Fails when: the myth is repeated until it sticks; nobody held it; the truth is "it depends".
Evidence: RESEARCH [1] (misinformation correction, text, other medium; a later edition reportedly softens backfire claims, unverified).

### experiment-test-result · Experiment / test result
Type: ARGUMENT · Aliases: experiment, test-result (shape twin: i-tested)
Does: a question, the method in one sentence, results on one measure, a conditional verdict, an honest caveat.
Use when: comparison, demo, news. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 QUESTION 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 METHOD 15% (3–7.5 | 6–15) · B3 RESULTS 40% (7.5–19.5 | 15–39) · B4 VERDICT 25% (19.5–27 | 39–54) · B5 CAVEAT 10% (27–30 | 54–60). @30: method joins B1 or B3; caveat the last line of B4.
V: [0, 0, +1, +2, +1] `▄▄▆█▆`
Lines: one measure for every option, said once ("I scored each on <metric>"); the surprising result earliest in B3; verdict conditional; caveat in one sentence with n. `[does <x> beat <y>?] [I ran <n> tests, scoring <metric>] [result 1 … result 2 (surprise)] [so use <x> when <c>] [caveat]`
  e.g. (made-up) "Does a ten-minute walk after dinner change how fast I fall asleep?" / "Fourteen nights each way, same bedtime. Walk nights: twelve minutes faster, on average, for me."
Edit: CARD-N scoreboard, SPLIT, one result per cut (CUT-M), INT @surprise result · verdict CUT-S, CARD-L condition.
Pairs: hooks question, number, proof-first; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: mystery-box.
Fails when: n is small or unsaid; the metric is subjective with no definition; the verdict ignores the caveat.
Evidence: INFERENCE; MANY [1]. Flag: weak

### mentor-wish-i-knew · "What I wish I knew"
Type: ARGUMENT/CURVE · Aliases: mentor, wish-i-knew
Does: a regret hook, the past mistake and its cost, up to three lessons each with a reason, one first action.
Use when: tip, list. 30–60 s. The mistakes-list framework with a story hat.
Beats: B1 REGRET HOOK 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) · B2 MISTAKE 18% (3.5–9 | 7–18) · B3 LESSONS 52% (9–24.5 | 18–49) ≤3, each rule + `because` · B4 GIFT LINE 18% (24.5–30 | 49–60).
V: [-1, -1, +1, +2] `▂▂▆█`
Lines: first person for the regret, second person for the lessons; one rule + one reason each; three max. `[what I wish I knew before <thing>] [I <mistake>, which cost <x>] [one: <rule> because <reason>; two …; three …] [so start with <one action>]`
  e.g. "Three things I wish I knew before my first solo trip." / "One: book the first night before you land, because tired-you makes bad choices."
Edit: CARD-N numbered rule cards, CUT-M, PUNCH @each rule's key word (≤3 per 30 s) · gift line CUT-S, direct address.
Pairs: hooks warning, secret, number, callout; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: mystery-box.
Fails when: lessons are generic ("be consistent"); no cost shown for the mistake.
Evidence: RESEARCH-adjacent [1] (practical usefulness predicts sharing, news articles, other medium) + INFERENCE.
