# Arcs: ladder

Family: each step is bigger than the last (scale, stakes, time pressure, rank or absurdity), and the top rung is the payoff; variants differ in what climbs and whether the pattern breaks.
Shared skeleton: `[rung 1] [so <rung 2, bigger>] [so <rung 3, bigger>] [top rung or break] [button: what it proves]`.
Evidence: no arc tested at 30–60 s; theory support only: suspense works best when belief swings grow over time and the answer comes last (economic model, no viewer data); the rule of three's applause effect is cited without sample details and its own source says scientific support is weak; the escalating-problem cascade is a real craft pattern under many names, with no retention data.
Runtimes: rule-of-three-with-break fits 15–30 s or sits inside another arc; escalation, escalating-problem and countdown-to-reveal fit 30–60 s (up to 7 beats at 60 s); ticking-clock 30–60 s; escalation-to-absurdity 20–45 s.

Card fields as in `arcs-setback.md`. Words per beat ≈ seconds × 2.4. All examples are made up.

### escalation · Escalation ladder
Type: LADDER
Does: the same move pushed further each rung, each rung bigger than the last, ending on what the top proves.
Use when: demo, comparison, list. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 RUNG 1 18% (0–5.5 | 0–11) hook · B2 RUNG 2 22% (5.5–12 | 11–24) · B3 RUNG 3 25% (12–19.5 | 24–39) · B4 TOP RUNG 23% (19.5–26.5 | 39–53) · B5 BUTTON 12% (26.5–30 | 53–60). @30: rungs 2–3 merge; button the last line of B4 → 3–4 beats.
V: arousal [0, +1, +1, +2, +1] `▄▆▆█▆`
Lines: same sentence frame each rung ("so I pushed it to …"); each rung ≥ the last in scale. `[basic: <level 1>] [so <level 2>] [so <level 3>] [<top level>] [button: <what it proves>]`
  e.g. "Salt on a steak: fine. Salt it an hour ahead: better." / "Salt it the night before, uncovered in the fridge: that's the crust you've been chasing."
Edit: cut rate rises one step per rung (CUT-M, CUT-M, CUT-F, CUT-F) · STEP rung number "1/4" · RISE per rung · INT only at the top rung (HIT, or SIL then slam) · button CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks number, result-first (as proof of the top rung), visual-prop; retention payoff-ladder; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: ticking-clock used at the same time; calm pacing.
Fails when: rung 2 is not clearly bigger than rung 1; the pattern is predictable with no break (add rule-of-three-with-break); every rung is the same explanation.
Evidence: INFERENCE [2]; theory: growing belief swings (economic model). Flag: weak

### ticking-clock · Ticking clock
Type: LADDER
Does: a stated deadline drives every beat; the time left is said and shown each beat until the result.
Use when: demo, test, challenge. 30–60 s. The on-screen timer is mandatory: without it the arc stops working.
Beats: B1 DEADLINE 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) hook · B2 ATTEMPT 26% (3.5–11.5 | 7–23) · B3 SETBACK 24% (11.5–18.5 | 23–37) clock shrinks · B4 LAST PUSH 26% (18.5–26.5 | 37–53) · B5 RESULT 12% (26.5–30 | 53–60). @30: B5 is the last line of B4 → 4 beats.
V: [0, +1, -1, +1, +2] `▄▆▂▆█` (arousal climbs the whole way)
Lines: deadline as a number in sentence one; time left in every beat; connectives `until`, `with <n> minutes left`. `[I have <time> to <task>] [first <attempt>, <time left>] [but <setback>, <less time>] [so I <last move>, <seconds left>] [result: done or not]`
  e.g. "I have twenty minutes to fix this door before guests arrive." / "Six minutes left, and the new hinge doesn't line up."
Edit: STEP timer on screen the whole time (mandatory) · TICK, tempo rising · cut rate M, F, F, X · INT at the setback: DROP or buzz · B5 SIL then HIT, CARD-N with the time left.
Pairs: hooks stakes, number, mid-action, visual-prop; endings payoff-stop, send-line; tease-next stage-gated. Clashes: calm pacing; silence used more than once; mystery-box.
Fails when: the deadline is invented; the clock is not visible; nothing is at risk; edit time is far longer than the shown time with no disclosure.
Evidence: INFERENCE; MANY [1–2] (challenge-video tradition); time pressure as arousal (other medium). Flag: weak

### countdown-to-reveal · Countdown to #1
Type: LADDER
Does: promises a ranked list with #1 teased in the hook, runs the lower ranks fast, gives #1 the most time.
Use when: list, comparison, tip; #1 is clearly the strongest. 30–60 s. The list-of-3 shape (`shapes.md`) bans a slow countdown to an untold #1; this card is the version where #1 is teased in the hook.
Beats: B1 PROMISE 8% (0–2.5 | 0–5) hook with the #1 teaser · B2 LOWER RANKS 54% (2.5–18.5 | 5–37) #3 and #2 ≈ 8 s each at 30; #5 to #2 ≈ 8 s each at 60 · B3 #1 REVEAL 30% (18.5–27.5 | 37–55) 1.4–1.5× an item's time · B4 OUTRO 8% (27.5–30 | 55–60) last line of B3 at 30 s.
V: arousal ramp [0, +1, +1, +2, +1] `▄▆▆█▆`
Lines: the number is the first word of each item ("Number three:"); each item one what + one why. `[3 <things>; #1 <teaser>] [#3 <item> because <why>] [#2 <item> because <why>] [#1 <item> because <why> + shown] [outro]`
  e.g. "Three things I'd pack even if I lost the rest. Number one saved a whole trip." / "Number two: a door wedge, for any room where the lock feels loose."
Edit: CARD-N rank number big and persistent, each number a new look, CUT-M · #1: PUSH, SIL 0.5 s, then the reveal with PUNCH @item word · outro CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks number, secret, callout; endings send-line, payoff-stop; comment stage-gated. Clashes: ticking-clock.
Fails when: #1 is not clearly the strongest; items are unrelated; too many items (each under 5 s).
Evidence: INFERENCE [2]; peak-end (weak, other medium). Strongest last is supported by comedy craft and recency effects; never put it in the middle. Flag: weak

### escalation-to-absurdity · Escalation to absurdity
Type: LADDER
Does: three reasonable-sounding rungs, then one absurd break, then a snap back to the serious point.
Use when: opinion, story (comedy). 20–45 s.
Beats: B1 RUNG 1 20% (0–6 | 0–12) hook · B2 RUNG 2 25% (6–13.5 | 12–27) · B3 RUNG 3 25% (13.5–21 | 27–42) · B4 ABSURD BREAK 20% (21–27 | 42–54) · B5 SNAP-BACK 10% (27–30 | 54–60). @30: rungs 1–3 can run as one 3-line block if each line is short; snap-back the last line of B4.
V: [0, +1, +1, +2, +1] `▄▆▆█▆`
Lines: rungs share one frame and sound sensible; the break does not; deadpan delivery; snap-back returns to the point. `[sensible use] [a bit more] [more] [absurd extreme] [which is why <serious point>]`
  e.g. "You can track your steps. You can track your sleep. You can track your water." / "You can also track how often you check the tracker. That's where I stop."
Edit: rungs CUT-M with identical timing (the pattern) · B4 breaks it: SMASH, PUNCH, prop, HIT (the INT) · B5 SIL then plain frame.
Pairs: hooks pov-setup, interrupt, contrarian; shape skit-pov; endings button, loop. Clashes: stakes, warning.
Fails when: the break isn't funnier than the pattern; no serious snap-back (it is just a skit).
Evidence: INFERENCE; MANY comedy craft [1–2]. Flag: weak

### rule-of-three-with-break · Rule of three with a break
Type: LADDER (micro-arc; also a device inside any arc)
Does: two items share a frame, the third breaks it, a button names the break.
Use when: opinion, tip. 15–30 s alone, or 5–15 s inside another arc. Once per Reel.
Beats: A 30% (0–9 | 0–18) · B 30% (9–18 | 18–36) · BREAK 28% (18–26.5 | 36–53) · BUTTON 12% (26.5–30 | 53–60). Inside another arc: one beat of ~3 short lines.
V: [+1, +1, +2] `▆▆█`
Lines: A and B in the same grammatical frame; the third breaks it. `[A <item>] [B <item>] [C break: opposite or surprise] [button]`
  e.g. "Pick a name. Pick a logo. Pick a supplier who has never shipped on time." / "Two of those are choices. One is a trap."
Edit: identical timing and framing for A and B · BREAK changes shot, speed or sound (the INT), POP @break word · button CUT-S.
Pairs: any hook; best with contrarian, callout; endings any default. Clashes: none; overuse.
Fails when: the pattern isn't clear after A and B; used more than once in one Reel.
Evidence: SINGLE [1] (rhetoric; applause observation cited without sample details; source says support is weak). Flag: weak

### escalating-problem · Escalating problem (each fix creates a bigger problem)
Type: LADDER · Aliases: rick-and-morty, problem-cascade
Does: fixing the first problem causes a second, worse one; the viewer's question grows instead of closing, until a real fix that has a price.
Use when: story, tip, comparison; troubleshooting, "I tried the shortcut", honest reviews. 30–60 s: one cascade at 30 s, two at 45–60 s. Differs from oedipus (one false fix) and but-therefore (a line rule): here each fix is the cause of the next, bigger problem.
Beats: B1 PROBLEM + FIX 25% (0–7.5 | 0–15) hook in the first 3 s · B2 FIX WORKS, BUT P2 20% (7.5–13.5 | 15–27) first complication at ~25–40% · B3 FIX P2 → P3, WORSE 25% (13.5–21 | 27–42) bigger one at ~55–75% (60 s only; at 30 s merge B2–B3) · B4 REAL FIX AT A PRICE 20% (21–27 | 42–54) · B5 RULE 10% (27–30 | 54–60). @30: 4 beats.
V: [+1, -1, -2, +1, +1] `▆▂▁▆▆`
Lines: `it worked, but it created <P2>, which is worse` · `fixing that needed <step>` · `now it works, but only because <price>`. Each problem must be caused by the previous fix. `[<fix A> solved <P1>] [but it caused <P2>, worse] [fixing <P2> meant <step>, which caused <P3>] [now it works, but only because <price>] [rule]`
  e.g. "The leak stopped when I tightened the nut." / "But the tight nut cracked the old pipe, which is worse."
Edit: each new problem gets WARN + PUNCH @problem word · cut rate rises per problem (CUT-M, CUT-F, CUT-F) · INT at the biggest problem (DROP or SMASH) · B4 CUTAWAY of the real fix, CARD-N · B5 CARD-L rule, CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks stakes, warning, mid-action; line rule but-therefore (the cascade is a run of buts); endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: result-first (spoils the cascade); bold-claim that says it was easy.
Fails when: the cascade is staged or untrue; the tool or fix simply works; a complication is unrelated to the fix before it.
Evidence: MANY [4] craft cousins (Story Spine "because of that", Save the Cat false victory with hidden costs, Story Circle "pay the price", per-segment escalation in creator memos); no retention data. The Rick and Morty attribution is unverified: no source found where that writers' room teaches it.
