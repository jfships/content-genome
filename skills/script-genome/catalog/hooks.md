# Hooks: standard shapes

Load when writing or checking the first ~3 s of a Reel. Psychological mechanisms: `hooks-psych.md`. Unusual deliveries: `hooks-unusual.md`. Load only the file you need.

## How to use

- A hook is three layers fired together: **spoken line**, **screen text (3–7 words)**, **first image**. All three carry the same idea. No layer is ranked first. Screen text comes in two versions; offer both in the hook step and let the creator pick per video: **(a) own words**: the text adds an angle and never repeats the spoken sentence; **(b) mirror**: the text shows the spoken line word for word while it's said (captions are hidden for that line so no word shows twice). The brand's house rules may set a default.
- The first image must make sense with sound off. Test: freeze 1 s in, mute; a stranger can say what the Reel is about.
- Nothing before the hook: no greeting, logo, name, intro card or "so today".
- Meta names three hook types: **value promise**, **statement of intent**, **question / invitation**. Every other hook here, and every one in `hooks-unusual.md`, is a delivery of one of these three. Name which one a draft delivers; if none, it is not a hook yet.
- First payoff ideally within ~3 s (craft default, not a measured threshold). Prove the promise by ~10 s.
- **≤ 8 spoken words carry the point.** The rest of the hook beat can add, never delay.
- Write 2–3 hooks per script, each as a full three-layer set, ranked, and name the type it delivers.
- Pick by idea (see `selector.md`): demo or tool → result-first / proof-first; personal story → story then proof (confession, mid-action); hybrid → promise the result, hold the proof.
- All example lines are made up. Numbers in examples are invented shape demos, never facts to reuse.

Grades: OFFICIAL · RESEARCH · MANY · SINGLE · VENDOR · INFERENCE. No hook shape has ever been tested against another on organic 20–60 s Reels. MANY means repeated, not proven.

### result-first · Result first
Does: shows the payoff before the work; the viewer stays for the route.
Use when: demo, tip, before/after, "I tested". Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `[specific result + number or time] + [using / from + method]`
  e.g. (made-up) "This loaf took 12 minutes of hands-on work." / "I fixed the dripping tap with one $4 part."
Layers: spoken = the result in plain words / text = the number or time / first image = the finished thing.
Edit: `CARD-N @result` hold ≥1.5 s; `CUTAWAY @method`; `J` into B2.
Pairs: number, proof-first, before-after. Clashes: mystery-box (result kills the mystery).
Fails when: the result cannot be seen or believed in frame 1; the number is fake or vague; the body then delays the method.
Evidence: MANY [7] practitioner + adjacent OFFICIAL (Meta value promise); spoiler/suspense research by analogy (other medium).

### proof-first · Proof first
Does: leads with the evidence (receipt, screen, side-by-side) before any claim.
Use when: demo, comparison, review; any claim a stranger would doubt. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `[evidence on screen] + "[what it proves], here's how."`
  e.g. "Same recipe, two pans. Look at the crust." / "This is the shelf before I touched it."
Layers: spoken = names what the proof shows / text = the verdict in 3–5 words / first image = the proof itself.
Edit: `SPLIT` or `WIPE @event`; `POP` the verdict word; `SFX @reveal`.
Pairs: result-first, number, two-sides. Clashes: secret (proof on screen leaves nothing hidden).
Fails when: proof is unreadable at phone size; proof is staged or cherry-picked without saying so.
Evidence: MANY [7] (same sources as result-first; "proof-first" is a named alias). Proof-shown-beats-stated: INFERENCE from authority research.

### value-promise · Value promise / statement of intent
Does: tells the viewer what they get and whether it is for them; removes confusion.
Use when: step-by-step teaching, tutorials. The safest default for a teaching account. Delivers: value promise / statement of intent.
Lines: `"In [time], [outcome] with [tool or method]."` · `"Here's how I [outcome] without [usual cost]."`
  e.g. (made-up) "Thirty seconds: how to sharpen a knife with a mug." / "Here's how I pack a week of lunches in one bag."
Layers: spoken = the promise / text = outcome + time / first image = the tool or the finished outcome.
Edit: `STEP` label `@time`; `CUTAWAY @tool`; `CAPTION emphasis`.
Pairs: number, callout, curiosity-gap (adds tension). Clashes: none; weak alone if generic.
Fails when: generic ("cooking tips"); all promise and no tension, so nothing holds past line two.
Evidence: OFFICIAL via press (Meta Marketing blog, Dec 2025; Instagram business page) + MANY [5].

### bold-claim · Bold claim
Does: a strong, true, surprising statement that the body proves.
Use when: opinion, result, myth-bust where the claim is checkable. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `"[thing] [surprising verb] [specific outcome]."`
  e.g. "Your oven dial is lying to you." / "A $10 level beats the laser one for shelves."
Layers: spoken = the claim / text = the claim's key noun + verb / first image = the object the claim is about.
Edit: `PUNCH @key-word`; `HIT` on the claim; `SIL` 0.3 s after.
Pairs: proof-first, number. Clashes: secret, warning (two loud openers stacked).
Fails when: the claim is not defended in the body; it is exaggeration dressed as surprise.
Evidence: INFERENCE (closest: prediction-error research, other medium; vendor lists name it). Flag: weak

### contrarian · Contrarian
Does: breaks a belief the viewer holds, then rebuilds it with a reason.
Use when: opinion + demo, myth-bust. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `"[popular advice] is [wrong / costing you] because [one-line reason you will prove]."`
  e.g. "Stop rinsing your rice for this dish. It's why it turns to paste." / "More stretching won't fix your squat. One cue will."
Layers: spoken = belief + break / text = the belief crossed out or "myth:" label / first image = the habit being challenged.
Edit: `SMASH` from belief to proof; `INT` at the flip if later; `POP "WRONG"`.
Pairs: proof-first, number. Clashes: callout aimed at the same people you contradict (reads as an attack).
Fails when: body does not defend it; fake outrage; the belief is a straw man; everyone in the niche opens this way this month.
Evidence: MANY [7]; Made to Stick (break then repair the expectation). Debunking research (other medium): state the fact first, one myth only.

### question · Question / invitation
Does: one question the viewer needs answered, or an invitation to think about their own case.
Use when: tip, explainer, Q&A. Delivers: question / invitation.
Lines: `"Why does [specific thing] [specific failure]?"` · `"Ever [specific moment]? Here's why."`
  e.g. "Why do your cookies spread flat every time?" / "Ever hang a picture and it's crooked by morning?"
Layers: spoken = the question / text = the question in 3–6 words / first image = the failure the question names.
Edit: `CARD-L` question `@why`; `CUTAWAY @failure`; answer starts by ~3 s.
Pairs: callout, curiosity-gap. Clashes: any question that asks for a comment in the first 3 s (engagement bait).
Fails when: yes/no or rhetorical; answer arrives late; question is broad ("Want to cook better?").
Evidence: OFFICIAL via press (Meta names question/invitation) + MANY.

### curiosity-gap · Curiosity gap
Does: names a specific missing piece the viewer can almost fill, then closes it.
Use when: comparison, test, "I tried X". Delivers: question / invitation.
Lines: `"[surprising specific outcome]. [the part you wouldn't guess]."`
  e.g. "The cheap pan won. Not for the reason you think." / "This $3 hinge fixed a door that two carpenters couldn't."
Layers: spoken = the gap / text = the known half / first image = both options or the odd outcome.
Edit: `SPLIT` options; `FREEZE` on the gap; payoff `CARD-N`.
Pairs: number, proof-first. Clashes: result-first (gap already closed); mystery-box only if the payoff lands by ~50%.
Fails when: too vague (nothing to hold) or too revealing (no reason to stay); payoff missing or late.
Evidence: RESEARCH (information-gap theory; headline work shows too much or too little information both fail) + MANY [10] + OFFICIAL type.

### number · Number / stat
Does: a specific, real figure signals experience and gives something to check.
Use when: any idea with a measurable result. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `"[number] [thing] [timeframe or comparison]."`
  e.g. (made-up) "22 minutes, one pot, four dinners." / "3 cues fixed my deadlift in a week."
Layers: spoken = number in a sentence / text = the number alone, big / first image = what was counted.
Edit: `CARD-N @number` hold ≥1.5 s; `PUNCH @number`.
Pairs: result-first, value-promise, stakes. Clashes: none; weak if the number is unbelievable.
Fails when: number is invented, rounded to vagueness, or unrelated to the viewer's goal.
Evidence: MANY [6]; concreteness research (RESEARCH, other medium, see `hooks-psych.md` concreteness).

### callout · Direct call-out / name the pain
Does: names a specific situation so the right viewer feels seen and the wrong one leaves.
Use when: fixing a common mistake, troubleshooting. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `"If you [specific situation or failed attempt], [try this]."`
  e.g. "If your sourdough is still flat after three tries, check this one thing." / "Renting and can't drill? This holds a mirror."
Layers: spoken = the situation / text = who it's for, 3–5 words / first image = the pain visible.
Edit: `POP` the situation word; `CUTAWAY @pain`.
Pairs: value-promise, question, number. Clashes: contrarian aimed at the same group.
Fails when: pain is vague ("struggling with fitness"); audience too broad; label feels like an insult.
Evidence: MANY [6]; self-reference attention research (RESEARCH, lab).

### warning · Mistake / warning
Does: names a real mistake that costs the viewer something, then the fix.
Use when: "stop doing", mistakes, safety. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `"The [habit] that's quietly ruining your [thing]."` · `"Most of us [habit]. It's costing [unit]."`
  e.g. "The way you store tomatoes is making them mealy." / "Don't paint over that crack yet."
Layers: spoken = the mistake / text = `WARN` 2–4 words / first image = the mistake happening.
Edit: `WARN` style card; `HIT @mistake`; fix by ~5 s.
Pairs: stakes, number. Clashes: commands like "you must" (reactance); sad tone (sadness lowers sharing).
Fails when: the harm is invented or exaggerated; the fix is missing; "Stop." as the whole opener (worn out).
Evidence: MANY (vendor lists) + RESEARCH (negativity bias, other medium; reactance meta-analysis).

### stakes · Stakes
Does: shows what is at risk or what the habit costs, sized honestly.
Use when: workflow upgrade, time or money saver, reversal stories. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `"[current habit] costs you [unit] every [period]."` · `"[x] was going perfectly, then [y]."`
  e.g. (made-up) "Washing dishes by hand costs you about 5 hours a week." / "One loose screw and the whole shelf comes down."
Layers: spoken = the cost / text = the unit, big / first image = the loss or the risk.
Edit: `CARD-N @cost`; `TICK` if time-bound; `DROP` at the turn.
Pairs: number, warning, man-in-hole, ticking-clock. Clashes: effort-min promises that undercut the stakes.
Fails when: the loss is vague ("falling behind"); viewer cannot verify it; reads as fear-selling.
Evidence: RESEARCH (framing effect replicated, other medium); loss-size claims contested.

### mid-action · Mid-action / cold open
Does: drops the viewer into something already happening; they stay for context.
Use when: story, mistake, "I tested". Delivers: any type, after one beat.
Lines: `[mid-thought line or mid-process shot], then rewind one beat.`
  e.g. "…and that's when the whole cake slid off the board." / Open on the finished shelf wobbling, then "here's what went wrong."
Layers: spoken = the late-arriving line / text = the missing subject / first image = the moment itself.
Edit: start on motion; `JUMP` to rewind; `CARD-L` anchor `@first-word`.
Pairs: in-medias-res, man-in-hole, confession. Clashes: context-first.
Fails when: reads as a gimmick; context never comes; text does not anchor the fragment.
Evidence: SINGLE-to-few [3] (screenwriting cold opens). Flag: weak

### secret · Nobody talks about / insider
Does: implies hidden knowledge or less effort.
Use when: tips, settings, shortcuts. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `"The [setting / trick] [people in your niche] skip."`
  e.g. "The oven setting most people never touch." / "The cue coaches use that nobody explains."
Layers: spoken = the hidden thing named / text = the thing's name / first image = where it is.
Edit: `PUNCH @reveal`; `CUTAWAY` to the setting.
Pairs: number, proof-first. Clashes: proof-first, bold-claim stacked (hype).
Fails when: the "secret" is ordinary; wording sounds like hype; overused in the niche.
Evidence: SINGLE-to-few [3], vendor-tinted; the source admits it is overused. Flag: weak

### pov-setup · POV setup
Does: "POV:" label puts the viewer inside a specific scene in one word.
Use when: relatable pain, skit. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `"POV: [very specific scene]."`
  e.g. "POV: guests arrive in 10 minutes and the rice is still crunchy." / "POV: the flat-pack has one screw left over."
Layers: spoken = optional, reacts in scene / text = the POV line / first image = the scene from the viewer's eyes.
Edit: `CARD-L` POV text; camera at eye height; `CUT-M`.
Pairs: callout. Clashes: value-promise in the same beat (two framings).
Fails when: the scene is generic; nobody can tell the topic.
Evidence: VENDOR (6+, copy-like); fatigue high. Flag: weak

### visual-prop · Visual / prop
Does: an object or image does the work; the viewer asks "why that?"
Use when: giveaway, "replace this", myth-bust. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[object enters frame] + [text names it] + [line ties it to the topic].`
  e.g. Hold up a single binder clip; "This replaced my cable tray." / Drop the old recipe card in the bin; "Not anymore."
Layers: spoken = ties object to topic / text = names it / first image = the object, big, readable.
Edit: object enters ≤0.5 s; `PUNCH @object`; `SFX @event`.
Pairs: before-after, result-first. Clashes: interrupt (two oddities).
Fails when: prop unrelated to the lesson; not identifiable at phone size.
Evidence: MANY-but-vendor [4] + one brand case (see `hooks-unusual.md` prop-reveal).

### interrupt · Pattern interrupt
Does: something in frame breaks the feed rhythm, tied to the topic; the eye stops.
Use when: demo with something visible. Delivers: any type, carried by the image.
Lines: `[unexpected image or motion tied to the topic] + [line that names it].`
  e.g. Open on a frying pan flipped upside down on the stove; "Your pan's been the problem." / Screen already mid-glitch, then the fix.
Layers: spoken = names the oddity / text = the topic, plain / first image = the oddity.
Edit: `INT` at frame 1 only if the turn comes later anyway; never two in a row.
Pairs: callout, number. Clashes: visual-prop, any second odd layer (`hooks-unusual.md` G3).
Fails when: unrelated to the content; a still face would have done better (one vendor sample found face-to-camera openers held engagement best).
Evidence: MANY [9] + OFFICIAL adjacent (Meta ads: visual + audio cue in first 1–2 s; TikTok guidance).

### confession · Confession / personal stake
Does: an honest admission or risk signals credibility; the viewer wants the lesson.
Use when: review, "what I stopped using", lesson-from-mistake. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `"I [spent x / got y wrong] so you don't have to."`
  e.g. (made-up) "I bought five knife sharpeners this year. Four were a waste." / "I painted this room twice because of one mistake."
Layers: spoken = the admission / text = the cost / first image = the evidence of the mistake.
Edit: face close; `SIL` 0.3 s after the admission; `CARD-N @cost`.
Pairs: result-first (competence first), number. Clashes: secret.
Fails when: performed humility with no real number; no competence shown (pratfall needs it).
Evidence: MANY, thin [4]; pratfall research (1960s, moderated).

### context-first · Context first, then the hook
Does: one establishing line says who and what, then the provocative line.
Use when: test it only, where a stranger would be lost without context. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[one establishing shot or line] + [provocative line].`
  e.g. "I run a tiny bakery. Here's the oven trick we never tell customers." / "Physio here. Don't stretch that."
Layers: spoken = context then turn / text = the turn / first image = the setting.
Edit: context ≤1 s; `SMASH` into the turn.
Pairs: callout, secret. Clashes: mid-action.
Fails when: context takes more than ~1 s; the turn never comes.
Evidence: SINGLE vendor; contradicts most of this file. Flag: weak

### habit-break · Habit-break / "you've tried the rest"
Does: assumes prior failure and positions this as the next step.
Use when: fix for a common problem with known failed fixes. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `"You've already tried [x, y]. Here's the one you haven't."`
  e.g. "You've tried ice and heat. Try this for a stiff neck." / "Vinegar, baking soda, nothing? Try this on the drain."
Layers: spoken = the failed attempts / text = "the one you haven't" / first image = the new fix.
Edit: `CUT-F` across the failed fixes; `PUNCH @new`.
Pairs: callout, warning. Clashes: contrarian (double attack).
Fails when: the viewer hasn't tried those things; the new fix is one of them.
Evidence: SINGLE. Flag: weak

| "One of my favorite (x) is (y)" | recommendation, value promise | value-promise | (y) must be specific and shown in frame 1 |
| "I have been thinking a lot about (x)" | statement of intent, opinion setup | bold-claim / contrarian | no promise yet; the claim must land in the next line |

## Failure modes

1. A hook is execution, not strategy. It cannot fix an idea with no point, no audience, no proof.
2. Bait without payoff erodes trust. A contrarian or gap opener the body does not defend is clickbait.
3. Overuse decays shapes (repeated by many, measured by none). Check the niche this month.
4. Too much information (nothing left to learn) or too little (nothing to latch on) both fail.
5. Engagement and watch share can trade off. Read skip rate and retention separately.
6. "Most common hook" lists show popularity, not performance.
7. Nearly all Meta numbers are ads, not organic teaching Reels. Directional only.
8. "Works on every platform" claims have no data.
9. Many loud stats are circular vendor claims. Never cite one.

## Specifics, no power-word list

There is no power-word list. Weak hooks get stronger through specifics: the real number, the named tool or object, the exact time, the visible result. Replace "fast" with "11 minutes to 90 seconds" (if measured). Replace "this tool" with its name. An invented specific is a lie; use only what the creator measured or can show.
