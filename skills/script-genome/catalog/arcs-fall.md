# Arcs: fall

Family: a high point slides or crashes, and the Reel ends on a rule the viewer can use; variants differ in where the cause is shown (riches-rags: gradual decisions; icarus: a visible flaw at the peak; cautionary-tale: someone else's real story).
Shared skeleton: `[high point] [but <cause>, so <effect>] [the cost, in a number] [rule + action]`.
Evidence: no arc tested at 30–60 s; in long-text data riches-rags has the worst record (negative box-office effect in 6,174 films) and icarus is mixed (top-3 in book downloads, weak in films); sad, low-arousal endings are shared less (news articles, other medium), so the last beat must hand over something useful or end on relief, never regret.
Runtimes: 30–60 s; riches-rags and cautionary-tale work at 30 s with 4 beats; icarus needs ~30 s minimum for the held peak.

Card fields as in `arcs-setback.md`. Words per beat ≈ seconds × 2.4. All examples are made up.

### riches-rags · Riches to rags
Type: CURVE
Does: shows a peak, then the decisions that compounded into a loss, the cost as a number, and a rule. The lesson beat is mandatory.
Use when: story, news (cautionary), opinion. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 HIGH 20% (0–6 | 0–12) hook in the first 3 s · B2 SLIDE 35% (6–16.5 | 12–33) · B3 BOTTOM 25% (16.5–24 | 33–48) · B4 LESSON-AS-RULE 20% (24–30 | 48–60).
V: [+2, 0, -2, +1] `█▄▁▆`
Lines: B1 the peak, brag-light · B2 `but` + small cause, then `so` + the decision that compounded · B3 the cost as a number · B4 `which means` + rule + action; the last line hands the viewer something to do. `[I had <peak>] [but I <decision>, so <effect>, so <effect>] [now <loss number>] [rule: never <x> without <y>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "My bakery had its best month ever, all from one café client." / "So when they left, 60% of my sales left with them."
Edit: B1 CUT-M, warm frame · B2 CUT-S slows step by step, grade cools (brand) · B3 SIL + FREEZE on the loss number, CARD-N · INT at B3/B4: grade snaps back, one HIT · B4 CUT-S, face to camera, CARD-L rule.
Pairs: hooks warning, stakes, number, secret; endings send-line ("send to the person who…"), payoff-stop, comment. Clashes: result-first; curiosity-gap that promises a happy payoff.
Fails when: the fall is the creator's own fault but blamed elsewhere; no rule; ends on sadness; it is a humblebrag.
Evidence: RESEARCH (long text, other medium) [3]: large book cluster; negative box-office effect, positive critic score; sad endings shared less [news articles].

### icarus · Icarus (rise, overreach, crash)
Type: CURVE
Does: a rise, a visible overreach at the top (the cause is on screen), the crash, what is kept.
Use when: story, news. 30–60 s. Distinct from riches-rags: the flaw is shown at the peak.
Beats: B1 START 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) hook · B2 RISE 33% (3.5–13.5 | 7–27) · B3 OVERREACH 15% (13.5–18 | 27–36) the flaw line · B4 CRASH 25% (18–25.5 | 36–51) · B5 RESIDUE 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). @30: B3 is one sentence closing B2; B5 the last line of B4.
V: [0, +1, +2, -2, -1] `▄▆█▁▂` (end the residue on a usable rule to lift the last beat)
Lines: B2 `so I <win>, <win>` · B3 the overreach ("so I stopped <safeguard>") · B4 `but` + consequence · B5 what I keep. `[<start>] [so I <win>, <win>] [at the top I <cut a safeguard>] [but <crash>] [residue: what I keep doing]`
  e.g. "Third trip in, I stopped checking entry rules because the first two were easy." / "At the border I learned this one needs a visa booked weeks ahead."
Edit: B2 CUT-F, RISE · B3 held, brightest frame, SIL 0.5 s (the exhale before the drop) · INT at B3/B4: SMASH + DROP · B4 WARN, CUT-X short burst · B5 CUT-S, MUS-LOW, CARD-L.
Pairs: hooks stakes, warning, mid-action; endings send-line, comment. Clashes: bold-claim (undercuts the fall).
Fails when: no visible flaw at the peak (reads as bad luck); the incident is invented (hard fail).
Evidence: RESEARCH (long text) [3]: top-3 by book downloads; weakest across film genres; an Icarus-like arc predicted comments in a study of 1,326 short films (weak model).

### cautionary-tale · Cautionary tale (third person)
Type: CURVE
Does: tells a real, checkable event about someone else: what they wanted, the shortcut, the consequence, the rule, the check to run.
Use when: news, opinion. 30–60 s. The creator is the narrator, not the protagonist.
Beats: B1 PROTAGONIST + WANT 14% (0–4 | 0–8.5) hook · B2 SHORTCUT 20% (4–10 | 8.5–20.5) · B3 CONSEQUENCE 36% (10–21 | 20.5–42) · B4 RULE 20% (21–27 | 42–54) · B5 ACTION 10% (27–30 | 54–60). @30: B5 is the last line of B4.
V: [+1, +1, -2, 0] `▆▆▁▄` (end with the action to lift the last beat)
Lines: `so <person> <shortcut>` · `which led to`. Must be a real, checkable event. `[<person> wanted <x>] [so they <shortcut>] [which led to <real consequence>] [the rule: <rule>] [do <check>]`
  e.g. (shape only; replace with a verified event) "A hiker wanted to travel light, so they left the paper map and trusted a phone." / "The battery died at the summit."
Edit: B1 CUT-M · B3 CUTAWAY of the real headline or screenshot with source label (STEP-style label), SIL · B4 CARD-L rule · B5 face, CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks warning, stakes, number; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: curiosity-gap that makes a joke of harm.
Fails when: unverifiable or exaggerated; victim-blaming; no rule.
Evidence: CRAFT/INFERENCE [2]; narrative transportation (attachment to a protagonist raises belief, text stories, other medium). Flag: weak
