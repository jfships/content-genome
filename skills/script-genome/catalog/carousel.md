# Carousels: craft rules

One file for every skill that touches a still carousel (Instagram, LinkedIn document). script-genome owns it. idea-genome (`carousel-form.md`), brand-genome (Stage 8 §8, the look) and growth-genome (content system, repurposing) point here. Load it when planning a carousel, writing its slide words, or checking one.

Every rule is a strong default, not a law. Nobody has a study on slide count, slide-1 drop-off or words per slide. Grades: OFFICIAL (the platform said it) · VENDOR (guide, no study named) · SINGLE (one real run) · HOUSE (a number we picked). Say "weak basis" in one line when a pick rests on VENDOR, SINGLE or HOUSE. Never quote vendor percentages as fact.

## 1. The job
A carousel earns saves and sends from people who already follow, and follows from people it was sent to. It is not the discovery format: Reels reach more strangers, and small accounts often get less reach from carousels than from single images. [VENDOR + secondary datasets, unchecked]
- Ideas that work: takes, checklists, orders of steps, before/after, myths. They must hold up with no face and no sound.
- Not carousel ideas: anything that needs a moment of video to land.
- Cadence is the growth plan's call. One a week is the usual ceiling for one person.

## 2. The brand's look wins
The look comes from what the brand actually posts (brand-genome records it as the **carousel look** field). Handle, page counter, swipe cue, brand fonts and a branded last-slide card are options a brand opts into, not requirements. This file never adds one the brand's posted look leaves out. A reviewer who flags a missing handle or counter reads the carousel look field first and drops the flag if the brand chose it.
No posted carousels yet: offer the starting rules below as labeled tests and write what was chosen into the look field.

## 3. Slide plan (default 8; 6–10 allowed)
| Slide | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Cover | stop the thumb | one specific claim, number or pain; written to be sent to a type of person |
| 2 Second cover | rescue the skipper | Instagram often re-shows slide 2 to people who skipped the post [OFFICIAL via secondary summaries]. It needs its own hook and a full reason to continue, as if a stranger saw it first |
| 3 to N-2 Body | one idea each | under about 25 words; each ends with a reason to turn the page |
| N-1 Keeper | the save | see §6 |
| N Close | one ask | see §7 |

Plan before words: write one line per slide (job + the claim), get the creator's yes on the plan, then write the words. Counting: the plan is a table with slide, job, line, what leaves the next slide open.

## 4. Slide-one hook
- One claim, number or pain a stranger gets in under 2 seconds with no context. Test: read slide 1 alone, out of order. If it needs the caption, rewrite.
- Specific beats broad: a named moment ("your phone buzzes during dinner") over a topic ("business freedom").
- No question hooks that a reader answers "no" and swipes on. No "Here is what I learned" openers.
- A swipe cue on slide 1 only, and only if the brand's look uses one.

## 5. Order: pain first or take first
Default order for a take idea: pain slides first (each second line leaves something open), the take lands about two-thirds of the way in, then the way out, then keeper and ask. One real run tried take-first and the creator said it "wasn't hitting"; pain-first with the take on slide 6 of 8 worked and a fresh reviewer named that placement as right. [SINGLE] Treat as the default to test, not a rule. A checklist idea can lead with the payoff instead.
The weakest spot to watch: the drop between slides 3 and 4 (the reviewer's prediction, unmeasured). Slide 3 must say where the carousel is going.

## 6. The keeper slide
The slide people screenshot or save: an ordered list of steps, a checklist, a test, a rule of thumb. Test: could someone act on it without the rest of the carousel? If the way out is squeezed into one line on the last slides with nothing to screenshot, there is no keeper; give the steps their own slide as a short ordered line ("first X, then Y, then Z"). A carousel with a take and no keeper gets sends at best, never saves.
No invented numbers, no named clients, nothing the proof list can't back.

## 7. Last slide: one ask, by growth stage
Read the brand's growth state (`cta_allowed_now`, `cta_not_yet`, `house_bans`; house bans always win). No growth plan: use "no audience yet".
| Stage | Allowed ask on the last slide |
|---|---|
| S0 Seed (0-100, mostly friends) | an implied send line ("send this to the one who still does X"), or save if the keeper is a checklist. No follow ask, no lead offer, no keyword comment |
| S1 Traction (100-1K) | send line; follow-with-reason on series posts; save |
| S2 Growth (1K-10K) | the above, plus a real opinion question; a lead mention in the caption if a real asset exists |
| S3 Scale (10K+) | standard asks per the growth plan; an offer slide only rarely |
Exactly one ask. It points back at slide 1 or 2 when it can (a callback line makes the ask feel earned). Keep the callback plain: do not add drama or vividness the creator would not say. The caption adds a thought and never retells the slides or states something untrue yet.

## 8. Text size at feed scale
A 1080-wide slide shows about 390 pt wide on a phone, roughly a third of its size. HOUSE minimums at 1080 wide: headline 80 px, body 44 px, two sizes at most. Caption-style slides (words laid over a photo panel): a bold sans around 46 px with a dark outline about 8 px, white text, one accent-colored key word per slide, centered, at least 100 px off the sides; this read fine at phone size in one run. [SINGLE]
Check: shrink the contact sheet to 360 px wide and read every slide without zooming. Fail any line you have to squint at. Keep words off faces and off busy areas; put captions over plain parts of the picture.

## 9. 4:5 layout
- Canvas 1080x1350, one ratio for the whole carousel; never mix 1:1 and 4:5. [OFFICIAL spec]
- The profile grid shows a centered crop (sources disagree on the exact shape, check live): keep key words and faces in the middle, at least 65 px off the sides.
- Keep text out of the top 120 px and bottom 150 px (HOUSE; the app's own marks sit there).
- A split layout (two panels stacked, 1080x675 each) works with one short line per panel, each in its own panel; keep each line to one idea.
- Logo or mark, if the brand uses one, under 15% of the slide.

## 10. Swipe cues and progress marks
Options, off unless the brand's look uses them: a swipe cue on slide 1 only; a page counter ("3 / 8") on body slides. A reviewer will suggest a counter on slide 3-4 drop-off worries; that is the creator's call, ruled once, written into the look field. Lines that leave something open at the end of each slide do the same job with no extra mark.

## 11. A carousel cut from a script
For a derivative (a script already exists, or a long video): do not paste the spoken script.
1. Pick the claim or the list the script proves. Drop beats that need voice, face or a demo clip.
2. Re-hook for the page: a new slide 1 line that works with no voice; the spoken hook is raw material, not the slide.
3. One script beat with a claim becomes one slide; merge small beats; stay inside 6-10 slides.
4. The keeper is the script's payoff turned into a list or order.
5. The ask comes from the growth state (§7), never from the script's spoken ending.
6. Copy rules of the brand voice apply to the slides (house bans, no invented facts).
7. Same plan-then-words gates as a new carousel.

## 12. Checks before the look is built
- Every body slide: one idea, about 25 words or fewer.
- Slide 2 works as a first look.
- A keeper exists and is saveable.
- One ask, on the last slide, allowed by the stage.
- Nothing invented, nothing the house bans or proof list forbids.
- Caption adds, never retells.
- Look matches the brand's carousel look field.

## Measures
Sends, saves and follows per 1,000 reached, against the brand's Reels baseline, never raw views. Completion only if Insights shows it (unconfirmed for carousels). Below the growth plan's floors say "too early to read".
