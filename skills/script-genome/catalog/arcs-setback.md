# Arcs: setback, then better

Family: something goes wrong (once or more) and the end state is better than the start; variants differ in how many setbacks, whether the first fix was false, and who admits what.
Shared skeleton: `[state] [but <setback>] [worst point] [so <move>, because <reason>] [better than before + rule]`.
Evidence: no arc has been tested at 30–60 s; man-in-hole and oedipus exist as shapes in long-text data (books, film scripts, 10,000+ word windows); pratfall research (competence before failure) is the one finding that changes lines here; everything else is craft.
Runtimes: ABT-sized variants (problem-solution-twist, before-after) fit 15–30 s; man-in-hole, fail-fail-win, oedipus, lesson-learned fit 30–60 s; cinderella needs ≥ 45 s.

Card fields: Type · Beats `B# NAME pct (s@30 | s@60) job` · V = viewer's felt state per beat, -2 to +2, sparkline `▁▂▄▆█` · Lines = connectives and skeleton · Edit = codes per beat (`card/schema.md` §8 "Edit codes") · Pairs/Clashes = hook slugs (`hooks.md`; the list is in `shapes.md` "Hook slugs") and ending slugs (see `shapes.md` "Endings used in cards"; comment, tease-next, lead are stage-gated). Words per beat ≈ seconds × 2.4. All examples are made up.

### before-after · Before / after (arc)
Type: ARGUMENT/CURVE · Shape twin: before-after (`shapes.md`, after-first order)
Does: opens on the mess, shows one to three causal moves, reveals the after in the same frame, hands the viewer the move.
Use when: demo, tip, comparison; the after is visible. Use the shape twin instead when the result should be the hook.
After-first variant → `shapes.md` before-after (its order wins for teaching ideas; B1 edits here are for the before-first order).
Beats: B1 BEFORE 20% (0–6 | 0–12) mess + pain in one concrete image · B2 WORK 35% (6–16.5 | 12–33) causal moves · B3 REVEAL 25% (16.5–24 | 33–48) same frame, after state · B4 BRIDGE 20% (24–30 | 48–60) what it means + one action.
V: [-1, 0, +2, +1] `▂▄█▆`
Lines: B1 present-tense pain with a number · B2 `so` + one instruction · B3 same noun as B1 ("Same <thing>. <result>.") · B4 `which means` + what to copy. `[I used to <slow thing>; it cost <x>] [so I <move>; it <did>] [same <thing>, now <result>] [that's <metric>; copy <this>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "Our grout was black and took an hour a week to scrub." / "Same corner. One paste, ten minutes."
Edit: B1 CUT-M, cluttered frame, MUS-LOW, POP @pain word · B2 CUT-F, CUTAWAY or SCREEN, STEP labels · INT at B2/B3: SIL 0.5 s then HIT · B3 WIPE or SPLIT on the same frame as B1, hold ≥2 s, CARD-N · B4 CUT-S, face to camera, CARD-L.
Pairs: hooks proof-first, number, visual-prop, callout, warning; endings send-line, payoff-stop, loop (end on after frame, hook on before frame). Clashes: result-first, only if the hook reveals the after AND this arc's before-first order is kept (then it is in-medias-res; drop B1 or switch to the shape).
Fails when: the after is not visibly different at phone size in 2 s; B2 is a tour of every option; the before is a strawman; the result needs sound to read.
Evidence: MANY [3] craft, no data at any length; same-frame proof is INFERENCE.

### man-in-hole · Man in a hole
Type: CURVE
Does: things are fine, something breaks, the worst point is named flat, one move climbs out, the end is better than the start.
Use when: story, demo, tip; a real setback with a transferable fix. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 FINE 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) hook · B2 FALL 20% (3.5–9.5 | 7–19) · B3 BOTTOM 18% (9.5–15 | 19–30) · B4 CLIMB 32% (15–24.5 | 30–49) · B5 BETTER 18% (24.5–30 | 49–60). @30: fold B1 into the hook (open already falling) → 4 beats.
V: [+1, -1, -2, +1, +2] `▆▂▁▆█`
Lines: B1 one-line normal · B2 `but` + the specific break · B3 the worst moment, said flat, with its cost · B4 `so` + the one change, `because` + why · B5 end state better than B1, plus the rule. `[<normal>] [but <broke>] [worst: <cost>] [so I <move>, because <reason>] [now <better>; rule: <rule>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "Dinner for eight was on track until the oven died at six." / "So I cut the roast into steaks, because a hot pan does in ten minutes what the oven needed ninety for."
Edit: B1 CUT-M, MUS-LOW · B2 WARN, PUSH · B3 the held beat: SIL 0.5–1.0 s on the bottom line, FREEZE or still, no cutaway · INT at B3/B4: DROP then HIT · B4 CUT-F, RISE, CUTAWAY of the fix, STEP · B5 CUT-S, CARD-N.
Pairs: hooks stakes, warning, mid-action (open in B2/B3), pov-setup, callout; endings send-line, payoff-stop, comment, tease-next. Clashes: result-first if the hook gives away the climb (a competence flash is fine: a number, a real screen, not the final reveal); bold-claim that states the lesson up front.
Fails when: the bottom is vague or cheap (reads as complaining); B4 gives no move the viewer can copy; "better" is only "back to normal".
Evidence: RESEARCH (long text, other medium) [2]: common book cluster; only arc with a positive box-office effect in 6,174 films, but lower ratings. Unknown at 30–60 s.

### fail-fail-win · Fail, fail, win
Type: LADDER
Does: two different honest failures, each teaching the next try, an insight line, then the attempt that worked.
Use when: demo, story, tip. Needs a competence signal first (result in the hook) or the failures read as incompetence. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 GOAL + HOOK 10% (0–3 | 0–6) show the win or a result · B2 FAIL 1 18% (3–8.5 | 6–17) · B3 FAIL 2 24% (8.5–15.5 | 17–31) · B4 INSIGHT 16% (15.5–20.5 | 31–41) · B5 WIN 22% (20.5–27 | 41–54) · B6 TAKEAWAY 10% (27–30 | 54–60). @30: fail 1 as a 3 s montage inside B1/B2, fail 2, win + takeaway → 4 beats.
V: [0, -1, -2, 0, +2, +1] `▄▂▁▄█▆`
Lines: two different failures, each joined by `so`; the insight carries `because` / `which told me`. `[goal; here's the third try that worked] [try 1: <what>, failed because <x>] [so try 2: <what>, failed because <y>] [which told me <insight>] [try 3: <what>, worked] [takeaway]`
  e.g. "Try one: I ran daily and my knees quit in a week. Try two: the bike, bored in four days." / "Which told me the problem wasn't the sport. It was the schedule."
Edit: B2 CUT-F quick montage (low stakes, light) · B3 longer, WARN, SIL 0.5 s on the failure · B4 face, PUSH, POP @because · INT at B4/B5 · B5 CUT-F, CARD-N, HIT · B6 CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks proof-first, result-first ("it took three tries"), stakes; endings send-line, payoff-stop, comment. Clashes: bold-claim that says it is easy; result-first if the hook gives away the climb (a competence flash is fine).
Fails when: failures are staged or the same failure twice; no competence signal; the idea needs 60 s and only 30 s is allowed.
Evidence: RESEARCH-adjacent [1] (pratfall effect, 1966: competent person's blunder raises liking, average person's lowers it) + MANY craft.

### cinderella · Cinderella (rise, fall, bigger rise)
Type: CURVE
Does: a first win the viewer believes, a fall that reverses it, a rebuild that out-sizes the first win.
Use when: story, demo; ≥ 45 s. At 30 s it collapses into fail-fail-win.
Beats: B1 LOW 10% (n/a | 0–6) hook · B2 FIRST RISE 20% (n/a | 6–18) false win · B3 FALL 25% (n/a | 18–33) · B4 BIGGER RISE 33% (n/a | 33–53) · B5 CLOSE 12% (n/a | 53–60). At 45 s: 0–4.5 | 4.5–13.5 | 13.5–24.75 | 24.75–39.6 | 39.6–45.
V: [-1, +1, -2, +2, +2] `▂▆▁██`
Lines: B2 must be a credible win · B3 opens with `but` and reverses it · B4 `so` + rebuild, number bigger than B2's. `[<low>] [first <try> looked <great>] [but in <real use> it <broke>] [so I <rebuilt>, now <bigger result>] [close]`
  e.g. (made-up) "My first market stall sold out in two hours." / "Next week: rain, four jars sold. So I rebuilt it around pre-orders, and now I sell out before I set up."
Edit: B2 bright, CUT-M · INT at B3: DROP, one sharp cut · B4 CUT-F, RISE, CARD-N bigger than B2's · B5 CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks stakes, pov-setup, visual-prop; endings send-line, tease-next. Clashes: result-first.
Fails when: B2 is not believable as a win; B4 is not bigger than B2; under 45 s.
Evidence: RESEARCH (long text) [2]: not a top performer in books, no effect in films. Weakest evidence of the curve arcs. Flag: weak

### oedipus · False fix (fall, rise, fall, real fix)
Type: CURVE · Aliases: false-fix
Does: a problem, an obvious fix that works for a moment, a worse failure caused by that fix, then the real fix.
Use when: tip, story, news (cautionary). 30–60 s. Pure Oedipus ends low; the Reel version adds the real fix.
Beats: B1 FALL 20% (0–6 | 0–12) hook in the first 3 s · B2 FAKE FIX 25% (6–13.5 | 12–27) · B3 WORSE FALL 30% (13.5–22.5 | 27–45) · B4 REAL FIX + RULE 25% (22.5–30 | 45–60).
V: [-1, +1, -2, +1] `▂▆▁▆`
Lines: `so I tried <obvious fix>` · `it worked, until` · `which meant`. `[<problem>] [so I <fix>; it worked] [until <bigger failure caused by the fix>] [the real fix: <x>]`
  e.g. "The tap dripped, so I swapped the washer. It worked, until the tight washer cracked the old valve seat." / "The real fix: replace the cartridge, not the washer."
Edit: B1 POP @problem word · B2 brief false-victory: CUT-M, upbeat MUS-LOW · INT at B3: DROP + WARN · B4 CUT-S, CUTAWAY of the real fix, CARD-L rule.
Pairs: hooks warning, stakes, curiosity-gap; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: result-first.
Fails when: the first fix isn't believable; the second fall isn't caused by the fix.
Evidence: RESEARCH (long text) [1]: sixth basic shape in book data, top-3 by downloads. Unknown at 30–60 s. Flag: weak

### problem-solution-twist · Obvious fix fails, real fix
Type: CONTAINER · Aliases: question-answer-twist
Does: names a pain, shows what most people do, says why that fails, gives the real fix.
Use when: tip, demo. 15–45 s.
Beats: B1 PROBLEM 18% (0–5.5 | 0–11) hook · B2 OBVIOUS FIX 22% (5.5–12 | 11–24) · B3 WHY IT FAILS 22% (12–18.5 | 24–37) · B4 REAL FIX 38% (18.5–30 | 37–60) payoff; any close is the last sentence of B4.
V: [-1, 0, -1, +2] `▂▄▂█`
Lines: `most people <obvious fix>` · `but that <fails because>` · `so do this instead`. `[<pain>] [most people <fix>] [but that <fails>] [so <real fix>, shown]`
  e.g. "Your roast potatoes come out soft." / "Most people add more oil, but that steams them. Boil them first and rough them up."
Edit: B1 first image the problem · B2 CUT-M, WARN tag "common fix" · INT at B3: DROP, a clear "no" mark · B4 CUT-F demo, CARD-N result, SFX @card.
Pairs: hooks callout, warning, contrarian; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: result-first.
Fails when: nobody actually does the obvious fix; the real fix is told, not shown.
Evidence: MANY [2] craft; video-genome frameworks (question-answer-twist, PAS). Twist timing is INFERENCE.

### lesson-learned · "I was wrong"
Type: CURVE · Aliases: i-was-wrong
Does: states a belief the creator held (and the audience holds), the trigger that broke it, a short admission at the low point, the new belief with evidence, what the viewer should do.
Use when: opinion, comparison, news. Needs a public past belief and proof; tiny accounts open with competence.
Beats: B1 OLD BELIEF 15% (0–4.5 | 0–9) hook · B2 TRIGGER 23% (4.5–11.5 | 9–23) · B3 ADMISSION 14% (11.5–15.5 | 23–31) · B4 NEW BELIEF 28% (15.5–24 | 31–48) · B5 FOR YOU 20% (24–30 | 48–60). @30: B3 is one sentence closing B2 (under 5 s).
V: [+1, -1, -2, +1, +2] `▆▂▁▆█`
Lines: the admission is one short sentence at the low point ("I was wrong."); B4 `because` + evidence. `[I said <belief>] [then <trigger>] [I was wrong] [because <evidence>; now I <practice>] [so if you <situation>, do <x>]`
  e.g. "I told everyone static stretching before a run prevents injury." / "Then I read the trials and logged my own runs for a month. I was wrong."
Edit: B3 SIL 0.5–1.0 s on the admission, FREEZE, no music · INT at B3/B4: music returns · B4 CARD-N or SCREEN for evidence · B5 face, CARD-L.
Pairs: hooks contrarian (about your own past view), warning, callout; endings send-line, comment. Clashes: result-first if the hook gives away the climb (a competence flash is fine); bold-claim.
Fails when: fake humility; no earlier belief anyone saw; no new information; no competence signal.
Evidence: RESEARCH-adjacent [1] (pratfall) + MANY craft.

### confession-redemption · Confession, then repair
Type: CURVE
Does: a plain first-person admission, its cost, a turn, the repair actions, a changed behavior as the end.
Use when: story, opinion; only a real event the creator chooses to tell. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 CONFESSION 14% (0–4 | 0–8.5) hook · B2 COST 24% (4–11.5 | 8.5–23) · B3 TURN 17% (11.5–16.5 | 23–33) · B4 REPAIR 30% (16.5–25.5 | 33–51) · B5 REDEMPTION 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). @30: B5 is the last line of B4.
V: [-2, -2, -1, +1, +2] `▁▁▂▆█`
Lines: plain admission as hook; redemption is a changed behavior, not a feeling. `[I <did thing>] [it cost <x>] [then <turn>] [so now I <actions>] [the rule I follow]`
  e.g. "For a year I called all my candles hand-poured. Half weren't." / "A customer asked to watch me pour. Now every label says which ones I make by hand."
Edit: B1 face, no music, POP · B2 SIL 0.5 s on the cost · INT at B3: music returns · B4 STEP list of actions, CUT-M · B5 CARD-L rule.
Pairs: hooks warning, stakes, secret; endings send-line, comment. Clashes: bold-claim; result-first if the hook gives away the climb (a competence flash is fine).
Fails when: the confession is a humblebrag; the harm is trivial; it ends sad with no action (low-arousal endings are shared less); the event is invented (hard fail).
Evidence: RESEARCH-adjacent [2] (pratfall; sadness shared less, news articles, other medium) + INFERENCE. Flag: weak
