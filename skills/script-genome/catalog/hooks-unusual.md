# Hooks: unusual openings (N1–N36)

Load when the creator wants something other than a standard opener, or when a run of similar hooks needs variety. Every shape here is a different **delivery** of one of Meta's three hook types (value promise, statement of intent, question / invitation); it never replaces one. Standard shapes: `hooks.md`. Mechanisms: `hooks-psych.md`.

## Read first

- Instagram frames every unusual hook must still fit: grab in the first 3 s; 9:16 with text off most of the screen; no watermark, blur or low quality.
- Sound is a weak first layer on Instagram (about half of scrollers reportedly have sound off). Any sound-led or silent hook needs a first image + screen text that carry the topic alone.
- No Instagram retention data exists for any shape below. Most numeric claims online are vendor claims with no method.
- Every unusual hook passes the safety gate G1–G7 below before it goes in a script.
- `Effort:` = shoot and edit cost for one solo creator. `Fatigue:` = how worn out the shape is in feeds now (mostly INFERENCE; re-check the niche).
- Example lines are made up; numbers in them are invented. Real-world examples are named only where the research cites a source; they are brand-scale or long-form unless stated, so none prove transfer to a small account.
- Layers: S = spoken · T = screen text · I = first image · A = non-speech audio.

---

## Group A. Sound-led

### silent-open · N1 Wordless gesture open
Does: 1–2 s with no voice; a face or hand reacts to a visible "hard way", then the simple fix. Reads with sound off, across languages.
Use when: "stop doing it the hard way", comparison, shortcut. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[0–1.5 s: no speech; gesture + visible hard way] → [T states topic] → [fix shown] → [speech starts]`
  e.g. A sink full of pans, creator deadpan, one eyebrow up, drops a dishwasher tab in a soak tray; T: "Same pans. No scrubbing." / A tangled drawer of cables; shrug; one clip-strip; T: "I stopped doing this part."
Layers: I + T carry it; S absent at first.
Edit: `SIL` 0–1.5 s; `POP` topic `@gesture`; speech enters with `J`.
Effort: low. Fatigue: low (screen or object version); high if copying a famous creator's exact gesture.
Fails when: topic abstract (no visible hard way); no comic timing; T missing.
Evidence: SINGLE creator (Khaby Lame, silent "simple way" replies). Flag: weak

### sound-first · N2 Sound cue leads the picture
Does: a distinct sound 0.3–0.7 s before its picture; the ear predicts, the eye confirms (J-cut logic; trailer "telegraphing sound").
Use when: demo, reveal, "tip of the day". Delivers: any type.
Lines: `[sound cue over a clear first image] → [image reveals the cause] → [line names topic]`
  e.g. Oven timer "ding"; I: a tray of perfectly even cookies; S: "Two changes. Paper, and the rack." / Drill whir over black + T "Hole #1"; cut to a straight shelf.
Layers: A first, I second, T backup, S third.
Edit: `J` 0.3–0.7 s; `SFX @event`; I must stand alone muted.
Effort: low. Fatigue: low–med.
Fails when: sound off and the image says nothing; sound unrelated; trending audio unavailable.
Evidence: VENDOR + one practitioner quote (trailer editor) + Mosseri names audio as a hook channel. No Reels data. Flag: weak

### asmr-open · N3 Tactile / ASMR open
Does: close, textured sound (taps, crinkles, whisper) triggers attention and calm in people who respond to it. Not everyone does.
Use when: calm "how I work", setting tips, recipe prep. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[close-mic texture + extreme close-up] → [whisper or normal line names topic] → [payoff]`
  e.g. Knife through a crusty loaf, close mic; T: "The bread knife trick." / Three taps on a jar lid; whisper: "One way to open any jar."
Layers: A + I; T needed for sound-off.
Effort: low–med (quiet room, close mic). Fatigue: high for AI glass-fruit ASMR; low for honest prep sounds.
Fails when: sound off; noisy room; whisper clashes with a teaching voice; some viewers dislike mouth sounds.
Evidence: RESEARCH thin and general (ASMR survey, imaging; only some people respond); short-video use SINGLE/VENDOR. Real example: AI glass-fruit ASMR clips (vendor-reported). Flag: weak

### cut-to-silence · N4 Beat of silence
Does: a loud bed stops dead at the claim; the contrast spikes attention.
Use when: "replace the clutter", consolidation, myth-bust. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[0–1.2 s busy bed] → [hard cut to silence + claim in T and S]`
  e.g. Music loud, ten jars of spices tumbling; silence; "You need four." / Upbeat track, then silence: "Throw out this sponge."
Layers: A (contrast) + I + T.
Edit: `DROP @claim`; `SIL` ≤1 s (the Reel's one held pause); I must show the change too.
Effort: low. Fatigue: low.
Fails when: sound off (silence invisible); the bed itself is slow.
Evidence: VENDOR + practitioner (trailer comic drops; sound-to-feeling maps). Real examples: a Netflix trailer dropping music for comic effect; the A Quiet Place teaser. Flag: weak

### false-start · N5 Interruption / false start
Does: an opening line gets cut off by a second voice, a text bubble or yourself; the viewer wants the answer.
Use when: tip, myth-bust, review. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[normal opening] → [interruption within 1.5 s] → [reply carries topic]`
  e.g. "Today I'll show you how to…" / T bubble: "Nobody asked for an intro." / "Fine. Salt first." / Off-screen: "That's not how you hold a hammer." "…thanks."
Layers: S (two voices) + T speaker labels.
Effort: low (self-interrupt) to med (second person). Fatigue: med.
Fails when: interruption carries no information; speaker unclear; sound off with no labels; reads as unprepared.
Ethics: a staged "second person" presented as real is a fake record; label skits.
Evidence: SINGLE brand case (Old Spice "Interruption", 2015) + pratfall research (slips help only the visibly competent). Flag: weak

## Group B. Text and picture-led

### word-reveal · N6 Text-only / word-by-word reveal
Does: 3–6 words appear one at a time on a plain frame; reading rhythm builds anticipation, fully silent.
Use when: lists, claims, rules. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[plain frame; 3–6 words in sequence over ~1.2 s] → [voice on last word or cut to demo]`
  e.g. "I / stopped / buying / bread." then the loaf. / "Three moves. No gym. Ten minutes." (made-up) then proof.
Layers: T carries it; I minimal; S optional.
Edit: `POP` per word ≤1.5 s total; then `SMASH` to demo.
Effort: low. Fatigue: med–high (word-pop text everywhere).
Fails when: reveal >1.5 s; text small or fills the screen; no face (Instagram pushes "who says it").
Evidence: text-on-screen hook is an OFFICIAL category; the word-by-word reveal itself INFERENCE. Flag: weak

### title-card · N7 Title card / stinger
Does: a 0.5–1 s hard title card with a hit sound states the premise like a trailer.
Use when: roundups, series, weekly formats. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `[0.6 s card: 3–6 topic words + hit] → [cut to action] → [line]`
  e.g. Card "KITCHEN TOOLS I ACTUALLY USE" (hit) → cut to the drawer. / "This week. One wall. One weekend."
Layers: T + A; I is the card.
Edit: `CARD-L` + `HIT`; card ≤1 s; never a logo.
Effort: low. Fatigue: med.
Fails when: it's a logo or slow intro; card has no topic words; reads as a bumper.
Evidence: VENDOR + practitioner [4] (TikTok warns against logo reveals; trailer editors hold logos back). Flag: weak

### prop-reveal · N8 Object / prop reveal
Does: a physical object enters frame and the viewer asks "why that?"; the object is the metaphor or the proof. Variant: physically throwing out the old method.
Use when: giveaways, "replace this", myth-bust. Delivers: statement of intent. Standard twin: `visual-prop`.
Lines: `[object enters ≤0.5 s] → [T names it] → [S ties it to topic]`
  e.g. Hold up one binder clip; T: "Cable fix, 10 cents." / Drop a stack of takeout menus in the bin; T: "Replaced by one list."
Layers: I + T; S third.
Effort: low. Fatigue: low–med.
Fails when: prop unrelated to the lesson; not identifiable at phone size. Weak fit for software topics unless the prop stands for the old way.
Evidence: MANY but vendor [4] + one brand case: Blendtec "Will It Blend?" (cheap, short, object-led; self-reported sales lift).

### flip-reveal · N9 In-camera flip / whip / quick zoom
Does: motion in the first 0.3–0.8 s, then the result revealed. Instagram lists "creative transitions" as a surprise tool.
Use when: reveals, before/after, results. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `[selfie cam mid-sentence] → [flip or whip to the work] → [result visible]`
  e.g. "…and that's the part nobody sands." flip to the tabletop. / Fast push-in on the finished cake; T: "Recipe in 3 steps."
Layers: I (motion) + S.
Edit: `RAMP` or whip ≤0.8 s; `PUSH` into result.
Effort: low–med (practice, two takes). Fatigue: med.
Fails when: motion makes the reveal unreadable; shaky reads as low quality.
Evidence: VENDOR [3] + weak OFFICIAL (Instagram tips, secondary). Flag: weak

### odd-frame · N10 Odd first frame / visual metaphor
Does: a strange first frame forces "what is that?", decoded within ~2 s as a metaphor for the topic.
Use when: before/after, pain-to-fix. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[odd frame] → [T names topic] → [pull back or voice explains]`
  e.g. A cereal bowl full of loose screws; T: "My toolbox, before this." Pull back to the organizer. / 40 receipts collapsing into one.
Layers: I + T.
Edit: `PUSH` out to decode; decode ≤2 s.
Effort: low. Fatigue: low–med.
Fails when: not decoded within ~2 s; looks random (that is the bad kind of `interrupt`).
Evidence: VENDOR [3] + weak OFFICIAL (Google ABCD: surprising images break clutter). Flag: weak

### lo-fi-open · N11 Lo-fi / unstaged first frame
Does: unpolished staging reads as real in a polished feed. Mosseri: "imperfection becomes a signal", rawness "is proof". Unstaged, not technically bad.
Use when: first try, "I just tried", honest review. Delivers: any type.
Lines: `[handheld, natural light, mid-action; keep the adjust or laugh] → [first line states the finding]`
  e.g. Phone propped on a flour bag, mid-adjust: "Okay, this just worked and I need to show you." / Raw shot with a dog wandering in: "Real run, no edit."
Layers: I + S.
Edit: `CUT-S`; no polish moves in the first 2 s.
Effort: lowest. Fatigue: med, rising (Mosseri expects raw to be imitated).
Fails when: blurry, dark or watermarked (Instagram says don't); topic unclear.
Evidence: OFFICIAL-ish (Mosseri year-end memo) + VENDOR [4]. Real example: Duolingo's lo-fi "Dead Duo" campaign (growth figure vendor-reported).

## Group C. Narrative position

### reaction-first · N12 Open on the reaction
Does: a face reacting to something off-screen; viewers read meaning into the face and stay for the cause (Kuleshov effect).
Use when: result demo, "I tried", case study. Delivers: question / invitation.
Lines: `[0–1 s real reaction] → [T: what happened] → [cut to cause] → [explain]`
  e.g. Face mid "no way" at the oven window; T: "It rose in 40 minutes." / A friend tastes, nods slowly; T: "Their face when the sauce worked."
Layers: I + T.
Edit: face close; `SMASH @cause` ≤3 s; `SFX` light.
Effort: low (real reaction, second take allowed). Fatigue: med.
Fails when: acted and obvious; cause not shown within ~3 s; topic not in T.
Ethics: real reactions only.
Evidence: RESEARCH (Kuleshov: original work, one 1992 replication failed, 2006 and 2016 tests support) + VENDOR [3].

### mid-thought · N13 Open on the end of a sentence
Does: the first words are the second half of a thought; the viewer feels late to something useful. "Arrive late, leave early."
Use when: tips, settings, opinions. Delivers: statement of intent. Standard twin: `mid-action` (physical; this one is verbal).
Lines: `[second half of a sentence] → [T supplies the missing subject] → [continue]`
  e.g. "…which is why I stopped preheating the pan empty." T: "Stop dry-heating nonstick." / "…and that's the second time this knot saved my tent."
Layers: S + T (T must anchor the fragment).
Edit: cut in on a natural phrase; `CARD-L` anchor `@first-word`.
Effort: low. Fatigue: low.
Fails when: fragment vague and T doesn't anchor it; sounds like a bad cut.
Evidence: MANY for the principle (screenwriting) + SINGLE for Reels (one vendor line). Flag: weak

### mid-conversation · N14 Mid-conversation open
Does: drops the viewer into a live exchange they need the rest of to understand.
Use when: interview clips, Q&A, myth-bust. Delivers: question / invitation.
Lines: `[two voices already talking; one line of conflict] → [T names topic] → [you step in]`
  e.g. Voice memo: "Wait, you fixed that with a coat hanger?" "Yes. Watch." / Overheard: "She says you never rinse pasta."
Layers: S (two voices) + T.
Effort: med (second person, consent). Fatigue: low–med.
Fails when: clip too long to land in 3 s; voices indistinguishable; clip gives the answer away.
Ethics: staged "real conversation" shown as real is a fake record; get consent from anyone recorded.
Evidence: SINGLE-type practitioner guidance (podcast cold opens). Real examples: The Artificial Intelligence Show and Diary of a CEO open on interview clips. Flag: weak

### fake-out · N15 Honest fake-out (smash cut)
Aliases: smash-cut, gilligan-cut
Does: a firm stated position, then a hard cut to the opposite; the flip is the hook, paid off inside the Reel.
Use when: changed opinions, reviews, "I was wrong", myth-bust. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[firm claim, 1 s] → [SMASH to contradicting proof, 1 s] → [what changed, by 10 s] → [truthful ending]`
  e.g. "I will never own an air fryer." SMASH "Day 30 of air-frying everything." / "This drill is garbage." … "…unless you change this one setting."
Layers: S + I + T.
Edit: `SMASH @flip`; `HIT` on the cut; `CARD-L` "Day 30" style label.
Effort: low (two clips). Fatigue: low–med; the "I'd never… Day 30" wording is a known template, vary it.
Fails when: you never held the first stance; payoff late; flip unrelated (rickroll pattern).
Evidence: MANY craft [4], zero Reels data. Real examples: This Is Us cold open (subverted expectation); the smash cut in Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade; the rickroll as the failure case.

### anti-hook · N16 Anti-hook / slow open
Does: sets up an expected hook and declines it, or opens slow and quiet.
Use when: almost never; only a meta-humor series for people who already follow. Delivers: none on its own.
Lines: `[still frame; T "this is a slow start"] → [turn within ~2 s]`
  e.g. Creator stares 1.5 s; T: "Hook goes here." then delivers. / Loading bar labeled "Hook" jumps to 100%.
Layers: I + T.
Effort: none. Fatigue: low (rarely used, for good reason).
Fails when: almost always on a small account; strangers have no reason to wait.
Evidence: counter-evidence OFFICIAL (Instagram first 3 s; Google >2 frames in 5 s; TikTok against slow openers); support SINGLE. Keep only N4's ≤1.5 s quiet-then-loud. Flag: weak

### wait-for-it · N17 Delayed payoff with a visible process
Does: states what will happen, then makes the viewer wait for it with a visible process and mini-twists.
Use when: build-in-front-of-you demos, transformations, tests. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `[T: the promised end state, or visible progress] → [fast middle with mini-twists] → [payoff exactly as promised]`
  e.g. T: "Bare wall to gallery wall. Watch the frames fill." / "By the end, this rusty pan looks new."
Layers: T + I; S supports.
Edit: `STEP` progress label; `CUT-M`; payoff `CARD-N` or `WIPE`.
Effort: low–med. Fatigue: high for the bare phrase "wait for it"; low with a visible progress mechanism.
Fails when: payoff smaller than the promise; wait too long for 20–60 s; cue is only "wait for it".
Ethics: withholding with no payoff is clickbait.
Evidence: MANY for delayed payoff in long form; none at Reels length. Real examples (long-form ads): Cadbury "Gorilla", Apple "1984", "Dumb Ways to Die". In a Reel the delay must be far shorter.

### news-tease · N18 News-style tease
Does: anchor-style framing ("New today:") signals novelty and stakes and holds back one concrete nugget.
Use when: launches, updates, "what changed". Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `[lower-third "NEW TODAY"] → [concrete fact + held-back nugget] → [details]`
  e.g. Lower-third "NEW THIS WEEK"; "The library now lends tools for free. The catch is the waitlist." / A free app just made budgeting one tap. The catch is in the settings.
Layers: T + S.
Edit: `CARD-L` lower third; nugget revealed by ~50%.
Effort: low. Fatigue: low.
Fails when: nothing is new; urgency fake; nugget never revealed.
Ethics: don't copy a real outlet's logo or style.
Evidence: SINGLE craft source (honest news teases). Flag: weak

### standup-frame · N19 Stand-up frame, then the turn
Does: a one-line frame orients, then a surprising turn within 3–4 s (clarity, connection, then punchline).
Use when: humor-led teaching, opinion. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[one-line frame naming topic + angle] → [turn by 3–4 s] → [teaching]`
  e.g. "There are two kinds of home cooks: the ones who measure, and the ones whose cakes sink." / "A level is just a very honest friend."
Layers: S + T.
Effort: low (writing). Fatigue: low.
Fails when: setup >3 s; topic unclear; the joke needs timing not yet built.
Evidence: SINGLE (comedy writer's essay). Flag: weak

## Group D. Format and character

### format-mismatch · N20 Serious advice in an absurd format
Does: the setting doesn't match the content (a lesson as a flight-safety announcement); instant curiosity.
Use when: humor-led series. Delivers: any type.
Lines: `[recognisable genre cue in frame 1] → [T: real topic] → [lesson in that genre's voice]`
  e.g. Flight-attendant voice: "In the event of a soggy pizza, reheat in a pan." T: "Leftover fix, 20 seconds." / Nature-documentary narration over a sourdough starter.
Layers: I (cue) + S.
Effort: med–high per episode. Fatigue: med.
Fails when: gimmick overshadows the lesson; topic named late.
Evidence: INFERENCE with adjacent cases: AI Bigfoot vlogs, Dollar Shave Club's launch video. Flag: weak

### skit-character · N21 Skit / recurring character
Does: an instantly legible character makes second one entertaining; recurring characters build recognition.
Use when: pain-point teaching, series. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[character cue in frame 1] → [character voices the pain] → [you answer]`
  e.g. Creator in a cap as "Landlord Larry" sighing at a dripping tap; T: "Larry's last repair call." / Creator plays a picky diner: "Can it be crispier?"
Layers: I + S.
Effort: med–high. Fatigue: low–med for original characters.
Fails when: nobody knows the character yet; frame 1 not self-explanatory.
Evidence: MANY at brand scale [5], none for small creators. Real examples: Duolingo's Duo skits; Old Spice 2010 direct-address ad.

### split-debate · N22 Two voices / split-screen debate
Does: two labeled positions in frame 1 promise a verdict.
Use when: comparisons, "A vs B". Delivers: question / invitation.
Lines: `[split, each side labeled] → [one-line clash] → [verdict]`
  e.g. Left "Me, cast iron" vs Right "Me, nonstick" arguing over one egg. / Angel and devil: "Buy the cheap drill." "Buy the good one."
Layers: I (split) + T (color labels) + S.
Edit: `SPLIT`; labels in contrasting colors; verdict `CARD-L`.
Effort: med (record twice). Fatigue: low–med.
Fails when: unclear who speaks; no verdict.
Evidence: INFERENCE (Instagram Remix layout is split-screen; no direct source). Flag: weak

### trend-hijack · N23 Trend / meme format
Does: a familiar audio or template tells the genre instantly; your topic is the twist.
Use when: reaching non-followers. Delivers: any type.
Lines: `[trend cue] → [topic slotted into the template] → [real teaching]`
  e.g. A trending "me vs me" format showing hand-whisking vs a stand mixer. / A trending sound over a before/after paint job.
Layers: A + I.
Effort: med (trend watching, fast turnaround). Fatigue: high, short half-life.
Fails when: trend dead by posting; works only with audio on; topic lost in the template.
Evidence: SINGLE test (Kapwing: one trend Trial Reel got more views than its regular twin, a product Reel got no gain; one account, one pair). Flag: weak

### pov-open · N24 POV open
Does: "POV:" puts the viewer inside a scene. Card lives in `hooks.md` as `pov-setup`.
Effort: none. Fatigue: high (in nearly every vendor list).
Evidence: VENDOR (6+, copy-like). Flag: weak

### permission-grant · N25 Permission grant / "feels illegal to know"
Does: "This is your sign to stop X" licenses dropping a habit; "feels illegal" implies hidden knowledge.
Use when: shortcut tips. Delivers: value promise. Standard twin: `secret`.
Lines: `[one-line permission or forbidden-knowledge claim] → [the thing] → [proof]`
  e.g. "This is your sign to stop peeling ginger with a knife." / "This laundry setting feels illegal to know."
Layers: S + T.
Effort: none. Fatigue: med–high.
Fails when: content is ordinary and "illegal" exaggerates it (misrepresentation).
Evidence: VENDOR only [3]. Flag: weak

## Group E. Process-native (screen, hands, tool)

### screen-first · N26 Process-first open
Aliases: screen-recording-first
Does: frame 1 is the work mid-action (screen recording, hands, overhead); the proof is the hook, face joins later.
Use when: demos, how-tos. Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `[process mid-action or at result] → [T: result + time] → [face or voice joins]`
  e.g. Overhead: dough already folding itself in the mixer; T: "No-knead. 5 minutes." / Screen recording, export bar at 80%; T: "Free editor. 40 seconds."
Layers: I + T; S second.
Edit: `SCREEN` or overhead; `PIP` face joins by ~3 s.
Effort: low. Fatigue: med.
Fails when: detail too small at phone size; no face (Google: show people early); result unclear; overlay crowds the screen.
Evidence: VENDOR [4] + weak OFFICIAL that partly conflicts (Google ABCD). Test against face-first (T2). Flag: weak

### tool-glitch · N27 The tool misbehaves
Does: the tool or interface does something unexpected in frame 1 (error, jam, wrong output); a visual question.
Use when: troubleshooting, hidden settings. Delivers: question / invitation.
Lines: `[real anomaly 0–1 s] → [T or S names it] → [fix]`
  e.g. The tape measure won't retract; T: "One screw fixes this." / The printer spits a blank page, then the one setting that fixes it.
Layers: I + T.
Effort: low (needs a real recording). Fatigue: low.
Fails when: anomaly staged to look real; unreadable.
Ethics: fabricated tool output shown as real is a fake record.
Evidence: INFERENCE. Flag: weak

### side-by-side · N28 Method reveal / side-by-side outputs
Aliases: prompt-reveal, i-asked-ai
Does: an experiment frame with a visible comparison promises a verdict and shows the method viewers want to copy.
Use when: tests, comparisons, case studies. Delivers: question / invitation. Standard twin: `proof-first` (this adds a visible A/B).
Lines: `[split: method A vs B, or the exact method on screen] → [T: what differs] → [result] → [verdict]`
  e.g. "Same dough. Different flour." split of two crumbs. / "Same prompt, two tools. Here's what each got wrong."
Layers: I + T.
Edit: `SPLIT`; `CARD-N` verdict; `SFX @verdict`.
Effort: low. Fatigue: high for "I asked ChatGPT to…" wording; low–med for honest A/B.
Fails when: cherry-picked or edited results; boring outcome.
Evidence: VENDOR [4]. Flag: weak

### impossible-shot · N29 AI-made visual as the hook
Does: a visually impossible AI-generated first 2 s is novel by construction.
Use when: video-tool tutorials, "what this model can do". Delivers: value promise.
Lines: `[AI clip 0–2 s] → [T: "Made with one prompt" + AI label] → [show the prompt] → [teach]`
  e.g. Camera dives into a teacup and lands in a garden; T: "AI clip. Prompt below." / A talking loaf gives a one-line baking tip, labeled AI.
Layers: I (clip) + T.
Effort: med (generation cost and iteration). Fatigue: high (2025–26 wave).
Fails when: viewers think it's real and feel tricked; clip unrelated to the lesson.
Ethics: label AI visuals in text; a teaching account loses trust if the first image fakes reality.
Evidence: SINGLE/VENDOR. Real examples (vendor-reported): AI glass-fruit ASMR, Bigfoot vlogs, "Fruit Love Island". Flag: weak

## Group F. Viewer involvement and response

### timer · N30 Countdown / constraint on screen
Does: a visible clock or limit makes the cost of watching small and bounded.
Use when: speed runs, lists. Delivers: value promise. Arc twin: `ticking-clock`.
Lines: `[timer or constraint in frame 1] → [content that obeys it] → [timer ends on the payoff]`
  e.g. Timer 0:60: "Breakfast, lunch, snack. One minute." / "Fix a squeaky door in 3 moves" with a move counter.
Layers: T + I.
Edit: `TICK` + on-screen timer; payoff lands at 0.
Effort: low. Fatigue: med.
Fails when: content overruns the timer; timer fake.
Evidence: VENDOR [3] + weak RESEARCH (goal gradient, mostly animal evidence). Flag: weak

### mental-challenge · N31 Challenge kept in the head
Does: a task in the first seconds (spot the difference, guess) makes the viewer an active participant; the answer stays in the video.
Use when: recognition, skill checks. Delivers: question / invitation (Meta's mental version).
Lines: `[challenge T + two options] → [~3 s pause] → [answer in the video]`
  e.g. Two steaks: "One rested. Which?" / "Pause: what's wrong with this shelf?" (answer in 5 s)
Layers: T + I.
Edit: `SPLIT`; `TICK` during the pause; `CARD-L` answer.
Effort: low. Fatigue: low–med.
Fails when: answer is in the comments; asks for a vote, tag or off-platform action.
Evidence: VENDOR + OFFICIAL on the policy side (bait rules; Meta's question/invitation type supports the mental version). Flag: weak

### comment-reply · N32 Reply to a real comment
Does: frame 1 shows a real viewer's question; proves demand and states the topic at once. Instagram's reply-with-Reel sticker supports it.
Use when: Q&A, myth-bust, objections. Delivers: question / invitation.
Lines: `[reply sticker or message screenshot] → [one-line answer promise] → [answer]`
  e.g. Sticker: "Does this work with frozen berries?" S: "Yes. Here's how." / Message: "How do you hang shelves without studs?" S: "Three anchors."
Layers: T + I (sticker); S second.
Effort: low (needs real comments; a small account may have few). Fatigue: low–med.
Fails when: the question is niche to one person; comments invented.
Ethics: real comments only; blur names.
Evidence: OFFICIAL for the feature (launched Dec 2021); SINGLE brand case (Duolingo replies in character). Flag: weak

### remix-reply · N33 Remix / response open
Does: opens on someone else's clip, then your answer (Instagram Remix: split screen with the original).
Use when: correction, "better way". Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[a few seconds of the source clip] → [your answer] → [your result]`
  e.g. Remix a 12-step "life hack" with the 2-step version. / Respond to "you need a $300 mixer" with a wooden spoon.
Layers: I (source) + S.
Effort: med (Remix-enabled source, etiquette). Fatigue: med.
Fails when: response is mean; source is a watermarked repost.
Ethics: use Remix or Collab with your own commentary; never a straight repost (originality rules).
Evidence: SINGLE creator (duets/stitches) + OFFICIAL feature facts. Flag: weak

### reverse-psych · N34 Reverse psychology ("don't watch this")
Does: forbidding something raises desire for it (reactance), and can boomerang.
Use when: not as a default. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[short prohibition aimed at a self-identified group] → [the thing] → [payoff]`
  e.g. "Don't watch this if you love scrubbing pans." / "Please don't use this cheap trick."
Layers: S + T.
Effort: none. Fatigue: high ("Stop scrolling if…").
Fails when: viewers sense manipulation; the threat is arbitrary.
Evidence: RESEARCH on reactance (other medium) + one warning-label study via secondary; Reels use INFERENCE. Flag: weak

### algorithm-talk · N35 Fourth wall about the algorithm
Does: talks about the platform as if the viewer is in on a secret.
Use when: only platform-news content, never as a plea. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[line about the platform] → [content]`
  e.g. "If this reached you, here's what changed in Reels this month." / "Here's the rule that changed in October."
Layers: S.
Effort: none. Fatigue: high.
Fails when: it works as a plea for shares or comments (engagement bait per Instagram).
Evidence: INFERENCE; policy side OFFICIAL via press. Flag: weak

### one-word · N36 One-word open
Does: the shortest hook ("Wrong." "Free."), voice and big text together.
Use when: bold-claim variants, quick tips. Delivers: statement of intent.
Lines: `[one word, S + T, 0.5 s] → [sentence that completes it]`
  e.g. "Wrong." + T "You're boiling eggs wrong." / "Free." + T "The tool library is free."
Layers: S + T.
Edit: `CARD-L` big word; `HIT`; completion by 1.5 s.
Effort: none. Fatigue: high for "Stop."; med otherwise.
Fails when: the word has no referent; it's "Stop.".
Evidence: INFERENCE (vendor advice leans to one 8–15-word sentence). Flag: weak

## Shortlist for a small solo teaching account

Ranked by fit × low effort × low policy risk × evidence. Ranking is INFERENCE.

1. fake-out (N15): cheapest to shoot, clear payoff, low risk.
2. silent-open (N1) over a process shot: works with sound off.
3. reaction-first (N12): real reactions only.
4. comment-reply (N32): real comments only; the only one with a first-party feature.
5. sound-first (N2) with a first image that stands alone.
6. lo-fi-open (N11): unstaged, never blurry.
7. side-by-side (N28): the most native to teaching by comparison.
8. screen-first (N26): test against face-first.
9. timer (N30).
10. mid-thought (N13) with a text anchor.

Avoid as defaults: reverse-psych (N34), algorithm-talk (N35), anti-hook (N16), one-word "Stop." (N36), mental-challenge versions that ask for comments or tags (N31).

## Safety gate (every unusual hook; all seven must be yes)

G1. Stranger test. Freeze 1 s after the odd moment lands (~2 s in), sound off. Can a stranger say what it's about and who it's for? If not, add one line of text or change the first image.
G2. Payoff test. The body pays the exact promise (the flip resolves, the cause is shown, the wait ends in the promised thing, the prop matters), inside the Reel, ideally in the first half.
G3. One odd layer. Only one layer (voice, text or image) is strange; another states the topic plainly.
G4. Sound-off pass. Topic carried by text and image without sound.
G5. Honesty pass. Every claim, reaction, comment and output is real. Skits and AI visuals are labeled.
G6. No-ask pass. The first 3 s ask for no comment, tag, share, word, number or emoji.
G7. Fatigue pass. Not this month's overused shape (POV, "I asked ChatGPT", AI glass ASMR, "Stop scrolling"). If it is, change the angle or test it against a control.

Fast rule: **Weird in one layer, plain in another, paid off by 10 seconds, nothing asked.**

## Clash list (unusual hook + Instagram guidance)

C1. Unusual hook + "comment WORD for the guide" in the first seconds: Instagram named this as non-recommendable, then softened for open-ended prompts. Line unclear: treat as risk; keep keyword offers out of the hook (and see `endings.md` stage-aware asks).
C2. Vote-bait ("A or B in the comments", "like for option 1"). Keep the choice mental, answer in the video.
C3. "Share to find out who this is about" fake-outs: counted as bait. A situational send line is allowed (see `endings.md`).
C4. Fake-out or tease with no payoff, or a payoff unrelated to the lure (rickroll pattern): clickbait.
C5. Reverse psychology or algorithm talk used to push engagement: bait if the goal is engagement count.
C6. Text-only hooks that fill the screen: Instagram says keep text off most of it.
C7. Lo-fi done as blur or low quality: Instagram says don't post it; "raw" means unstaged.
C8. Remix or response built on a watermarked clip or straight repost: originality rules limit reach.
C9. Fake comments, DMs, reactions, staged "real" conversations, invented tool outputs: fake records (hard fail in `check/gates.md`).
C10. Hook Reels far beyond the format: Instagram sources conflict (90 s sweet spot vs up to 3 min); 20–60 s is safe either way.
C11. AI visuals presented as real: label them; current label rules unverified.
C12. Sound-led hooks on trending audio you may not be licensed to use (business-account licensing unverified).

## Trial Reels test pairs

Facts to re-check in the app: Trial Reels show to non-followers only; eligibility sources conflict (all professional accounts vs ~1,000 followers); metrics after ~24 h; auto-share threshold unpublished.

Protocol: same body, caption and cover; change only the first 1–3 s. Compare Trial to Trial, never Trial to a regular post. Pick the metric before posting. Read at ~72 h: skip rate, average watch time vs length, retention cliff at the hook, sends and saves per view. At a few hundred views one pair is noise: 3–4 same-direction pairs = a lean, 6+ = evidence (see `learn/log.md`). Vendor kill thresholds (e.g. "replace if 3-s hold under ~20%") have no method; sanity check only.

| T1 | spoken bold-claim | silent-open + text |
| T2 | face-first | screen-first |
