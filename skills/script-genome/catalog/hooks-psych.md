# Hooks: psychological mechanisms

Load when a hook from `hooks.md` feels flat and you need the reason a line might work, or when checking a hook's honesty. A mechanism is the why; the standard shape in `hooks.md` is the how. Each card names the shape(s) it is delivered through.

## Read first

- No mechanism here has been tested on Instagram Reels. Evidence comes from news articles, tweets, lab studies (often students, hypothetical choices), ads and online lectures. Every card is a hypothesis to test on the creator's own Reels. Lab effects shrink in a feed.
- `Evidence:` grades the mechanism itself, with replication status. The hook form built on it is INFERENCE on every card unless stated.
- `Ethics:` is part of the card. A hook that breaks its ethics line fails `check/gates.md` (accuracy) whatever it does for views.
- Instagram does not recommend content that explicitly asks for comments, shares, tags or a specific word/emoji (stated May 2024, then softened for open-ended questions). Nothing in the first 3 s asks the viewer to act.
- Small-account limits: no own numbers to cite (social proof, authority), no "us" yet (tribalism), competence must be proven inside the Reel (pratfall).
- Layers: SL = spoken line · OT = on-screen text · FI = first image. Examples are made up; numbers in them are invented.

### info-gap · Curiosity / information gap
Does: the viewer notices a specific hole in what they know and wants it closed.
Use when: comparison, test, myth. Delivered by: curiosity-gap, question.
Lines: `"[specific known half]. [specific missing half, withheld one beat]."`
  e.g. "Two loaves, same flour. One rose twice as high." / "This screw fixes wobbly chairs. It's not the one you'd buy."
Layers: SL best, OT second (states the topic).
Pairs: concreteness. Clashes: taboo + vague claim (reads as clickbait).
Fails when: the teaser withholds the very thing promised; payoff lands after the viewer left; gap has no specifics.
Ethics: close the gap inside the Reel. A gap never closed is clickbait.
Evidence: RESEARCH (Loewenstein 1994 theory) but headline tests mixed: clickbait headlines did not raise curiosity (CHI 2021, 3 studies). Transfer weak. Flag: weak

### loss-frame · Loss aversion / framing
Does: a cost framed as a loss draws attention.
Use when: workflow upgrade, time or money saver with a real, countable loss. Delivered by: stakes, warning.
Lines: `"[thing you do now] is costing you [unit] every [period]."`
  e.g. (made-up) "Buying lunch every workday costs about $2,000 a year." / "Skipping warm-ups is costing you range every session."
Layers: SL best, OT second (the number).
Pairs: effort-min ("6 hours lost, 10 minutes to fix"), concreteness. Clashes: "you must" commands (reactance).
Fails when: loss is vague; viewer can't verify; reads as fear-selling.
Ethics: loss true and sized honestly. No invented deadlines, no "everyone is already doing this".
Evidence: RESEARCH: framing replicated (31/36 labs, d≈0.31); prospect theory replicated in 19 countries. Loss-aversion size contested; never say "losses hurt 2x".

### negativity · Negativity bias
Does: bad news, mistakes and harms pull attention more than neutral or good ones.
Use when: "mistakes", "stop doing", warnings with a fix. Delivered by: warning.
Lines: `"The [mistake / habit] that's quietly ruining your [thing]."`
  e.g. "The way you're cutting onions is making you cry more." / "This common shelf bracket fails under books."
Layers: SL best, FI second (visible problem).
Pairs: practical utility (the fix). Clashes: humor + threat (tone mismatch); sad or hopeless tone.
Fails when: tone is sad (sadness lowered sharing); negativity outruns the payoff; the brand loses warmth.
Ethics: the harm must exist. No fake alarms.
Evidence: RESEARCH (review, general bias). On platforms mixed: anger/anxiety raised NYT sharing, sadness lowered it (Berger & Milkman 2012, text); emotional contagion study found no negativity edge.

### social-proof · Social proof
Does: seeing that people like you do something makes it feel safe and worth a look.
Use when: answering a question that genuinely recurs. Delivered by: question, number.
Lines: `"[real count] of you asked me about [thing]."` · `"[verifiable number] of [people like you] [do thing]."`
  e.g. (made-up) "Four people asked me this week how I keep basil alive." / "Most gyms skip this machine. Here's why it matters."
Layers: OT best (numbers read fast; show the message screenshot), SL second.
Pairs: concreteness. Clashes: tiny account claiming big numbers.
Fails when: number invented or unverifiable; proof comes from a group the viewer doesn't identify with.
Ethics: no fabricated counts, fake testimonials, or "everyone's switching" without data.
Evidence: RESEARCH (Salganik 2006 music market, 14,341 people; hotel-towel norms 2008), not video. Closer reference group works better.

### self-reference · Identity and self-reference
Does: naming the viewer's exact situation captures the right viewer's attention.
Use when: any idea; strongest as a filter for niche teaching. Delivered by: callout.
Lines: `"If you [specific situation] and [specific problem], this is for you."` then one concrete promise.
  e.g. "If you cook for one and hate leftovers, this is for you." / "Training at home with one kettlebell? Do this."
Layers: SL best, OT second (as a label).
Pairs: concreteness, face-gaze. Clashes: contrarian aimed at the same group.
Fails when: too broad (nobody feels named); too narrow; the label feels like an insult.
Ethics: deliver for the person you named. No flattery bait.
Evidence: RESEARCH (lab attention: own-name capture is partial, 20–65% by person; self-disclosure is rewarding). Hook form INFERENCE.

### status · Status and aspiration
Does: a visible step up in quality or taste pulls viewers who want it.
Use when: look-expensive-for-less, quality upgrades. Delivered by: result-first, proof-first.
Lines: `"How [higher tier] get [visible quality] without [expensive thing]."`
  e.g. "How restaurants get crispy skin without a fryer." / "The cheap trick that makes a rental kitchen look custom."
Layers: FI best (the better look must be visible), SL second.
Pairs: anchoring, awe. Clashes: authority claims a small account can't back.
Fails when: reads as a flex; envy instead of curiosity; the better look isn't in frame 1.
Ethics: no implied income or follower claims; never imply the viewer is low status.
Evidence: RESEARCH, single paper (costly signaling, 2007, 4 experiments); hook use INFERENCE. Flag: weak

### prediction-error · Prediction error / violated expectation
Does: a true, checkable break of what the viewer expects triggers orienting and interest.
Use when: myth-bust, counterintuitive result. Delivered by: contrarian, bold-claim.
Lines: `[state the belief the viewer holds] + [break it with something true and checkable].`
  e.g. "Everyone says sear meat to seal in juices. It doesn't." / "I stopped stretching before runs. My knees feel better."
Layers: SL best, FI second (show the contradiction).
Pairs: pratfall (expert admits error), concreteness. Clashes: trick edits that habituate.
Fails when: the viewer never held the belief; surprise without payoff.
Ethics: break beliefs people actually hold. No straw beliefs.
Evidence: RESEARCH (surprise/orienting in radio and ads; experts persuade more when expressing uncertainty, 3 experiments). Surprise was a smaller sharing predictor than awe or utility (Berger & Milkman 2012, text).

### selective-exposure · Selective exposure (not dissonance)
Does: people lean toward information that confirms what they already suspect, unless the challenge serves a goal they hold.
Use when: confirming a hunch, or challenging only in service of the viewer's goal. Delivered by: callout, contrarian.
Lines: `"You already suspect [belief]. Here's the proof."` · `"You want [goal]; here's what blocks it."`
  e.g. "You already feel your bread is too dense. Here's why." / "You want stronger legs, so here's the habit working against you."
Layers: SL best, OT second.
Pairs: concreteness. Clashes: challenges aimed at identity instead of goal.
Fails when: the challenge targets who they are, not what they want.
Ethics: confirming a false belief to get a view is manipulation.
Evidence: RESEARCH: selective-exposure meta-analysis d=0.36, reverses when the uncongenial info serves a current goal. Dissonance free-choice effect did NOT replicate (39 labs); don't try to induce dissonance.

### novelty · Novelty and salience
Does: something the feed hasn't shown today, or a sudden visual onset, pulls the eye.
Use when: new tool, odd object, unusual shot. Delivered by: interrupt, visual-prop.
Lines: `[first frame shows something new] + [line names it].`
  e.g. "This peeler came out last month. Watch it do a whole squash." / Frame 1 = raw vs finished side by side: "Same wall. One afternoon apart."
Layers: FI best, OT second.
Pairs: concreteness, face-gaze. Clashes: same visual trick every Reel (habituation).
Fails when: novelty irrelevant to the viewer's goal; "new" isn't new.
Ethics: "brand new" must be true and dated.
Evidence: RESEARCH (orienting to onsets and voice changes, radio); eye-tracking of video (one video). Reels transfer INFERENCE.

### concreteness · Specificity and concreteness
Does: exact object, exact number, exact time make a line believable and easy to picture.
Use when: nearly every idea. The default upgrade for any weak hook. Delivered by: number, result-first.
Lines: `[exact tool] + [exact object] + [exact number or time].`
  e.g. (made-up) "A 45-minute ironing pile, done in 12 with one steamer." / "CapCut, one template, 30 seconds: that's my whole caption setup."
Layers: OT best (numbers, names), SL second.
Pairs: anchoring, self-reference. Clashes: none; fails only when false.
Fails when: the number isn't true or can't be shown; jargon names the tool but not the benefit.
Ethics: invented specifics are a lie in better clothes. Only numbers the creator measured.
Evidence: RESEARCH (concrete language raised satisfaction and purchases, >1,000 service interactions, Packard & Berger 2021); fluency research.

### authority · Authority and credibility cues
Does: shown competence earns belief; claimed titles don't, on a small account.
Use when: test, review, how-to with a visible result. Delivered by: proof-first.
Lines: `"I [did specific thing, on screen]. Here's exactly how."`
  e.g. (made-up) "I tested 6 bike locks on the same chain. One held." / "This is the 10th fence I've hung. Here's the trick."
Layers: FI best (proof shown), OT second.
Pairs: concreteness, proof-first. Clashes: effort-min ("anyone can" lowers expertise); humor (lowers credibility).
Fails when: credentials borrowed or vague; result not shown.
Ethics: no fake credentials or results; disclose AI use where the viewer would care.
Evidence: RESEARCH, single line of work (expertise × expressed certainty); humor lowered credibility in an ad meta-analysis. Flag: weak

### gift · Reciprocity / gift framing
Does: a usable thing given inside the Reel reads as value.
Use when: template, checklist, recipe card, setting. Delivered by: value-promise, visual-prop.
Lines: `"Free: [exact usable thing]. It's on screen."`
  e.g. "Here's my exact grocery list. Pause and screenshot it." / "The cut list for this shelf, on screen now."
Layers: OT best (the gift visible), FI second.
Pairs: concreteness. Clashes: scarcity ("free" and "limited" contradict).
Fails when: gift is vague or gated behind a comment keyword.
Ethics: "Comment WORD and I'll send it" is the example Instagram named as non-recommendable. Show the gift in the video.
Evidence: INFERENCE (no primary study read; nearest: concrete language approximates a gift). Flag: weak

### pratfall · Pratfall / vulnerability
Does: a small slip after shown competence raises liking; without competence it lowers it.
Use when: lesson-from-mistake. Delivered by: confession.
Lines: `[competence signal] + "…after I [specific small mistake]."`
  e.g. "I can tile a bathroom in a day. My first one took three weeks." / "My first loaf could have broken a window. Here's the fix."
Layers: SL best, FI second (face reaction).
Pairs: prediction-error, result-first. Clashes: unknown account with no competence shown.
Fails when: no competence shown first; blunder is severe or about trust.
Ethics: no staged failures or fake backstories.
Evidence: RESEARCH, old and moderated (Aronson 1966, male students; effect depends on prior competence and severity). Flag: weak

### humor · Humor / incongruity
Does: an absurd comparison or deadpan mismatch earns attention and liking.
Use when: review, comparison, relatable struggle. Delivered by: pov-setup, bold-claim.
Lines: `[absurd comparison or deadpan mismatch in line one] + [the lesson].`
  e.g. "My smart oven has one job and it still burns the edges." / "Me, spending 4 hours building a shelf to save 4 minutes a day."
Layers: SL best (timing), FI second (visual gag).
Pairs: face-gaze, prosody. Clashes: outrage, threat, authority.
Fails when: the setup takes over ~3 s; teaching credibility matters more than reach; in-joke excludes.
Ethics: low risk; don't mock a named private person.
Evidence: RESEARCH (ad meta-analysis, 369 correlations: more attention and liking, less source credibility). Not Reels.

### awe · Awe
Does: a visible jump in scale ("one step, whole thing") stops the viewer.
Use when: demo, before/after, transformation. Delivered by: result-first, proof-first.
Lines: `[show the scale jump] + "here's how."`
  e.g. "One sheet pan. Dinner for eight." / "This 3-second shot used to take a crew."
Layers: FI best (scale visible), SL second.
Pairs: practical-utility (both top sharing predictors). Clashes: claims larger than the shown result.
Fails when: the output looks fake or ordinary.
Ethics: no faked demos; a best-of-many shown as typical must be stated.
Evidence: RESEARCH, correlational + small lab (awe raised NYT sharing odds, text only, 2008; small awe experiments). Flag: weak

### moral-outrage · Outrage / moral emotion
Does: a practice that violates fairness drives sharing, mostly within a camp.
Use when: price gap, gatekeeping. Weak for a warm teaching brand. Delivered by: contrarian, warning.
Lines: `[name an unfair practice, never a person].`
  e.g. "Shops charge $80 for what a $3 washer fixes." / "Gyms sell this as a course. It's one free cue."
Layers: SL best, OT second.
Pairs: concreteness (facts). Clashes: humor; small account carrying a righteous tone.
Fails when: audience isn't already angry about it; the account is too small to carry it.
Ethics: attack practices, not people; never a private individual; no misleading facts. Engagement from outrage is not value.
Evidence: RESEARCH (platform data: moral-emotional words ~+20% retweets per word, Brady 2017; feedback reinforces outrage). Text platforms; Instagram video transfer weak.

### nostalgia · Nostalgia
Does: recalling an old shared pain creates warmth and connection.
Use when: "how far it's come" ideas. Delivered by: question, before-after.
Lines: `"Remember when [old pain]? That's gone."`
  e.g. "Remember when ironing a shirt took ten minutes?" / "Remember paper maps on road trips?"
Layers: FI best (old visual cue), SL second.
Pairs: before-after arc. Clashes: audience too young for the reference.
Fails when: nostalgia is unrelated to the lesson.
Ethics: nostalgia lowers price sensitivity; never use it to sell.
Evidence: SINGLE paper (6 small experiments, 2014). Flag: weak

### us-vs-them · Tribalism / us vs them
Does: naming an out-group raises sharing; keep the "enemy" a habit, not people.
Use when: workflow debates. Delivered by: two-sides, contrarian.
Lines: `"[practice A] people vs [practice B] people."`
  e.g. "Meal-preppers vs cook-every-night people: one of you wastes less." (only with data) / "Hand-sanders will hate this."
Layers: SL best, OT second.
Pairs: proof-first (a verdict). Clashes: beginners as the target (excludes them).
Fails when: it excludes the target viewer; claims lack proof.
Ethics: no punching at people, identity groups, or competitors by name.
Evidence: RESEARCH (out-group words raised shares 35–57% for news accounts on Facebook/Twitter; Brewer review). Text platforms only.

### scarcity · Scarcity (real limits only)
Does: a real time or access limit raises perceived value.
Use when: news or launch with a real expiry. Delivered by: stakes.
Lines: `"[real limit]: this works until [date / version / end of offer]."`
  e.g. "Strawberries are cheapest for two more weeks. Freeze them now." / "This trick only works on the new model, not the old one."
Layers: OT best (date on screen), SL second.
Pairs: concreteness. Clashes: gift ("free forever" vs "limited").
Fails when: the limit is invented; evergreen content ages out.
Ethics: false scarcity or countdowns are deception.
Evidence: RESEARCH (product-scarcity meta-analysis, 131 studies), not video. Scarcity MINDSET is a different, failed claim (see do-not-use).

### anchoring · Anchoring (relevant anchors)
Does: a real reference price or time set first makes your number land.
Use when: comparison, cost or time saving. Delivered by: number, stakes.
Lines: `[relevant reference price or time] + [your number].`
  e.g. (made-up) "A plumber charges $150 for this. The part is $6." / "Takeout: 40 minutes. This: 12."
Layers: OT best (two numbers side by side), SL second.
Pairs: concreteness, status. Clashes: irrelevant or exaggerated anchors.
Fails when: the anchor is irrelevant or visibly inflated.
Ethics: the anchor must be a real, checkable price or time.
Evidence: RESEARCH: estimation anchoring replicated (Many Labs, ~5,000); irrelevant anchors often fail. Read via one secondary summary.

### direct-address · "You" address and reactance
Does: "you" plus a situation invites; "you must / stop" commands provoke pushback.
Use when: teaching tone; swap to "we/most of us" for correction. Delivered by: callout, warning.
Lines: `"You've probably tried [x] and it didn't work. Here's why."` · `"Most of us [habit], and it's quietly costing [y]."`
  e.g. "You've probably tried oiling the hinge. Here's why it still squeaks." / "Most of us salt pasta water too late."
Layers: SL best (tone), OT second.
Pairs: self-reference. Clashes: loss-frame + imperative.
Fails when: commanding, blaming, adversarial.
Ethics: no shaming the viewer.
Evidence: RESEARCH, one 2024 study ("you" reads as aggressive in disagreement) + reactance meta-analysis (20 studies). Flag: weak

### face-gaze · Faces, eye contact, gaze
Does: a large, expressive face looking into the lens draws the eye; text near the eyes gets read.
Use when: every talking-head idea. Delivered by: any shape on camera.
Lines: `[frame 1 = face large, eyes at lens, already mid-expression] + [text placed near the eyes].`
  e.g. Open mid-grin: "Wait, this actually worked." / Face left, key phrase beside the eyes, then a gesture toward the work.
Layers: FI only.
Pairs: prosody, contagion, concreteness (three layers on one idea). Clashes: dead stare, reading off screen.
Fails when: face static all video; eyes off-lens.
Ethics: disclose AI faces or avatars where viewers assume a real person.
Evidence: RESEARCH (infants prefer direct gaze) + MANY (eye-tracking; lecture video: face alternating with slides held attention, correlational, 6.9M sessions).

### prosody · Voice prosody and sound
Does: pitch movement and a pause before the payoff word keep the ear oriented.
Use when: every spoken hook. Delivered by: any shape.
Lines: `[start mid-thought] + [vary pitch in sentence one] + [drop pitch and slow on the payoff word].`
  e.g. Line one with real surprise, then slow on the number. / Half-second pause before the promise.
Layers: SL (voice) only.
Pairs: face-gaze, contagion. Clashes: music bed over the first words.
Fails when: monotone; forced energy; background audio covers the first words.
Ethics: never clone or fake another person's voice.
Evidence: RESEARCH, thin (voice changes re-trigger orienting in radio; preliminary pitch-range work; faster lecturers more engaging, correlational). Flag: weak

### contagion · Emotional contagion
Does: the emotion shown in face and voice in second one sets the viewer's.
Use when: real reactions to a result. Delivered by: result-first, mid-action.
Lines: `[show the emotion you want felt in the first second] + [the cause].`
  e.g. Genuine laugh at the result, then "watch this." / Real relief: "Finally fixed it."
Layers: FI (face) best, SL second.
Pairs: face-gaze, prosody. Clashes: faked enthusiasm.
Fails when: faked; tiny effect can't carry a weak idea.
Ethics: never sell a faked reaction as real.
Evidence: RESEARCH, text only, tiny effects (Facebook 2014, d 0.001–0.02, expression of concern); video contagion INFERENCE. Flag: weak

### threat · Threat / real risk
Does: a real, specific risk plus its fix commands attention.
Use when: safety, rules, damage. Delivered by: warning, stakes.
Lines: `[name a real, specific risk] + [the fix].`
  e.g. "That extension cord under the rug is a fire risk. Here's the fix." / "Reposting someone's clip unchanged can stop your account being recommended." (cite the rule)
Layers: SL best, OT second.
Pairs: practical-utility. Clashes: humor.
Fails when: risk exaggerated; fix missing.
Ethics: no fear-mongering with unsourced threats.
Evidence: INFERENCE (snake/spider attention studies not read; informational risk is a different stimulus). Platform-rule facts are OFFICIAL. Flag: weak

### taboo · Taboo / forbidden knowledge
Does: "they won't tell you" implies hidden value.
Use when: rarely; only with a true, specific omission. Delivered by: secret.
Lines: `"Most [courses / shops] won't say this: [true specific]."`
  e.g. "What paint shops don't mention: one coat of primer saves two coats of colour." (only if true) / "Trainers rarely say this: rest days build the muscle."
Layers: SL best, OT second.
Pairs: concreteness, proof-first. Clashes: info-gap + vague claim (clickbait).
Fails when: sounds like hype; overlaps the secret shape with nothing behind it.
Ethics: false "they don't want you to know" claims are manipulation.
Evidence: INFERENCE (forbidden-fruit study seen as a title only; reactance suggests restrictions raise desire and anger). Flag: weak

### effort-min · Effort minimization
Does: people choose the less demanding option; "less work" pulls.
Use when: automation, time savers. Delivered by: value-promise, number.
Lines: `"[result] in [short time] with [no skill / cost]."`
  e.g. "No knife skills, 5 minutes, a full salad." / "No drill. Two hooks. Shelf up."
Layers: OT best ("5 minutes" on screen), SL second.
Pairs: loss-frame. Clashes: authority ("anyone can" lowers perceived expertise).
Fails when: feels too easy without proof; the audience values craft.
Ethics: no hidden steps or omitted setup time.
Evidence: RESEARCH (effort avoidance, 6 lab experiments, 61–73% chose the easier task); effort-heuristic replication mixed.

### controversy · Controversy (defensible disagreement)
Does: a specific, defensible disagreement with common practice sparks attention.
Use when: opinion, framework. Delivered by: contrarian.
Lines: `[a defensible, specific disagreement with a common practice].`
  e.g. "Hot take: you don't need a stand mixer for bread." / "Unpopular: cardio before weights is fine for most people."
Layers: SL best, OT second.
Pairs: proof-first. Clashes: unsupported opinion on a teaching account.
Fails when: opinion unsupported; costs trust.
Ethics: no manufactured controversy for reach.
Evidence: INFERENCE (measurable parts live in moral-outrage and us-vs-them). Flag: weak

### Also well supported (not hooks on their own)
- **practical-utility**: usefulness predicted NYT sharing as strongly as awe (Berger & Milkman 2012, text). Teaching Reels already sit on it: state the usable outcome and who it helps. Evidence: RESEARCH, one archival study, text.
- **fluency**: easy-to-read, easy-to-say wording is judged truer and more likeable. Short common words, pronounceable names, short screen lines. Evidence: RESEARCH (review).
- **resume urge**: the urge to finish an interrupted task is real; the memory advantage is not. Promise a numbered payoff ("3 steps, step 2 is the trick"). Evidence: RESEARCH (meta-analysis, 59 studies). Flag: weak for the hook use.

## Combos

Compound:
- concreteness + anchoring: exact number against a real reference ("$150 plumber vs $6 part"). Both RESEARCH.
- self-reference + concreteness: "If you cook for one…" then the exact outcome. Combo INFERENCE.
- face-gaze + prosody + one on-screen number: three layers on one idea.
- awe + practical-utility: impressive and usable (both top sharing predictors, text study).
- loss-frame + effort-min: named cost, quick fix.
- prediction-error + pratfall: "I got it wrong" breaks the expert-is-right expectation; competence shown first.

Clash:
- moral-outrage + humor: humor lowers credibility; outrage needs it.
- authority claims + small account: unprovable authority breeds distrust; show proof.
- scarcity + gift: "free forever" vs "limited time".
- effort-min + authority: "anyone can" lowers perceived expertise.
- loss-frame + "you must": reactance.
- taboo + info-gap + vague claim: reads as clickbait; promise not kept.
- humor + threat: tone mismatch.

## When the idea has no obvious angle

1. Don't force outrage, scarcity or taboo. They fail first and carry the ethics risk.
2. Run the safest four, in order: (a) Who exactly is this for? (self-reference) (b) What exact number, tool or time? (concreteness) (c) What is the one true surprising fact? (prediction-error) (d) Face and voice in frame 1 (face-gaze, prosody).
3. How-to idea: lead with the finished result (`hooks.md` result-first); add anchoring if a real price or time comparison exists.
4. Still flat: shorten, or say it plainly: "Small but useful: 20 seconds to [specific gain]." An honest low-key hook beats a fake dramatic one.
5. No honest angle: tell the creator. Never invent one.

## Do not use (dead, failed or misquoted)

Never build a hook on these or state them as fact:
- **Priming words / priming hooks**: behavioral priming failed preregistered replication (d≈.02).
- **Scarcity mindset** ("poverty costs 13–14 IQ points"): 4 of 20 effects replicated.
- **Zeigarnik memory** ("open loops are remembered"): no recall advantage in a 59-study meta-analysis. Only the resume urge holds.
- **Dissonance free-choice effect**: did not replicate (39 labs, ~4,900 people).
- **"Losses hurt twice as much"**: magnitude contested. Say "losses often weigh more", no number.
- **Ego depletion, effort heuristic as stated**: replications mixed or failed.
- **"Own name always grabs attention"**: 20–65% by person; a Reel doesn't know the viewer's name anyway.
- **"Viewers decide in 1.7 s"**: Facebook 2016 average time on a feed item, not a decision time.
- **"60%+ 3-second hold needed for distribution"**: vendor, no method, internally inconsistent.
- **"Instagram confirmed watch time / likes per reach / sends per reach as top signals, sends = 3–5x likes"**: vendor repetition; the weighting is unverified.
- **"Reels max 90 s"**: outdated (3 min).
