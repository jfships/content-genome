# Endings and asks

How a 20–60 s Reel ends, and which ask (if any) it carries. Load when writing the last beat (job A) or checking it (job B). Ask selection is stage-aware (§4): read the brand's strategy block before proposing any ask.

Read first:
- Instagram has said nothing found about loops or rewatches as a ranking signal. Its Views metric counts replays (secondary reports). "Loops feed watch time" is an inference, not a documented fact.
- Mosseri's reported top signals: watch time, likes, sends. Sends weigh more with non-followers, likes with followers. Saves and comments are not in that top three. He said not to force it.
- No controlled test of "ask vs no ask" on Reels exists. All ask advice is craft or vendor.
- Fixed design decision, not an option: the ending is never an outro, a "thanks for watching", a subscribe/follow montage or an end card. The last spoken line carries value or the one ask.
- Slugs reuse video-genome CTA slugs (`plan/story-libraries.md` §9, if video-genome is installed; otherwise the map in §5 is all you need) where they match; map in §5.

## 1. Ending cards

### payoff-stop · Payoff, then stop
Does: the last thing said is the answer, sharpest version, then cut. No sign-off, no trailing pause, no fade. A sign-off gives people a place to leave.
Use when: how-to, rule, myth-bust; any idea with a clean answer. Default ending.
Lines: `[Hook question/claim] … [steps] … [the answer in one sentence]. (cut)`
  e.g. "So the pan was never too small. You were filling it." / "That's the whole trick: let the wall dry overnight."
Edit: cut on the last stressed word, inside its final consonant; no L-hold on the face; music ends with the cut (DROP) or before.
Pairs: payoff-ladder, info-density. Clashes: tease-next (reopens what just closed).
Fails when: the payoff is weak or already said earlier (feels like running out of road); the viewer needs a next step to use it.
Evidence: MANY [8] (Hoyos, vendors) + RESEARCH (peak-end, lab film clips; other medium).

### deadpan-hold · Deadpan line, abrupt cut
Does: ends on a flat line or reaction and cuts at once; lingering costs replays and gives time to leave.
Use when: product fails, comparisons, "I tried X", dry humor.
Lines: `[Payoff line, delivered flat]. (hard cut, no beat)`
  e.g. "Anyway. It still burned." / "Total cost: one coffee." (cut)
Edit: hard cut 0–3 frames after the last word; no hold on face (a held face reads as hesitation).
Pairs: button, but-therefore. Clashes: send-line (tone break after a deadpan).
Fails when: the line needs setup the viewer doesn't have; the creator holds a beat and it reads as hesitation.
Evidence: SINGLE [3] (one Shorts creator, relayed twice). Flag: weak.

### button · Button line
Does: a short sharp last line that lands the idea and ends the scene; the cut right behind it makes it hit.
Use when: rules, definitions, opinions; any idea that compresses to a line.
Lines: `[Three-to-eight-word rule / reversal of the opening claim].`
  e.g. "Same pan. Half the food. That's it." / "If it needs two coats, it needed primer."
Edit: optional POP of 1–2 words from the line (never the whole line); cut on the last word.
Pairs: payoff-stop, callback, follow-reason (series rules). Clashes: a button that repeats the previous sentence.
Fails when: clever but adds no new meaning; duplicates the last spoken sentence.
Evidence: SINGLE [4] for Reels; long comedy convention (one comedian's glossary). Flag: weak.

### callback · Callback / echo
Does: the end returns to the opening word, object or image with one thing changed, so the idea feels closed; a visible change rewards a rewatch.
Use when: before/after, fixes, mistake-then-repair.
Lines: `Open: [claim/image A]. … End: [A again, now with new meaning].`
  e.g. Open: "This sauce is broken." End: "Same sauce. One spoon of water." / Open on a cluttered shelf, end on the same shelf, sorted.
Edit: MATCH or same framing as frame 1; WIPE if the change is visual; repeat the opening overlay word.
Pairs: full-circle operator, visual-loop, button. Clashes: callbacks to older videos (new viewers lack the setup).
Fails when: the opener was a throwaway line that can't carry it; the callback points outside this video.
Evidence: VENDOR for a Reels effect; comedy craft supports the idea only. Flag: weak.

### verbal-loop · Split-sentence loop
Aliases: loop
Does: one sentence is split; its second half opens the video, its first half closes it, so the replay reads as one thought.
Use when: short (under ~15–25 s), dense, one-idea teaching Reels.
Lines: `Close: "[first half of sentence]" → Open: "[second half]."`
  e.g. Close: "The reason your bread is flat is" → Open: "you skipped the second rise." / Close: "And that's why you never paint over" → Open: "glossy trim without sanding."
Edit: no breath, pause or room-tone gap at head or tail; audio runs through the join; captions must not restart mid-line at the join.
Pairs: visual-loop (both = most fragile, most seamless). Clashes: any ask after the last line; long or emotional Reels.
Fails when: the opening was bent into a weak hook to make the join work; dead air at either end; viewers feel tricked.
Evidence: VENDOR [6]; case-study numbers have no method. Flag: weak.

### visual-loop · Visual match loop
Aliases: loop
Does: last frame matches first frame (same framing, posture, prop), sound continuous across the join.
Use when: talking head with steady framing and simple inserts; cheap when framing doesn't change.
Lines: `End frame = start frame; no fade; no end beat.`
  e.g. End reaching for the pan, start with the hand already on it. / End holding the tile up, start with it up.
Edit: MATCH at the join; same layout and overlay position at both ends; no text on the last frame that isn't on the first.
Pairs: verbal-loop, callback. Clashes: an on-screen ask in the last seconds (breaks the match).
Fails when: captions or a different shot sit on frame 1; lip position or breath differ visibly.
Evidence: VENDOR [5]. Flag: weak.

### open-question-loop · Last line asks, first line answers
Aliases: loop
Does: the closing question is answered by the opening line on replay.
Use when: concept explainers.
Lines: `Close: "[Question]?" → Open: "[Answer-as-hook]."`
  e.g. Close: "So what do you call a knife that never gets sharpened?" → Open: "A very expensive butter spreader."
Edit: as verbal-loop; the question gets no music swell (it's a join, not an ask).
Pairs: visual-loop. Clashes: comment (if viewers answer in comments, it's a comment prompt, not a loop).
Fails when: the question reads as a real viewer question; the opening answer is weak alone.
Evidence: VENDOR [2]. Flag: weak.

### tease-next · Next-step tease
Aliases: part-two
Does: resolves one step and shows the next is needed; the viewer looks for the next video.
Use when: multi-step workflows told as a series that really exists or will exist.
Lines: `[Finish step 1.] [Name the one thing step 2 fixes] (no date promised).`
  e.g. "That gets you a sharp knife. Keeping it sharp is a different habit." / "Works now. Next problem: it leaks again in winter."
Edit: shown, not said, where possible: a glimpse of the next problem as a CUTAWAY; no "part 2" end card.
Pairs: progress (series count). Clashes: payoff-stop (reopens the close); a Reel meant to stand alone for strangers.
Fails when: part 2 never gets made; non-followers will never see part 2; framed as "follow to find out" (bait).
Evidence: VENDOR [4]; Zeigarnik basis is the memory claim that fails (see retention §5). Flag: weak.

### send-line · Situational send line
Aliases: save-share (send half)
Does: names a specific person or situation who needs this, so the viewer thinks of someone to forward it to. Feeds sends per reach.
Use when: tips with a clear "who needs this". Optional, only when one fits naturally (rules in §4.3).
Lines: `[Specific person with specific problem] needs this.` / `For whoever [specific situation].`
  e.g. "Your friend who crowds the pan every time needs this." / "For whoever just painted over wet filler."
Edit: spoken or as a one-line CARD-L; never with arrows to the share button; no SFX.
Pairs: payoff-stop, button (send line after the button, ≤1.5 s). Clashes: deadpan-hold, keyword-comment, any second ask.
Fails when: not something people would privately forward; generic ("share this"); a quota ("send to 5 friends"); a gate ("share to find out").
Evidence: OFFICIAL (reported) that sends are a top signal, more for non-followers. VENDOR that a spoken line raises sends; no test. Instagram reportedly said explicit share asks can limit recommendation, then clarified it targets hacks, not sincere engagement: the situational form is the low-risk form.

### save-prompt · Save prompt
Aliases: save-share (save half)
Does: asks the viewer to keep the Reel for a specific later moment.
Use when: checklists, templates, reference lists the viewer will come back to.
Lines: `Save this for the next time you [specific moment].`
  e.g. "Save this for the next time your rice goes mushy." / "Keep this one for your next paint job."
Edit: one STEP-style label at most; never during the payoff.
Pairs: progress (list Reels). Clashes: one-line opinions (nothing to come back to); send-line in the same Reel.
Fails when: there's nothing to return to; it replaces a weak payoff.
Evidence: VENDOR [3]; one dataset shows Reels save rates are tiny next to carousels. Flag: weak.

### follow-reason · Follow with a reason
Aliases: hard (generic form)
Does: gives a concrete reason to follow: more of this specific thing on a cadence, often tied to a numbered series.
Use when: a recurring series exists and strangers are arriving (stage rule §4).
Lines: `I post one [specific thing] every [cadence]. [That was number N.]`
  e.g. "One knife skill a week. That was number seven." / "Fixing one thing in this house every Friday."
Edit: STEP/series label ("#7") can carry it on screen; one line, after the payoff or at peak (§4.4).
Pairs: button, progress. Clashes: stacked asks (follow + comment + share); generic "follow for more".
Fails when: generic; stacked with other asks; placed after the payoff when most viewers have left; no series exists.
Evidence: VENDOR [5] plus one practitioner on placement; follows from non-followers convert weakly (small sample). Flag: weak.

### comment · Real opinion question
Does: a specific, answerable question that invites comments without a keyword.
Use when: opinion, product-vs-product, workflow choices with real disagreement.
Lines: `[A or B]? I go [A] because [reason].`
  e.g. "Cast iron or nonstick for eggs? I go cast iron, preheated." / "Roller or brush on trim? I brush."
Edit: no overlay of the full question; optional POP "A or B" words.
Pairs: viewer-job (retention). Clashes: open-question-loop; settled how-tos (nothing to disagree about).
Fails when: no real disagreement; vague ("thoughts?").
Evidence: OFFICIAL (reported) that open-ended questions are acceptable; effect size unknown; comments not in the top three. Flag: weak.

### keyword-comment · Comment a word to get something
Aliases: lead (link in bio / lead offer: same stage gate)
Does: the viewer comments a trigger word and a tool sends a private message with the resource (Meta limit: one private reply per comment, within 7 days).
Use when: only when a real offer/asset exists and the brand strategy allows lead asks (§4). Never by default.
Lines: `Comment [WORD] and I'll send you [specific thing].`
  e.g. (shape only; no example by design)
Edit: one CARD-L at most; never in the first 3 s.
Pairs: none. Clashes: send-line, follow-reason, any loop.
Fails when: the asset is generic; no audience exists to convert; a viral post fires bulk identical replies (reported DM blocks, anecdotal).
Evidence: very common (vendor sample, no method). Policy mixed: Instagram (reported, 2024) said explicit single-word comment prompts can lose recommendation, then softened to "intent". No test either way. Flag: weak.

### slogan · Signature close
Does: unproven. Hypothesis: a repeated closing phrase builds recognition.
Use when: only if the brand folder defines one and the creator wants it; never proposed by default.
Lines: `[Brand's own phrase, verbatim from the brand folder].`
Edit: same position and style every time if used.
Pairs: follow-reason (series). Clashes: verbal-loop, visual-loop (breaks the join).
Fails when: it becomes a sign-off that signals "leave now".
Evidence: none retrieved. Flag: weak.

## 2. Loop construction guide

A seamless loop: the last moment flows into the first so there is no felt restart. Documented: Instagram Views and YouTube Shorts views count replays. Not documented: that either platform ranks loops higher. Vendors themselves limit loops to short, dense Reels (under ~15–25 s) and say they work poorly for long or emotional ones. Instagram counting total watched seconds makes chasing 100%+ retention less necessary.

Ways to build one:
1. **Split sentence** (verbal-loop). Write the full sentence first, cut it in two. The opening half must still work as a hook alone (hook gate applies to it).
2. **Echo** (callback). Last line repeats a keyword or image from line one; line one re-reads with new meaning.
3. **Question / answer** (open-question-loop). Last line asks; first line answers.
4. **Visual match** (visual-loop). Same frame, position, prop at both ends; motion continues through the join.
5. **Sound match.** Continuous room tone or music across the join; no tail silence, no music ending on the last frame.
6. **Verbal + visual together.** Most seamless, most fragile to edit.

Card notation: last beat `ending: verbal-loop`, `edit: MATCH @join`, `sound: continuous @join`; first beat marks the matching half.

Loop failure modes:
- Dead air or a breath at head or tail breaks the join.
- A wave, "thanks", sign-off or slogan at the end guarantees an exit and kills the join.
- The opening line went weak because it was bent to fit the loop.
- Obvious "watch again" prompts read as manipulation.
- Long Reels: the loop is invisible and adds nothing.
- Captions restart mid-line at the join (visual tell; inference).
- An on-screen ask in the last seconds sits across the join and breaks the visual match (inference).

## 3. Ask → signal → fit

| Ask | Signal it feeds | Official support | Fit when the audience is tiny |
|---|---|---|---|
| none (payoff, button, loop) | watch time, replays | watch time is a top signal (reported) | best default |
| send-line | sends per reach | top-3 signal, more for non-followers (reported) | best ask: no audience needed; the viewer is the distribution |
| save-prompt | saves | listed as an engagement action | only for reference-style Reels |
| follow-reason | follows | none specific | weak until strangers arrive and a series exists |
| comment | comments | open questions OK (reported) | needs real disagreement |
| keyword-comment | comments, DMs | mixed | no audience to convert; spam-filter risk if viral |
| lead (link/bio/offer) | clicks | none | nobody to catch until traffic exists |

Caption and pinned comment can carry an ask instead of the video. No head-to-head test exists; vendors favor the caption for the specific next step.

## 4. Stage-aware asks

The skill never decides the business stage. It reads it.

### 4.1 Read the strategy block
At run time, read the brand folder's strategy block (path from the brand folder's index; video-genome/brand-genome name it if installed; otherwise look for a `## Strategy` / `content-strategy` file in `00 Decisions/`, else use the default below). Fields used:

| Field | Used for |
|---|---|
| stage | which asks are open (table 4.2) |
| follower count, and whether followers are mostly people the creator knows | stage check when `stage` is missing |
| goal per Reel (reach / trust / follow / lead) | which single ask fits this Reel |
| offers (real, live, with a link or DM flow) | gate for keyword-comment and lead |
| CTA policy (allowed, banned, house rules like "no CTA", "vertical loops") | hard filter; overrides the table |

Write the result into the card header as `stage read: <stage> | asks open: <slugs> | source: strategy block|default`.

### 4.2 Ask → stage table

| Ask | Signal | Starts to make sense when |
|---|---|---|
| payoff-stop, button, callback, loops, deadpan-hold | watch time, replays | always |
| send-line (situational, optional) | sends from non-followers | always, when a real "who needs this" exists |
| save-prompt | saves | reference content at any stage; rarely the best pick |
| comment (real question) | comments | any stage, only with real disagreement; more useful once comments get answered by strangers |
| tease-next / part-two | follows, series views | the series exists (or the next part is already scripted) |
| follow-reason | follows | strangers are arriving (non-follower reach is regular) and a named recurring series exists |
| keyword-comment | comments, DMs, leads | a real offer exists, the strategy allows lead asks, and reach is past "mostly people the creator knows" |
| lead (link/bio/offer) | clicks | as keyword-comment |
| hard ("follow") | follows | rarely; only if the strategy names it |
| slogan | recognition (unproven) | only if the brand defines one |

Stage shorthand:
- **No audience yet / small audience of mostly people the creator knows:** payoff-stop, button, deadpan-hold, callback, verbal-loop, visual-loop + optional situational send-line. Nothing else.
- **Strangers arriving:** add follow-reason (with a series) and tease-next (with a real series); comment questions where opinions split.
- **Offer live and strategy allows:** keyword-comment or lead, one per Reel, never stacked with a send line.

**Default when no strategy block exists:** payoff-stop, button, deadpan-hold, callback, verbal-loop, visual-loop + an optional send-line. No follow asks, no comment-a-word, no lead doors. Say in the card header `source: default (no strategy block)`.

One ask per Reel, or none. A pure-value Reel with no ask is complete.

### 4.3 Send line rules
- Situational: name the person or the moment ("your friend who crowds the pan", "whoever just painted over wet filler").
- Never "share this", "send this", "tag a friend".
- Never quotas ("send to 3 people").
- Never gated ("share to find out", "send it and I'll post part 2").
- Optional: propose it only when a real "who needs this" exists. If none fits, end on the payoff.
- One line, ≤12 words, after the payoff or button; or folded into the payoff line itself.
- The engagement-bait scan may flag it; a relevant situational line is a false positive (human decides; see `check/report.md` "False positives").

### 4.4 Placement options (both valid; label which one the card uses)
- **A. Mid-video at peak** (Hoyos, Shorts): any ask lands right after a payoff in the middle (video-genome `mid-value`), while attention is high; the ending stays a clean payoff. Basis: end-of-video asks hurt replays and many viewers leave after the payoff. SINGLE, YouTube Shorts. Flag: weak.
- **B. One short ask after the payoff:** a single ask, ≤1.5–3 s, right after the final payoff, no stack (vendor guidance: one action). VENDOR. Flag: weak.
Card field: `ask_place: mid-peak | after-payoff | caption | none`. Loops: an after-payoff ask breaks the join, so loops use mid-peak, caption or none.

### 4.5 Never
Outro, "thanks for watching", wave, "that's it for today", subscribe/follow montage, end card, logo sting at the end, a stack of asks (like + comment + follow + share), an ask in the first 3 s.
