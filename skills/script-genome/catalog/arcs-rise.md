# Arcs: rise

Family: the state climbs from low to high; variants differ in what carries the climb (a growing number, a montage of steps, a tactic against a bigger rival, or the creator's own beginning).
Shared skeleton: `[start low, concrete] [step: action → result] [step: action → bigger result] [peak] [the one thing that mattered]`.
Evidence: no arc tested at 30–60 s; rags-riches is rare in book data and shows a small rating gain but no box-office effect in films; a pure rise with no setback reads as a brag and loses the belief swings that suspense needs (economic theory, no viewer data), so add one honest dip or a hard constraint.
Runtimes: rags-riches and transformation-montage fit 20–60 s; underdog 30–60 s; origin-story 45–60 s (at 30 s: spark, struggle, now only).

Card fields as in `arcs-setback.md`. Words per beat ≈ seconds × 2.4. All examples are made up.

### rags-riches · Rags to riches
Type: CURVE
Does: starts at nothing, and each step is a number that grows, ending on the one thing that mattered.
Use when: demo (a growth result), story. 30–60 s. Needs on-screen receipts for "from nothing" claims.
Beats: B1 START LOW 15% (0–4.5 | 0–9) hook · B2 STEP 1 25% (4.5–12 | 9–24) · B3 STEP 2 25% (12–19.5 | 24–39) · B4 PEAK 23% (19.5–26.5 | 39–53) · B5 SO-WHAT 12% (26.5–30 | 53–60). @30: steps 1–2 merge; B5 is the last line of B4 → 3–4 beats.
V: [-1, 0, +1, +2, +2] `▂▄▆██`
Lines: each step a growing number ("day 1: <n>", "week 4: <n>"); joined by `so` or `which got me to`; the counter carries the sequence, never "and then". `[started at <n> with <nothing>] [step 1: <action> → <n>] [step 2: <action> → <n>] [now <n>] [the one thing: <x>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "Day one: I couldn't do a single push-up." / "Week four: twelve in a row. Week eight: thirty."
Edit: CARD-N counter that ticks up each step (the counter is the story) · cuts speed up per step (CUT-M, CUT-F, CUT-F), BEAT if music · no dip; INT only at B4 (HIT + number slam) · B5 CUT-S, CARD-L.
Pairs: hooks number, proof-first, result-first (fine here: the how is the countdown); endings send-line, loop (counter resets), payoff-stop. Clashes: stakes (no downside to stake), mystery-box.
Fails when: no setback anywhere (reads as a brag); numbers can't be checked; no step the viewer can copy.
Evidence: RESEARCH (long text) [2]: ~5% of book arcs; +0.14 rating, no box-office effect in films. Flag: weak

### transformation-montage · Transformation montage
Type: CURVE/ORDER
Does: an ugly start, a fast run of labeled steps, the final state, one line of close. Almost no sentences.
Use when: demo, process; the change can be shown in shots. 20–60 s. Not for talking-head only.
Beats: B1 BEFORE 15% (0–4.5 | 0–9) hook · B2 MONTAGE 65% (4.5–24 | 9–48) 5–6 flashes at 30 s, 9–12 at 60 s, 3–4 s each · B3 FINAL 15% (24–28.5 | 48–57) · B4 CLOSE 5% (28.5–30 | 57–60) one line or on-screen text only.
V: [-1, ramp 0→+2, +2, +1] `▂▄▆██▆`
Lines: step labels, one-line voice-over or none; when teaching, every flash needs a step label or it entertains and teaches nothing. `[<ugly start>] [step 1 label … step N label] [final] [one line]`
  e.g. "Step 1: strip the old paint." / "Step 4: two thin coats, not one thick one."
Edit: CUT-X on BEAT, MATCH cuts between states, STEP label on every flash (key word only), RISE across B2, HIT on B3, CARD-N if measurable; captions emphasis or off (labels carry it).
Pairs: hooks visual-prop, result-first, interrupt, number; endings send-line (steps in the post caption), loop. Clashes: any hook that needs explaining.
Fails when: step labels are unreadable at that speed; the change can't be shown in 1–2 shots; talking head only.
Evidence: VENDOR/INFERENCE [2]; signaling principle (labels help learning, other medium). Flag: weak

### underdog · Underdog (David and Goliath)
Type: CURVE/ARGUMENT · Aliases: david-goliath
Does: names a giant's real advantage and the underdog's real constraint in numbers, then the unconventional tactic and a fair result.
Use when: comparison, demo, story. 30–60 s.
Beats: B1 GIANT 14% (0–4 | 0–8.5) hook · B2 LIMITS 20% (4–10 | 8.5–20.5) · B3 TACTIC 30% (10–19 | 20.5–38.5) · B4 SHOWDOWN 26% (19–27 | 38.5–54) · B5 MEANING 10% (27–30 | 54–60). @30: B5 is the last line of B4.
V: [-1, -1, +1, +2, +1] `▂▂▆█▆`
Lines: both sides in numbers; the tactic is the creator's real, repeatable move; `so` into the tactic. `[<giant> has <advantage>] [I have <constraint>] [so I <tactic>] [result: <comparison>] [so <meaning>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "A restaurant kitchen has a 450-degree oven and six cooks." / "I have a cast-iron pan and a grill setting, so I heat the pan under the grill for twenty minutes."
Edit: B1 big-scale visual, CUT-M · B2 small, handheld · B3 CUT-F, STEP · B4 SPLIT side by side, CARD-N, INT @result · B5 CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks stakes, number, callout; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: warning.
Fails when: the giant is a strawman; the result is unproven or the test is unfair; claims without receipts.
Evidence: INFERENCE; MANY craft [1]. Flag: weak

### origin-story · Origin story
Type: CONTAINER
Does: the creator's ordinary before, the spark, the struggle, the breakthrough, and why that makes the channel exist, tied to the viewer's problem.
Use when: story, brand; best once the audience knows the creator. 45–60 s; at 30 s: spark, struggle, now only.
Beats: B1 ORDINARY 15% (0–4.5 | 0–9) hook · B2 SPARK 15% (4.5–9 | 9–18) · B3 STRUGGLE 35% (9–19.5 | 18–39) · B4 BREAKTHROUGH 20% (19.5–25.5 | 39–51) · B5 NOW 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60).
V: [0, +1, -1, +2, +1] `▄▆▂█▆`
Lines: `until <spark>` · `then <breakthrough>` · B5 ties to the viewer ("that's why I only show <x>"). `[I was <ordinary>] [until <spark>] [I struggled with <x>] [then <breakthrough>] [that's why I <do/teach>]`
  e.g. "I used to plan every trip in a forty-tab spreadsheet." / "That's why everything I post is about one bag and one hour of booking."
Edit: CUTAWAY of real old photos or screenshots, STEP date labels, MUS-LOW, CUT-M · INT at B4 · B5 face to camera, CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks pov-setup, stakes; endings callback, loop; lead and tease-next stage-gated. Clashes: result-first, hard ask.
Fails when: strangers don't know the creator yet (little pull for a new account: INFERENCE); no tie to viewer value.
Evidence: CRAFT [1]; INFERENCE on fit for new accounts. Flag: weak
