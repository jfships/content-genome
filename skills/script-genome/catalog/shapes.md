# Shapes: whole-script order

A shape is the order the viewer gets information in, start to end. An arc (`arcs-*.md`) is how the feeling moves. One skeleton drives the beats of a Reel: pick a shape when the idea is teaching-led (demo, tip, list, news, comparison), an arc when it is story-led or the feeling is the point. Many shapes have a twin arc; when they do, use the shape's word budget and the arc card's valence and edit lines. When a shape and its twin arc differ in order, the shape's order wins for teaching ideas and the arc's order for story ideas; take only the edit lines that fit the order you keep. Operators and line rules (`arcs-operators.md`) stack on either.

Evidence first: no shape has been tested on 20–60 s Instagram Reels. Instagram publishes no script structure. Support is craft tradition, vendor blogs and a few lab principles from other media (fact-first debunking, fewer arguments beat more, order effects). Treat every timing below as a starting default to check against the creator's own retention graph.

## Read before picking

**Budgets.** Planning pace 2.4 words/s (use the creator's measured pace if known): 20 s ≈ 48 words, 30 s ≈ 72, 45 s ≈ 108, 60 s ≈ 144. Words per beat ≈ beat seconds × 2.4, minus silent demo time (cut ~10% when a screen demo or a card needs reading time). Beats ≥ 5 s and ≥ 12 words, except the hook beat (≤ 8 spoken words carry the point in the first ~3 s). Beat counts: 15–30 s → 3–4 · 31–45 s → 4–5 · 46–60 s → 5–6 (7 for lists and ladders; steps and list items count as one block of sub-beats).

**Beat line format.** `B# NAME pct (s@30 | s@60) job`. Where a shape is capped below 60 s, the second figure is `n/a`. Where a beat falls under 5 s at 30 s, the card says what it folds into.

**Where the strongest item goes.** First (as the hook) or last (as the payoff). Never buried in the middle. "Strongest in the center" has no support; order-effect research shows primacy or recency depending on conditions, and comedy craft opens fast and closes on the biggest bit. This replaces any "put the strongest item at the center" advice elsewhere in the package.

**Result first or story first: pick by the idea.**
| Idea | Order | Shapes |
|---|---|---|
| Demo or tool: the viewer wants the output | Result first | result-first-demo, hook-payoff-loop, before-after, i-tested |
| Personal story: the viewer must care about the person first | Story, then proof | abt-story, seven-part-story; arcs in `arcs-setback.md`, `arcs-rise.md` |
| Hybrid (a story with an output) | Promise the result in the hook, hold the proof until late | seven-part-story with a result-teaser hook; mystery-box (`arcs-curiosity.md`) |

**Endings used in cards.** Default set for "no audience yet": `payoff-stop`, `button`, `deadpan-hold`, `callback`, `verbal-loop`, `visual-loop`, plus optional `send-line` (a situational line naming who needs it; never "share this", never quotas). `loop` is an umbrella alias (verbal-loop, visual-loop, open-question-loop); old slug save-share is an alias split into send-line + save-prompt (`endings.md` §5). Stage-gated (only when the brand's strategy block allows; none by default): `comment`, `tease-next` (old slug part-two), `lead`, `hard`. Cards live in `endings.md`; if it names one differently, it wins.

**Hook slugs** (cards in `hooks.md`; they follow video-genome's hook slugs if video-genome is installed; otherwise this list is complete): result-first, proof-first, value-promise, bold-claim, contrarian, question, curiosity-gap, number, callout, warning, stakes, mid-action, secret, pov-setup, visual-prop, interrupt, confession, context-first, habit-break.

## Result-first family

### result-first-demo · Result first, then how
Aliases: hook-proof-process
Does: shows the finished output in the first seconds, then the few steps that made it, then the result again with what changed.
Use when: demo, tip, any idea with a visible or measurable output. Decision: demo/tool → result first.
Lines: `[result on screen + one sentence] [you need <x>; step 1 / step 2 / step 3] [same result, what changed]`
  e.g. (made-up) "This shelf went up in eight minutes." / "Step two is where most people strip the screw."
Beats: B1 RESULT 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 STEPS 70% (3–24 | 6–48) one need-line, then 3 steps ≈ 6.5 s | 13 s each · B3 RESULT AGAIN 20% (24–30 | 48–60) payoff + close. Words ~72 | ~144 (hook ≤8; steps ~15 | ~30 each). Re-hook: before step 2 or 3 ("step three is where it breaks").
Edit: B1 result on screen @first word, CARD-N or POP 1–3 words, CUT-M · B2 SCREEN or CUTAWAY of hands, STEP "1/3", J into each step, MUS-LOW; re-hook PUNCH @key word · B3 same frame as B1 (WIPE if a before exists), CARD-N, L hold 0.3 s, SFX @card.
Pairs: hooks result-first, proof-first, visual-prop, number; one snag in step 2 (fail-fail-win logic); endings payoff-stop, loop (last frame = B1 frame), send-line. Clashes: mystery-box (result kills the box); curiosity-gap hook that hides the output (contradicts B1).
Fails when: the result is not visible or impressive at phone size in 2 s; more than 3 steps; a flat click list with no snag or re-hook after the hook.
Evidence: MANY [5] demo craft (product-demo playbook, suspense-over-surprise, practitioner reports) + VENDOR view correlations. Not tested on Reels.

### hook-payoff-loop · Payoff first, then loop
Aliases: hook-context-payoff-loop
Does: states the claim or result in 1–2 s, gives one supporting beat, and the last line runs back into the first.
Use when: a fast tip, superlative, cost or constraint idea that fits in 15–35 s. It is result-first-demo plus the loop-back wrapper at short runtime.
Lines: `[claim] [one proof: shown] [...and that's why <claim>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "This $4 pan beats my $200 one." / "...and that's why this $4 pan beats my $200 one."
Beats: B1 CLAIM 15% (0–4.5 | n/a) hook · B2 PROOF 60% (4.5–22.5 | n/a) one context line max, then the proof shown · B3 LOOP LINE 25% (22.5–30 | n/a) completes the first line. Runtime 15–35 s. Words ~36–84.
Edit: B1 POP @claim word, first image is the object · B2 CUTAWAY or SCREEN of the proof @named thing, CUT-M · B3 MATCH to B1 frame, no DROP, music continuous through the join, no breath or sign-off.
Pairs: hooks bold-claim, number, visual-prop; endings loop only. Clashes: tease-next (they open the end the loop closes).
Fails when: the loop does not read as one sentence (feels like a glitch); the proof needs more than one beat; runtime pushes past ~35 s.
Evidence: VENDOR [2] (loop for replays); Instagram names watch-through, not replays. Flag: weak

### before-after · Before / after (bridge)
Aliases: bab, before-after-bridge
Does: states the after as the hook, shows the vivid before, then the one to three changes that bridged them, then the after with a number and a first step for the viewer.
Use when: a personal workflow change, a rebuilt process, a renovated thing. For a feeling-led version that opens on the before, use the before-after arc (`arcs-setback.md`).
Lines: `[after, shown] [<n> weeks ago it looked like this] [change 1, change 2] [after + number; start with <one step>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "Three weeks ago my sourdough looked like this." / "One change: I stopped adding flour when it felt sticky."
Beats: B1 AFTER 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 BEFORE 20% (3–9 | 6–18) · B3 BRIDGE 50% (9–24 | 18–48) 1–3 changes, causal · B4 AFTER + FIRST STEP 20% (24–30 | 48–60) payoff. Words ~72 | ~144. Re-hook: between bridge items.
Edit: B1 after frame @first word, CARD-N if measurable · B2 SMASH to the before, same angle, MUS-LOW · B3 CUT-M, STEP per change, CUTAWAY @named change · B4 WIPE same frame before→after, hold ≥1.5 s, CARD-N, SFX @wipe.
Pairs: hooks result-first, visual-prop, number, proof-first; endings send-line, payoff-stop, loop (end on after frame, hook on before frame). Clashes: mystery-box; a bridge of "I worked harder" (no mechanism).
Fails when: the after has no visible or measurable detail; the bridge becomes a tutorial (one change, not ten); the after needs context to read.
Evidence: MANY/VENDOR [4] transformation craft. Same-frame proof mechanic is INFERENCE.

### i-tested · "I tested X"
Aliases: experiment shape (twin arc: experiment-test-result)
Does: teases the result, states the setup and constraint, shows what happened including the surprise, then the result with a number and the takeaway.
Use when: a test, a cost or time comparison, a challenge with a measurable end.
Lines: `[result teaser] [I tried <x> for <time/count>, rule: <constraint>] [what happened; surprise] [result: <number>] [so <takeaway>, with <caveat>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "I meal-prepped for 14 days. Day 9 broke the plan." / "Result: 31 dollars a week saved, but only if the freezer is empty first."
Beats: B1 TEASER 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 SETUP 15% (3–7.5 | 6–15) · B3 WHAT HAPPENED 40% (7.5–19.5 | 15–39) surprise early in the beat · B4 RESULT 20% (19.5–25.5 | 39–51) payoff · B5 TAKEAWAY 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). @30: B2 folds into B1 or B3; B5 into B4. Re-hook: the surprise line.
Edit: B1 CARD-N teaser or POP · B2 STEP "Day 1" style label · B3 CUT-M, SCREEN or CUTAWAY per event, INT @surprise · B4 CARD-N hold ≥1.5 s, SFX @card · B5 face, CUT-S, CARD-L caveat.
Pairs: hooks number, question, proof-first; endings payoff-stop, send-line. Clashes: mystery-box (the teaser is the result).
Fails when: n = 1 shown as proof; inconclusive result not said; the constraint is never shown.
Evidence: VENDOR + INFERENCE [3]. Flag: weak

## Teaching-order family

### walkthrough · Three-step walkthrough
Aliases: 3-step-walkthrough, tutorial
Does: same timing as result-first-demo, but the hook is the problem or promise, not the result.
Use when: tip or demo where the result is not visual, or the steps are the value.
Lines: `[promise or problem] [step 1, step 2, step 3, one action each] [one-line recap]`
  e.g. "Hang a picture level without a level." / "Step one: tape across the back, mark both hooks on the tape."
Beats: B1 PROMISE 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 STEPS 70% (3–24 | 6–48) 3 steps ≈ 7 s | 14 s · B3 RECAP 20% (24–30 | 48–60) payoff = the last step's visible result. Words ~72 | ~144. Re-hook: before the hardest step.
Edit: B1 POP @promise word, first image the object · B2 STEP "1/3", CUT-M, CUTAWAY @each named tool, SFX @step label · B3 CARD-L recap, L hold.
Pairs: hooks callout, question, number; endings payoff-stop, send-line. Clashes: a hook that promises a result the steps never show.
Fails when: more than 3 steps; a step needs a previous video; no visible end state.
Evidence: VENDOR [3]. Flag: weak

### list-of-3 · List of 3 or fewer
Aliases: numbered-list, mistakes-list (variant)
Does: a number and a promise, then up to 3 parallel items with one example each, then a close.
Use when: list, tip; several parallel, independent tips or rules. Variant mistakes: each item = mistake → why → fix.
Lines: `[<3> <things> + promise] [one: <item>, e.g. <example>] [two…] [three…] [callback or button]`
  e.g. "3 knives you don't need. Number one is in every starter set." / "Number three is the one I used wrong for a year."
Beats: B1 HOOK 10% (0–3 | 0–6) number + promise · B2 ITEMS 75% (3–25.5 | 6–51) 3 items ≈ 7.5 s at 30; 3–4 items ≈ 11–15 s at 60 · B3 CLOSE 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60) callback or button. Words ~72 | ~144 (items ~18 | ~30). Strongest item first (hook) or last (payoff), never middle. Re-hook: the item number said aloud with a teaser.
Edit: B1 CARD-N "3" or POP · B2 STEP "1/3" persistent, new look per item, CUT-M, PUNCH @each item's key word (≤3 per 30 s) · B3 CUT-S, CARD-L.
Pairs: hooks number, warning, callout, secret; endings send-line, button, payoff-stop. Clashes: a slow countdown to an untold #1 (use countdown-to-reveal, `arcs-ladder.md`, with #1 teased in the hook); items that depend on each other (that is a walkthrough).
Fails when: items are not parallel; a ranking is implied but not justified; more than 3 items under 45 s (each beat drops under 5 s).
Evidence: VENDOR [4] for the format; RESEARCH (other medium) [1] for the cap (3 counter-arguments beat 12, overkill effect). List effect itself unproven.

### myth-bust · Myth-bust, fact first
Aliases: myth-bust-fact-first (twin arc: myth-vs-truth)
Does: states the true fact first, names the myth once and small after a warning, says why it feels true, gives the replacement with one concrete example, ends on the true statement.
Use when: a widely held wrong belief; tip, opinion, news.
Lines: `[truth] [a lot of people think <myth>] [it feels true because <reason>] [what actually happens: <example>] [so <do this>]`
  e.g. "Searing doesn't seal in juices. Weigh it." / "It feels true because the crust looks like a seal."
Beats: B1 FACT 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 MYTH ONCE 10% (3–6 | 6–12) · B3 WHY IT FEELS TRUE 30% (6–15 | 12–30) re-hook · B4 REPLACEMENT + EXAMPLE 35% (15–25.5 | 30–51) payoff · B5 TAKEAWAY 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). @30: B1+B2 one beat (0–6); B5 inside B4 if short. Words ~72 | ~144.
Edit: B1 POP the fact's key word, first image the evidence object · B2 small WARN "MYTH" card, never the biggest text on screen · B3 CUT-M, CUTAWAY @source of the belief · B4 SCREEN or CUTAWAY proof, CARD-N, INT @proof · B5 CARD-L with the true statement (the true thing is the last card, never the myth).
Claim-clip variant (from the older Short shape): opening on a real clip or comment of the myth is allowed if it is ≤ 2 s, small, and the fact is on screen text by ~3 s.
Pairs: hooks warning, contrarian, secret, number; endings send-line, payoff-stop, button. Clashes: curiosity-gap that withholds the truth; more than one myth per Reel.
Fails when: the myth is repeated until it sticks; nobody held the myth; no replacement explanation (leaves a gap); "it depends" as the truth.
Evidence: RESEARCH (misinformation correction, text, other medium) [1] + VENDOR [3]. Up to 3 counter-points only.

### pas · Problem, agitate, solve
Aliases: hook-problem-solution (agitate shrinks to one line)
Does: names a pain in the viewer's words, shows a consequence they had not considered, gives one fix, then proof.
Use when: tip, demo; a pain the viewer already feels with one clear fix. For a solution-aware audience, drop agitate to one line.
Lines: `[<pain> in their words] [and it costs you <hidden consequence>] [here's the fix: <one move>] [proof: <shown>]`
  e.g. "Your shower grout goes black every month." / "Here's the fix, and it's not a stronger cleaner."
Beats: B1 PROBLEM 20% (0–6 | 0–12) hook in the first 3 s · B2 AGITATE 35% (6–16.5 | 12–33) · B3 SOLVE 30% (16.5–25.5 | 33–51) payoff · B4 PROOF 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). Words ~72 | ~144. Re-hook: the pivot sentence into the solve.
Edit: B1 first image is the problem, POP @pain word · B2 PUSH, WARN on the consequence, MUS-LOW · B3 INT @pivot, CUT-M, CUTAWAY or SCREEN of the fix, STEP · B4 CARD-N or same-frame proof, SFX @card.
Pairs: hooks callout, warning, stakes; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: a proof beat with nothing to show; agitation longer than the solve.
Fails when: the audience already knows the fix (agitation reads as padding); agitation exaggerates (manipulative); the fix is several moves.
Evidence: VENDOR/craft [3]; no outcome research. Flag: weak

### answer-first · Answer first (inverted pyramid)
Aliases: inverted-pyramid, what-sowhat-nowwhat
Does: gives the answer or what changed in the first sentence, then why it matters, one how-to detail, a caveat, a close.
Use when: news about a tool, product or rule; a reply to a viewer question.
Lines: `[<what changed / the answer>] [it matters because <stakes>] [here's how: <detail>] [one catch: <limit>]`
  e.g. "The airline just cut its free bag size by 5 cm." / "One catch: it only applies to basic fares."
Beats: B1 ANSWER 12% (0–3.5 | 0–7) hook · B2 WHY IT MATTERS 20% (3.5–9.5 | 7–19) · B3 HOW 45% (9.5–23 | 19–46) payoff · B4 CAVEAT + CLOSE 23% (23–30 | 46–60) re-hook = the caveat. Words ~72 | ~144.
Edit: B1 CARD-L with the change, or comment sticker for replies · B2 POP @stakes word · B3 SCREEN or CUTAWAY @named thing, CUT-M, STEP · B4 WARN on the caveat, CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks number, callout, secret; endings payoff-stop, button. Clashes: a story forced into answer-first (loses its payoff).
Fails when: the answer has no stakes (no reason to stay past sentence 1); the caveat is missing on a rule that has one.
Evidence: RESEARCH mixed [3] (inverted pyramid lost to narrative in two of three text studies) + craft. Use for news, not stories.

### objection-answer · Objection, then answer
Does: raises the viewer's own doubt in their words, concedes what is true in it, then settles it with proof.
Use when: tip, opinion, comparison; a known reason people don't try something.
Lines: `["<objection>" in their words] [fair: <true part>] [but <answer>, here's proof: <shown>] [button]`
  e.g. "'Cast iron is too much work.'" / "Fair, if you scrub it. You don't: wipe, heat, one drop of oil."
Beats: B1 OBJECTION 15% (0–4.5 | 0–9) hook · B2 CONCEDE 20% (4.5–10.5 | 9–21) · B3 ANSWER + PROOF 50% (10.5–25.5 | 21–51) payoff · B4 BUTTON 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). @30: B2 folds into B1 if under 5 s. Words ~72 | ~144.
Edit: B1 CARD-L quote card of the objection · B2 face, CUT-S · B3 CUTAWAY or SCREEN proof, CUT-M, INT @but · B4 POP button word.
Pairs: hooks callout, contrarian, question; endings payoff-stop, send-line. Clashes: more than 3 counter-points (overkill).
Fails when: the objection is a strawman nobody holds; no concession (reads as dismissive); proof is a claim.
Evidence: INFERENCE; ≤3 counter-points from RESEARCH (other medium) [1]. Flag: weak

## Story and opinion family

### abt-story · And, but, therefore micro-story
Aliases: abt micro-story (line-rule twin: abt in `arcs-operators.md`)
Does: a hook teasing the turn or outcome, the ordinary situation, the complication, what was done and what happened, the lesson in one sentence.
Use when: story, opinion; "I got X wrong, here's what changed". Decision: personal story → story then proof.
Lines: `[hook: the but or outcome teaser] [AND <normal state>] [BUT <complication>] [THEREFORE <what I did, what happened>] [lesson in one sentence]`
  e.g. "I baked bread every week, and it was fine, but every loaf came out dense." / "So I weighed the water instead of measuring it, and it rose."
Beats: B1 HOOK 10% (0–3 | 0–6) · B2 AND 20% (3–9 | 6–18) · B3 BUT 25% (9–16.5 | 18–33) re-hook · B4 THEREFORE 30% (16.5–25.5 | 33–51) payoff · B5 LESSON 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). @30: B1+B2 one beat. Words ~72 | ~144.
Edit: B1 POP the turn word · B2 CUT-M, MUS-LOW, scene words on screen · B3 visible change @but (cut, PUNCH or DROP), INT · B4 CUTAWAY of the action @therefore · B5 face, CARD-L.
Pairs: hooks confession, mid-action, callout; endings button, callback, send-line. Clashes: result-first hook that also gives the lesson.
Fails when: the story runs past ~60% of runtime and the lesson is vague; the but has no real stakes; the lesson contradicts the story.
Evidence: MANY craft [3] (ABT, Story Spine, Pixar rules) + RESEARCH (narrative aids comprehension, other medium) [2].

### seven-part-story · Story with proof after
Aliases: story-then-proof
Does: introduction, struggle, rise, proof, the mechanism that did it, what to expect, close. Proof lands at ~40% of runtime, not in the hook.
Use when: story; 60 s only. Decision: personal story → story then proof; hybrid → the intro promises the result, proof is held.
Lines: `[intro with tension or result teaser] [struggle] [rise] [proof] [the one mechanism] [what to expect] [close]`
  e.g. (made-up) "Two years ago my shop sold nothing online." / "The thing that changed it wasn't ads: it was photographing every product on the same counter."
Beats: B1 INTRO 8% (n/a | 0–5) hook · B2 STRUGGLE 15% (n/a | 5–14) · B3 RISE 15% (n/a | 14–23) re-hook · B4 PROOF 15% (n/a | 23–32) · B5 MECHANISM 22% (n/a | 32–45) payoff, re-hook going in · B6 EXPECTATIONS 10% (n/a | 45–51) · B7 CLOSE 15% (n/a | 51–60). At 30 s collapse to abt-story. Words ~144.
Edit: B1 POP the tension word · B2 MUS-LOW, CUTAWAY of real old material · B3 CUT-M, RISE · B4 CARD-N hold ≥1.5 s, SCREEN of real receipts · B5 PUSH, INT @mechanism, STEP · B6 CARD-L · B7 CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks stakes, result-first teaser (hybrid), callout; endings button, send-line; lead only if stage allows. Clashes: a plain introduction as hook (fails the first 3 s).
Fails when: over 60 s; parts under 5 s each; proof is generic ("it worked").
Evidence: VENDOR + single-creator order [2]; StoryBrand and Story Spine orderings corroborate story-before-proof (craft). Flag: weak

### hot-take · Hot take with a concession
Aliases: contrarian-stack, claim-evidence
Does: a claim, who it affects, one reason with an example, the one case where the claim does not hold, then the claim restated as a usable rule.
Use when: opinion. claim-evidence variant: claim → evidence → so what (drop the concession).
Lines: `[claim] [if you <who>, this matters] [because <reason>, e.g. <example>] [the one case I'm wrong: <case>] [so: <new rule>]`
  e.g. "Stop buying knife sets." / "The one case I'm wrong: if you'd never buy a second knife anyway."
Beats: B1 CLAIM 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 STAKES 10% (3–6 | 6–12) · B3 REASON + EXAMPLE 40% (6–18 | 12–36) · B4 CONCESSION 15% (18–22.5 | 36–45) re-hook · B5 NEW RULE 25% (22.5–30 | 45–60) payoff. @30: B1+B2 one beat; B4 one sentence inside B5 if needed. Words ~72 | ~144.
Edit: B1 face, POP @claim word · B3 CUTAWAY @example, CUT-M · B4 INT (tone drop), PUSH · B5 CARD-L rule, CUT-S.
Pairs: hooks contrarian, bold-claim, warning; endings button, send-line; comment if stage allows. Clashes: stacking several hot takes in one Reel.
Fails when: the claim is a strawman; attitude with no reason; ranting to the already-convinced (shares, not persuasion).
Evidence: VENDOR [3] for format; concession beat is INFERENCE; worldview-backfire caution from RESEARCH (other medium). Flag: weak

### comparison · A vs B
Aliases: a-vs-b (twin arcs: debate-verdict, experiment-test-result)
Does: teases the verdict, runs up to three rounds with one criterion each (both subjects per round), then the verdict and when to pick the other.
Use when: comparison; two tools, methods or options.
Lines: `[<A> or <B>: one wins for <most people>] [round 1: <criterion>: A <x>, B <y>] [round 2…] [verdict: pick <A> if <c>, <B> if <d>]`
  e.g. (made-up) "Gas or induction: one wins, and it's not the one chefs use." / "Round two, cleanup: induction, 30 seconds."
Beats: B1 TEASER 10% (0–3 | 0–6) hook · B2 ROUNDS 60% (3–21 | 6–42) 2–3 rounds ≈ 6 s | 12 s · B3 VERDICT 30% (21–30 | 42–60) payoff + when to pick the other. Re-hook: before the last round. Words ~72 | ~144.
Edit: B1 SPLIT of both · B2 SPLIT per round, STEP "round 1/3", CARD-N score per round · B3 verdict in a different framing (centered, CUT-S), CARD-L condition.
Pairs: hooks question, number, contrarian; endings send-line, payoff-stop. Clashes: no verdict ("it depends" without conditions).
Fails when: criteria differ between sides; more than 2 subjects in 30 s; a strawman side.
Evidence: INFERENCE [3]; sources name the format only. Flag: weak

### joke-compressed · Setup, punch, tag, callback
Does: minimal setup, the punch word at the end of a sentence, a tag that adds a second laugh, a callback to close.
Use when: opinion or story with humor; any shape's closer.
Lines: `[setup: only what the joke needs] [punch: last word lands it] [tag: second turn] [callback to the opening]`
  e.g. "Every recipe blog starts with the author's childhood." / "I scrolled past three summers in Tuscany to find out it needs one egg."
Beats: B1 SETUP 40% (0–12 | 0–24) hook = the premise in the first line · B2 PUNCH 15% (12–16.5 | 24–33) · B3 TAG 25% (16.5–24 | 33–48) · B4 CALLBACK 20% (24–30 | 48–60) payoff. 60 s: two setup/punch pairs, then callback.
Edit: B1 CUT-M, deadpan framing · B2 SIL ≤0.5 s before the punch word, PUNCH @punch word · B3 CUT-S, POP · B4 MATCH to the opening frame.
Pairs: hooks pov-setup, interrupt, callout; endings callback, loop, button. Clashes: humor on the claim itself in a teaching Reel (keep jokes off the fact).
Fails when: setup over-explains; the punch is buried mid-sentence; the callback was never planted.
Evidence: MANY [3] comedy craft. No video data.

### skit-pov · Skit / POV
Does: the creator plays both sides (or the viewer's POV), deadpan; up to 4 exchanges, each escalates; stops at the biggest.
Use when: opinion, relatable pain; familiar-twist, insider, reverse angles.
Lines: `[POV: <setup shown, not captioned>] [exchange 1] [exchange 2, bigger] [exchange 3, biggest] [button]`
  e.g. "POV: the recipe blog's life story vs you looking for the oven temperature."
Beats: B1 SETUP 15% (0–4.5 | 0–9) hook: frame 1 shows the situation · B2 EXCHANGES 70% (4.5–25.5 | 9–51) ≤4, each bigger · B3 BUTTON 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). Runtime 15–45 s best.
Edit: B1 two-role framing (wardrobe or angle swap) · B2 SMASH on each role switch, CUT-M rhythm identical per exchange, INT on the biggest exchange · B3 hold ≤1 s deadpan, CUT.
Pairs: hooks pov-setup, interrupt; twin arc escalation-to-absurdity; endings button, loop. Clashes: a caption that explains the setup; teaching beats inside the skit.
Fails when: frame 1 needs a caption to make sense; exchanges don't escalate; runs past the biggest exchange.
Evidence: INFERENCE; no sourced skeleton. Flag: weak

### reaction-reply · Reaction / reply
Does: shows the clip, comment or post being answered, then the creator's take, then a button.
Use when: a viewer question or comment, a claim in another clip; myth-test, matchup, insider angles.
Lines: `[<comment or clip> on screen + face] [my take: <answer with proof>] [button]`
  e.g. "'You can't cook rice in a kettle.'" / "Let's find out."
Beats: B1 SOURCE 20% (0–6 | 0–12) hook; creator's face on screen in frame 1 · B2 TAKE 65% (6–25.5 | 12–51) payoff · B3 BUTTON 15% (25.5–30 | 51–60). Source footage ≤ 1/3 of runtime (moving picture of the source at any size counts; a still counts as a graphic).
Edit: B1 comment sticker or PIP clip with face, POP @key word of the comment · B2 CUT-M, CUTAWAY or SCREEN proof · B3 CUT-S, POP.
Pairs: shapes answer-first, myth-bust inside B2; hooks question, callout; endings payoff-stop, button. Clashes: the clip carrying the content (the take is the content).
Fails when: the source runs over a third; rights to the clip unclear; the take is a reaction face with no point.
Evidence: OFFICIAL [1] (reply-with-video feature exists); no script shape sourced. Flag: weak

## Wrapper

### loop-back · Loop-back closer
Does: the last line completes or echoes the first, so the end runs into the start on replay.
Use when: any shape under ~20–35 s; untested longer. Wraps hook-payoff-loop, list-of-3, myth-bust.
Lines: split sentence (`[first half] … [second half that needs the first]`), echo (same words, new meaning), Q/A (last line asks what the first answers), visual match, sound match.
  e.g. "…and that's why you never rinse raw chicken." → loops into "Washing raw chicken spreads it."
Beats: wraps the last beat only: last 1–2 s must be the join. No breath, sign-off or end card at the join.
Edit: MATCH last frame to first; music continuous across the join (no DROP); caption chunk splits across the join cleanly.
Pairs: endings loop; operator full-circle (`arcs-operators.md`). Clashes: tease-next, comment asks at the end.
Fails when: the join is felt as a glitch; the last line makes no sense without the first; a sign-off sits at the join.
Evidence: VENDOR [2]. Replays as a ranking factor is a vendor claim; Instagram officially names watch-through. Flag: weak
