# Spoken copy: rules and notation

## Speech is not writing
Speech chains ideas with and / but / so / because instead of nesting clauses; uses more pronouns (I, you, it); fewer adjectives and noun-stacks; repeats key words. The listener can't re-read, so no sentence may make them hold two open items.

| Rule | Check | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Most sentences 6–14 words; flag >20; hard flag >25 | count | craft (web-writing and broadcast conventions) |
| Vary length: short claim (≤6) then a longer explanation (10–16); flag 5+ sentences of near-equal length in a row | count | weak (detector-vendor tell) |
| Contractions where the creator would say them | count | craft; keep emphasis ("do NOT paste your password") |
| Fragments and And/But openers allowed | — | speech norm |
| Subject–verb–object, active verbs | read | craft |
| One new idea per sentence | read | craft |
| Numbers: say them simply AND put them on screen; write them as said ("twenty-two days") | read | broadcast convention + Mayer (screen carries the object) |
| Tool or product names with a pronunciation hint when unclear | read | craft |
| "You" for benefits and steps; "I" for evidence ("I tested", "I lost an hour"); avoid "guys"/"we" unless that's the creator's voice | read | weak (comment-sentiment study: first-person, plain voice reads warmer) |
| Hedges: cut stacked weak ones; keep one calibrated first-person qualifier when uncertain | read | lab studies on hedges; persuasion study on confident qualifiers (secondary) |
| No readability formulas on speech (Flesch was built for school texts) | — | — |

## Humor
Optional. One line, observational or self-deprecating about the process; where attention would sag (after the proof) or as the closing button. Never on the claim itself. Never at the viewer's expense.

## Accents and non-native phrasing
Keep the creator's idiom when it's clear; only fix grammar that makes meaning unclear. Avoid words they stumble on. Captions match what they actually say, not the polished script. Simple wording is never treated as an "AI tell".

## Script notation (one breath group per row)
| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `WORD` in caps | the one stressed word in the line (max one) |
| `/` | micro-beat ~0.3 s |
| `//` | full pause ~0.6–1 s, usually before the payoff word |
| `(screen: "text")` | on-screen text (POP) |
| `(show: …)` | proof / visual moment |
| `[cut]` | hard cut |
| `[beat 1.5s]` | silent visual beat with its length |
| `{fast}` `{slow}` | pace change, max 2 per script |
Timestamps: running estimate per row at the creator's pace.

## Pauses
No controlled evidence on pause length in short narration. Conventions: a pause before the payoff word, after a surprising claim, between demo steps; never mid-clause. Cut pauses that exist only because the speaker needed time; keep the beat after a claim (0.3–0.5 s).
