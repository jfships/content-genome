# Stage 1: Who they are, what it feels like, what it's called

**Short on purpose.** This stage asks only what every later stage needs: who they are, why the content exists, who it's for, what watching it should feel like, and what to call it. Taste in looks is asked at the start of Stage 4; the writing voice is asked here, in 1d, so each answer lands next to the choice it shapes.

Every question follows the voice in `../voting-pages.md`: say what the answer changes, then ask.

## 1a. Core interview (2 rounds)

**Demo mode: the interview builds the persona.** Run the same questions a real creator gets, as AskUserQuestion cards. On each card the first option is the persona's answer, invented in character on the spot and labeled "(invented in character)". The operator confirms with one click, or picks or types another answer (a veto). In fast-forward, show the questions and the persona's answers as a list instead of cards. Never fill the doc silently. Viewers should see that a real creator gets interviewed and that the seed was only a seed. Stage 1 is the one place the operator clicks; from Stage 2 on the run moves by itself.

**Round 1: the basics (4–5 questions).**
- What they do, in their words, and roughly where they are in it (starting / years in / known for it locally).
- **Who's on screen.** Say what it changes: "This decides the whole route: which looks, styles and rules the rest of this builds." Multi-select: *Me on camera* · *My hands or my work, no face* · *Screen recordings* · *An AI character or avatar of me* · *A made-up AI character* · *Fully generated visuals, no person*. Then "Which is the main one?" Store main + secondary routes as "Who's on screen" in `Who I Am.md`; Stages 3–10 read them (footage looks only if something is filmed; a whole-channel art style only if generated is main; likeness and avatar rules only if an AI character is used).
- **Why the content exists.** Ask it plainly: "What's the content for?" fame · customers for a service · selling a product · a hobby that should pay for itself · something else. It's often lead generation, not fame. This is the top line of the doc.
- **Who they picture watching.** 3–4 plausible audiences as options, plus Other.
- **Proof:** 3–5 real, checkable things they've done that a script may claim. Keep their qualifiers ("almost", "about"). In demo: fictional, marked so.
- **Truth rules:** "Anything that must never be said or shown?" real numbers · family · health · named clients · labels they reject · Other.

**Round 2: the person (4–5 questions).**
- A moment they'd tell on camera, and what it proves (story beat).
- A strong opinion in their field they'd argue for. Follow up: why, or an example.
- A pet peeve about how others in their field show up online.
- What makes them different from the ten other channels on the same topic.
- **How far they'll go.** Say what it changes: "This sets how bold the videos are allowed to get: the effects, the jokes, the moments that step outside reality." Options: *Keep it clean and simple* · *A little playful* · *Go for it, I like weird* · *As far as it takes to be remembered*. One pick.
- One open question: a rant, a weird fact, a thing nobody asks them. "Tell you in Other" / "Skip".

**How to interview.**
- Gap questions have 3–4 plausible, distinct guesses as options. If they pick one and write the real answer in Other, the text is the answer.
- Ask about specific moments and real things, not abstractions.
- After each round, follow up on the 2–3 most interesting answers (a strong opinion → an example; a tension → where their own work fits; a vague answer → one concrete example).
- Ask which new takes are OK on camera. Anything left unpicked goes to Background only.
- **Your reads go to Unconfirmed**, even if they nodded along.
- **Contradictions:** one question per conflict, quoting both versions. Never keep both silently.
- **Sensitive things** (family, health, money, named people or places) default to Background only unless they say otherwise.

**The doc.** `Who I Am.md` on the creator's computer is the master, with a pointer copy in the project. It outranks every other doc for scripts, hooks and titles. Written for agents: one idea per bullet, distilled, not a transcript. ~60 lines now; Stages 4 and 9 add to it.

**Sections, in order:** Identity (what's OK to say on camera) · Who's on screen (main route + secondary) · What the channel is about (one line, written from the interview; a direction, never a topic list) · Proof · Why the content exists · Audience · How far they'll go (the pick, plus one line on what it means in practice) · How they think · Takes they hold · Story beats (one line each + what it proves) · Risks (how far they'll go on camera) · Background only · Unconfirmed · Changes (up to 5 dated lines). Stage 4 adds "Taste" under Takes; 1d adds "Voice and delivery".

**Demo: the persona card.** At the end of 1a, write `00 Decisions/persona-card.md` from the interview: name, basics, why the content exists, audience, proof (fictional), story beats, opinions, truth rules, filming setup (the place and gear their life would have), publishing rhythm their life allows, and a one-line note on how they'd be drawn (a drawn avatar or silhouette; no generated face unless the operator asks at the image question in Stage 4). Show it for approval **once, at the end of 1c**, after the name is settled (1b and 1c add to the card before it is shown). From here on, later stages answer from the card and add to it as new answers get invented.

## 1b. Content personality (what watching it should feel like)

Ask after the interview, before anything visual. Say what it changes: "This is the yardstick every video gets judged by. If two choices pull apart later, the one you put first wins."
- **Ask with AskUserQuestion, multi-select, with Other.** Options, each with a one-line "feels like…": entertaining · informational / useful · unique / unlike anything else · broad appeal · quirky / absurd · calm / thoughtful · serious / authoritative · high-energy / hype · provocative / contrarian · inspirational. Recommend 3–4 that fit the interview; don't let them pick everything.
- **Rank them.** "Which one leads?" One leads as the tiebreaker; the rest support. Write one line per pick on what it means in practice ("entertaining = every scene has a reason to keep watching: a joke, a reveal, a payoff").
- **The fallback, when the lead is "entertaining" (or any feel that's hard to force).** Ask one more question: "Some topics are hard to make entertaining without forcing a joke. When that happens, what carries the video?" AskUserQuestion, with Other: **Motion graphics** (screens and ideas moving in 3D space, top-agency level; Recommended for anyone who explains, teaches or demos) · **Visual gags and cutaways** · **Pace** (faster cuts, tighter talk) · **Story** (stakes, a person, a before and after). Write it as one line under the ranking: "Fallback: when a topic is hard to make entertaining without forcing it, <answer> carries it." Video skills use it in their personality check instead of forcing a joke. If they pick motion graphics, Stage 5 builds their spatial library.
- **Write it down** as "Content personality" in `Who I Am.md` and in the Brand Genome. It feeds formats, motion, sound, voice and every self-review score.
- **Re-check after the first draft video.** First drafts tend to come out informative and flat.

## 1c. Name and handle

- **Accounts they're keeping (Stage 0):** the name is settled; skip the search for that platform. Only check that the same handle, or a close variant, is free on the platforms they're adding.
- **New accounts:** recommend the real name on screen for trust and portability; the handle can be a short variant. In demo, the persona's invented name.
- **Check availability on every target platform** before they fall for a handle. Fetching `youtube.com/@handle` returns "not found" when it's free; Instagram and TikTok usually have to be checked by hand at signup.
- **Check for clashes:** search the name for existing channels and brands in any niche. In demo, drop the name if it matches a real creator or public figure.
- **Platform rules** (check live): dots are allowed on YouTube and Instagram but not on X, so an underscore variant usually works everywhere. The display name everywhere is the on-screen name.
- **Keep company names separate** from a personal channel. The channel is the person; the company is what they sell.

## 1d. Writing voice (chat questions, no page)

Words alone are enough to judge a writing style, so this is asked in chat. Say what it changes: "Scripts, titles and captions get drafted for you to record or post. These answers keep them sounding like you."
1. **Writing styles** (AskUserQuestion, multi-select, Other): 8–12 styles from `../catalog/vertical-voice-formats.md` ("Voice styles"), each shown as its name plus 1–2 plain lines on how it sounds (e.g. **Straight talker**: short sentences, no fluff · **Storyteller**: sets up a moment, pays it off · **Hype**: fast, high energy, exclamations). No sample scripts.
2. **"Which one is your default?"** among the ones picked.
3. **"Say the first thing you'd tell a new viewer, the way you'd actually say it."** Free text (Other), with 3 plausible starts drawn from the interview.
4. **"Which of these would you never say?"** 4–5 phrases people in their field overuse, plus Other.

Write up to 5 short verbatim lines as **voice anchors** under "Voice and delivery" in `Who I Am.md`. In demo, answer in character and add them to the persona card.

**The voice kit is written automatically** right after (no page), from these answers, the interview and the content personality, into `00 Decisions/voice-kit.md`:
- **Spoken:** pace, energy, how to open and close, how to show enthusiasm on camera, what to do with hands and eyes.
- **Written:**
  - sentence length
  - words used and avoided
  - humor level and type
  - signature phrases (only ones they'd actually say)
  - honesty rules: every claim is countable or checkable; keep their qualifiers
  - proof before admission
- **Doesn't sound like AI.** These are on by default; the creator can switch any one off:
  - no em dashes
  - no hype words (unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, supercharge, delve)
  - no "It's not X, it's Y" constructions
  - no rhetorical-question openers
  - no automatic lists of three
  - contractions as they speak
  - no exclamation marks they wouldn't say out loud
- **Take-review scorecard,** used on every recorded take, scored 1–5: energy, clarity, pace, eye line, did the personality come through, and one line on what to change next take. Most first-time creators start out dry; score it every time.

## Output

`Who I Am.md` with truth rules and why the content exists, the content personality (ranked, one line each), the name, and one handle checked on every target platform. `voice-kit.md` (default style + kept styles, anchors, AI-tell rules, take scorecard). In demo, `persona-card.md`.
