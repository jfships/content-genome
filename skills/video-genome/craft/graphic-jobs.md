# What a graphic is for

Read before planning the look of any beat, long or short, and again before the beat brief (`paths/footage-first.md` B5, `paths/graphics-first.md` A1, `shorts/build.md` "Beat brief").

In an entertaining video a graphic is a second performer, not a caption. It does one of five jobs. A graphic that only draws the spoken word (boxes for "tools", a chart for "prices went up", the word on a block) is cut unless it also does one of these.

## The five jobs

| Job | What it does | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Straight man** | The subject's own material plays it straight while the creator reacts: their ad, their words, their page, their product shot, word for word, unedited. The comedy is the gap between what they say and what the creator says. | Their footage full-bleed with the creator over or under it; their quote big; their product doing the thing. |
| **Unsaid line** | Says the joke or the fact the script had no room for. The viewer reads it while the creator says something else. | A label that contradicts the line; a notification that interrupts; a second caption track; a detail on an object. |
| **Exaggeration** | Takes the line further than words could. | The small thing made enormous; the scene the line implies, built for real (a courtroom, a funeral, a border crossing); a count that keeps climbing. |
| **Proof** | Backs a claim at the moment it is made. Only for lines that are claims. | The source's own page, number or quote, readable for at least 1.1 s, nothing else on screen with it. |
| **Breather** | Nothing. The creator's face and the line carry it. Planned, not leftover. | Full frame, a punch-in, a hold on the reaction. |

A beat can do two jobs (a proof that is also the straight man). It can't do none.

## Choosing, per beat

For every beat that carries a graphic, list the candidates from **every** source before picking:
1. the subject's own material (footage, pages, posts, product shots: `craft/asset-library.md`);
2. the brand's kept devices (its moments, extras, backdrops, layouts, spatial and 3D moves);
3. a new device designed for this line inside the brand's limits (`craft/brand-fit.md`);
4. a generated image or clip (stylized by default, following the brand's image rules and likeness references);
5. plain face (a breather).

Then pick, and write the reason in one line in the beat brief. These are not reasons: "used it last time", "first on the list", "it's in the brand", "it illustrates the line". A reason names the job and why this beats the two alternates.

Never the same device on two consecutive beats unless the repeat is the joke. Never the creator's own face as the graphic (a grid of them, a photo of them) more than once a video, long or short.

## Design, don't pick

The brand's kept devices and `craft/graphics-library.md` are vocabulary, not a menu. They set a floor, never a ceiling. For every beat that carries a graphic, design the graphic for that line: composition, motion, timing, built at the bar of a top agency's launch film, with everything the current model can do. Use a kept device when it is the best design for the line, not because it exists.

Motion can't be judged from a still. Every graphic that moves is proved as a rendered 2–4 s clip of that beat before the full build (stills settle layout; clips settle motion). The creator sees clip cards, never a description.

## Execution bar

A device must look like the real thing. A stamp reads as a rubber stamp, an invoice as an invoice, a text thread as a text thread, a report card as a report card: the texture, the type, the wear, the small details someone would recognize. A thin version of a good device (flat shapes, placeholder type, a box with a label) is a failure of execution, not a reason to drop the device. Fix the execution first; swap the device only when a better job exists for the line.

Numbers and stats never appear as plain corporate bars or slides; they are a real object (a receipt, a scoreboard, a counter) or a parody of one. `craft/motion.md` §9 (anti-slideshow) applies to every graphic.

## Self-check, before any render

- [ ] Every beat names a job; none is "illustrates the line".
- [ ] Every pick names the two alternates it beat and why.
- [ ] No device repeats on consecutive beats; the creator's face is not the graphic.
- [ ] Every moving graphic has a rendered clip.
- [ ] Every proof is readable for 1.1 s alone; nothing else is a proof.
- [ ] Each device passes the execution bar.

## Ledger

The brand's style ledger (`00 Decisions/style-ledger.md`) records what worked and what was killed in the creator's real videos. Read it before the beat brief; update it at session close (`review/session-close.md` 2a). Header when creating one:

```
# <Brand> style ledger
What's working and what was killed in published videos and judged drafts. Observations, promoted to Genome rules after 2–3 videos.
## Working      | Device or layout | Job | Seen in (video, time, line) | Still | Note |
## Promoted     (now a Genome rule; date)
## Dropped      | Device or layout | Seen in | Creator's words | Why it failed (device or execution) |
```
