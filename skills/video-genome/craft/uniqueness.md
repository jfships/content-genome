# Uniqueness: recognizably theirs, never a template

A polished video can still look like everyone else's. These rules keep each video unmistakably the creator's.

## Rules

- **Something signature every video:** at least one of the brand's signature moments, the sting and sound logo (long-form), and the pack's title style. A video with none of these could belong to anyone.
- **At most one wildcard,** on the hook or a turning point. It's allowed to fail; it's not allowed to be the default.
- **No composition twice in a row.** Rotate layouts (centered, thirds, split, full bleed, cutout left/right).
- **No transition twice in a row.**
- **Rotate backgrounds.** Never the same flat background for two scenes in a row.
- **Show their real things.** Their screens, objects, places, notes and pages beat stock stand-ins every time.
- **Their words on screen.** Headlines echo what they actually said, in their voice kit, not generic phrasing.
- **Their quirks.** Every video gives the quirks or running gags from the brand's Creator section at least one moment.

## Tests (run at gate 4)

- **"Whose channel is this?"** Take 5 random stills from different minutes, with no logo or name visible. Would a regular viewer know whose channel it is? If 2 or more could be anyone's, rework those scenes with the pack's title style, texture, palette or a signature move.
- **"Is this a template?"** For each scene, ask whether it could appear unchanged in a stock template pack. If yes, change one thing that only this brand would do.
- **Competitor check.** Compare 3 stills against the benchmark channels listed in the brand's Research section. Too close to one of them (same layout, same title style, same palette) → change it.
- **Generic-AI tells.** No default gradients that aren't in the palette, no sparkle icons, no glass panels over footage, no stock "tech" blue unless it's the brand's, and no filler words on screen (unlock, seamless, elevate).
